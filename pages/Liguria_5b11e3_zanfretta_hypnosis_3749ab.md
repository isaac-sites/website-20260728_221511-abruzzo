## Introduction

Hypnosis did not begin the Zanfretta case, but it transformed it. Before the first session, the core claim was already extraordinary: security guard Pier Fortunato Zanfretta said that he had encountered towering non-human figures near Torriglia, in the Ligurian hills, on the night of 6 December 1978. What hypnosis added was the structure of a full alien-abduction narrative — entry into a spacecraft, physical examination, communication with reptile-like beings and, in later accounts, an expanding history of repeated contact.

That distinction matters. Hypnotic recall is not a recording recovered from storage. Research shows that hypnosis can encourage people to produce while also increasing errors, susceptibility to suggestion and confidence in inaccurate memories. The most dramatic parts of the Zanfretta saga should therefore be treated not as independent evidence about what happened near Torriglia, but as evidence of how an uncertain and frightening experience was elaborated through questioning, expectation and repeated retelling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11832514/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[nih.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pub Med Central (PMC)the role of hypnosis in memory recall and false</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by DG Leo · 2025 · Cited by 10 — In this paper, we review the current literature on the mechanisms behind the creation of false memori...</span></span></span>

## How the later narrative expanded

The story that entered Italian UFO folklore was not delivered as a single, stable account immediately after the incident. Zanfretta reportedly described strange lights, an object and frightening beings before hypnosis, so it would be wrong to claim that the entire encounter was invented during a session. The evidential problem is narrower but crucial: the spacecraft-abduction sequence and many of the saga’s richest details emerged after investigators sought hidden memories.

Journalist Rino Di Stefano, who became the case’s principal chronicler, encouraged Zanfretta to undergo hypnosis. The first session is generally dated to 23 December 1978, little more than two weeks after the initial event, and was conducted in Genoa by psychotherapist and medical hypnotist Mauro Moretti in the presence of journalists and UFO investigators. Under hypnosis, Zanfretta reportedly described being taken into a brightly illuminated craft and subjected to painful procedures. Further sessions and later alleged encounters enlarged the account into a continuing relationship with non-human visitors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanfretta_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span></span></span>

This sequence changes how the evidence should be weighed. A detail independently recorded before investigators introduced an abduction interpretation has more value than one first produced after the witness had been questioned, exposed to press coverage and placed in a setting where everyone expected missing information to emerge. The later account may preserve elements of Zanfretta’s genuine recollection, but the method provides no dependable way to separate those elements from inference, imagery and suggestion.

The setting of the sessions also raises concerns. Later summaries of the controversy state that possible questions and interpretations were discussed while Zanfretta was already hypnotised. Even without deliberately leading him, investigators could communicate assumptions through wording, repetition, tone or the simple expectation that there must be a concealed episode to recover. Italian UFO commentators themselves have not been unanimous in defending the procedure: accounts of the debate report criticism that investigative intrusion had irreversibly contaminated the hypnotic material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanfretta_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span></span></span>

## Suggestion, confidence and false memory

Hypnosis is a genuine psychological procedure with clinical uses, including pain management and focused therapeutic work. The problem arises when it is treated as a truth-finding device. Focused attention, heightened expectation and responsiveness to suggestion can be useful in therapy, yet those same qualities are hazardous when the aim is to reconstruct the factual sequence of a disputed event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/22676-hypnosis" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: my.clevelandclinic.org">[Cleveland Clinic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">my.clevelandclinic.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">It&#x27;s a type of complementary therapy.Read more</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cleveland ClinicHypnosis: What It Is, How It Works, Benefits &amp; Risks28 Jan 2025 — Hypnosis, also called hypnotherapy, is a state of deep...</span></span></span>

Memory is reconstructive. People do not normally retrieve a complete internal film of an experience. They rebuild an account from surviving impressions, later information, expectations and repeated attempts to make sense of gaps. False memories can therefore feel coherent, emotional and autobiographical rather than vague or knowingly fabricated. Researchers have repeatedly demonstrated that misleading information and suggestive procedures can alter reports of events, and that subjective vividness does not reliably distinguish an accurate recollection from an inaccurate one.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3143501/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed Central (PMC)]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nih.gov.</span></span></span>

Hypnosis creates three particular risks for a case such as Zanfretta’s.

**It increases pressure to produce an answer.** A witness has been told, directly or indirectly, that important memories may be hidden. Silence and uncertainty can begin to feel like failures, while images or guesses may be accepted as recovered material.

**It can blur imagination and recollection.** Instructions to return mentally to a scene, observe it from another position or describe what happens next invite imagery. Once generated, that imagery may later be remembered as part of the original experience rather than as something constructed during questioning.

**It can raise confidence without raising accuracy.** Experimental findings indicate that hypnotised participants may become more certain about erroneous recollections even when their overall accuracy does not improve. This “memory hardening” is particularly damaging because later interviews may present a more confident witness whose story now appears stronger, although the confidence was partly produced by the retrieval procedure itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.osu.edu/hypnosis-may-give-false-confidence-in-inaccurate-memories/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.osu.edu">[osu.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.osu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">hypnosis may give false confidence in inaccurate memories</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ohio State NewsHypnosis May Give False Confidence In Inaccurate Memories21 Aug 2001 — A new study suggests that hypnosis doesn&#x27;t help peo...</span></span></span>

That last effect helps explain why hypnosis can be persuasive to audiences. A hesitant witness who later supplies vivid detail may look as though a barrier has been removed. Scientifically, however, the change can mean that ambiguity has been converted into narrative certainty.

## The abduction script was already available

By the late 1970s, the idea that UFO witnesses might suffer missing time and recover memories of spacecraft examinations through hypnosis was already circulating internationally. Psychological analysis of alien-abduction reports has noted a recurring pattern: an initially unclear episode — a light, fear, paralysis, lost time or a disturbing dreamlike experience — becomes a specific abduction account after consultation with a therapist or UFO investigator. Hypnosis is not present in every case, but it has often served as a mechanism for filling narrative gaps.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gwern.net/doc/psychiatry/1996-newman.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gwern.net">[gwern.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gwern.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gwern.net.</span></span></span>

This does not require a witness to copy another account consciously. Cultural material supplies templates: strange beings, medical examination, telepathic speech, missing time and a mission or warning. When a person is invited to explain an anomalous experience, familiar story components can shape what seems plausible and what images arise.

The Zanfretta material also developed within an intense media environment. Journalists became involved quickly, the case attracted national attention and the witness’s account was repeatedly revisited. Repetition can stabilise a story, but stability after repeated interviewing is not the same as independent corroboration. Once details have appeared in hypnosis, press reports or conversations with investigators, later consistency may merely show that the witness has learned and retained the developed version.

Sceptical commentary has also pointed to possible similarities between names or creature imagery in the later story and science-fiction or comic material available in Italy at the time. Such comparisons are suggestive rather than decisive: resemblance cannot prove the source of a memory, and ordinary people absorb cultural imagery without remembering exactly where it came from. The stronger criticism is methodological. Because the sessions occurred after publicity and within an explicitly extraterrestrial frame, normal cultural influence cannot be excluded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanfretta_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span></span></span>

## Why sincerity does not prove accuracy

Zanfretta may have been sincere. Reports of his condition after the original incident describe severe distress, and even some sceptical treatments have distinguished possible good faith from factual reliability. That is an important distinction: rejecting the hypnotically recovered narrative does not require accusing him of deliberate fraud.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanfretta_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span></span></span>

People can honestly report events that did not occur exactly as remembered. Fear, poor visibility, shock, expectation, sleep-related experiences, repeated questioning and later information can all influence recollection without conscious deception. Hypnosis may strengthen the witness’s conviction because the images produced during the session feel immediate and emotionally real.

Nor does a finding that someone shows no obvious psychiatric disorder verify the content of an extraordinary claim. Psychologically healthy people misperceive events and form false memories. A clinical assessment may help rule out certain illnesses, but it cannot establish that a remembered spacecraft or medical examination existed outside the witness’s experience.

Emotional intensity is similarly limited as evidence. Distress can establish that the witness felt threatened, frightened or overwhelmed. It cannot by itself identify what caused that reaction. In the Zanfretta case, the reported fear belongs to the evidential record; the reptilian abductors remain an interpretation whose most developed form arose through a method unable to authenticate it.

## What hypnosis leaves investigators unable to recover

Once suggestive recall has been introduced, it is extremely difficult to reconstruct the untouched original memory. Later investigators cannot simply remove the hypnotic additions, because the witness may no longer know which details came from the first night, which arose during a session and which were reinforced by later discussion.

A more reliable investigation would have preserved an early, detailed statement before publicity; used open, non-leading questions; recorded every interview in full; separated witnesses; and tested each new detail against evidence unknown to the interviewer and witness. A genuinely useful hypnotic statement would still require external verification — for example, a precise fact recovered during the session that could later be independently confirmed and had not been available through ordinary inference or prior discussion.

The Zanfretta abduction narrative does not meet that standard. Its hypnotically produced elements are not supported by confirmed records from inside a craft, verifiable medical findings or information uniquely obtained through the sessions. The procedure generated narrative richness without an equivalent increase in independent evidence.

This does not erase the original Torriglia incident from [Liguria]({{ 'why-liguria-became-italys-ufo-coast/' | relative_url }})’s UFO history. There was a reported encounter, a distressed witness, police interest and a public controversy. It does change the classification of the later saga. The early episode remains disputed and incompletely explained; the hypnotic spacecraft narrative is substantially weaker because the method used to reveal it could also have helped create it.

## The case’s real lesson for Liguria

Within Ligurian UFO history, hypnosis is the mechanism that turned a local close-encounter report into Italy’s most famous extended abduction story. It supplied scenes, beings and explanations that could be dramatised in newspapers, television programmes, books and later retellings. At the same time, it made the central claims harder to evaluate by contaminating the witness’s memory with an investigative process known to increase suggestion and misplaced confidence.

The fairest assessment is therefore neither that hypnosis proved Zanfretta’s account nor that it demonstrated conscious invention. It produced testimony whose sincerity may be genuine but whose factual accuracy cannot be established. The reptilian beings and spacecraft examinations belong to the history of the story’s development, not to the strongest independently supported evidence from the original Ligurian incident.

That difference is the key to reading the Zanfretta saga responsibly. The more elaborate the account became, the more memorable it was — and the less securely it could be separated from the method used to uncover it.
