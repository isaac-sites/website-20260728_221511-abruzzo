## Introduction

[Apulia]({{ 'why-apulias-ufo-record-still-resists/' | relative_url }})’s UFO totals do not match because private investigators and the Italian Air Force are not counting the same thing. A regional archive may preserve almost every reportable trace of an unusual event: newspaper stories, witness letters, photographs, second-hand accounts, retrospective memories and cases later explained as ordinary objects. The Air Force records only the much narrower set of incidents that enter its formal reporting and assessment procedure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/en/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[difesa.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica MilitareOVNI - Aeronautica Militare...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Apulia_98f3e8_private_vs_official_87f263-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Record Systems" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

This difference matters whenever someone claims that Apulia has produced a particular number of UFO sightings. A large private total can show the scale of public reporting and local interest, but it is not a count of unexplained craft. A small official total does not prove that little happened; it may simply mean that few witnesses used the prescribed channel. The apparent disagreement is therefore mainly a question of definitions, access and institutional purpose rather than evidence that one side is necessarily concealing or inventing cases.

## What private archives include

The most substantial regional collection has been assembled through the Italian Centre for UFO Studies, usually known as CISU, and the long-running work of Apulian investigator Arcangelo Cassano. CISU says that its regional catalogue project began in 1981, with local curators continuously gathering cases and adding basic data to a national archive. Crucially, CISU defines its catalogue as the whole body of reports of unusual aerial phenomena, only a small proportion of which remain unidentified after adequate analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/ufo-in-italia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Ufo in Italia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciUfo in Italia - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

That distinction is easy to miss. In ordinary conversation, “1,000 UFO cases” may sound like 1,000 unexplained objects. In an archival catalogue, it can mean nearly 1,000 entries requiring description, source checking and possible identification. CISU’s Apulia map now presents almost 1,000 regional cases gathered over nearly four decades, with dates, locations, documentary sources, possible [explanations]({{ 'explanations-0128e2/' | relative_url }}) and, in some entries, drawings or photographs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/geo-localizzazione-degli-avvistamenti-lesempio-della-puglia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciGeo-localizzazione degli avvistamenti: l&#x27;esempio della Puglia - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

The map’s categories reveal how broad the collection is. It separates night lights, daylight discs, close encounters and reports associated with water, but also marks entries in which there was no actual UFO observation. Users can search by town and read a short account together with the information source. The archive covers material from 1950 onwards and divides the cases by province, period and type.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/ufo-in-puglia-geolocalizzazione-degli-avvistamenti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciUFO in Puglia: geolocalizzazione degli avvistamenti - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

A private regional archive may therefore include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a contemporary newspaper report whose original witness was never interviewed;
* several accounts of the same light from neighbouring towns;
* a photograph submitted years after the event;
* a retrospective childhood memory;
* an incident eventually attributed to a meteor, aircraft, satellite or lantern;
* an enquiry, rumour or alleged trace linked to UFO discussion without a direct sighting.

</div>

Including such material is not necessarily poor practice. For historical research, discarded and explained reports are valuable because they show how sightings spread, how the press framed them and which ordinary phenomena repeatedly generated confusion. The problem arises only when the catalogue total is quoted as though every entry had equal evidential weight.

Private coverage also depends heavily on researcher activity. CISU itself warns that differences between Italian regions partly reflect how active local investigators have been. A region with energetic collectors, good newspaper access and decades of follow-up can appear unusually “active” even when the underlying rate of strange observations is similar to elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/ufo-in-italia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Ufo in Italia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciUfo in Italia - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

Apulia is a strong example of that collection effect. Cassano maintained a regional digital archive from 1999 to 2021, while the present geolocated catalogue represents almost forty years of sustained work. The resulting density is partly a record of sightings and partly a record of successful preservation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/arcangelo-cassano/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arcangelo cassano</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciArcangelo CassanoDal 1999 al 2021 ha realizzato ed aggiornato un archivio digitale della casistica...</span></span></span>

## What the Air Force records

The Italian Air Force has a different mandate. After the national wave of 1978, Prime Minister Giulio Andreotti designated it as the institutional body responsible for collecting, verifying and monitoring UFO reports. The work is now handled by the General Security Department of the Air Force Staff and is explicitly connected to flight safety and national security.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/en/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica MilitareOVNI - Aeronautica Militare...</span></span></span>

A member of the public is directed to complete an official form and deliver it to the nearest Carabinieri station. The Air Force can then compare the report with known human activity and natural phenomena, involving other competent bodies where necessary. Once the investigation is finished, an incident is classified as unidentified only when no technical or natural explanation can be established.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/en/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica MilitareOVNI - Aeronautica Militare...</span></span></span>

That procedure creates a much narrower dataset. A sighting mentioned only on social media, reported to a local newspaper or sent directly to a private investigator does not automatically become an Air Force case. Even a striking event may be absent if the witness did not complete the form, if the report arrived too late, or if the information was too vague to enter the [official process]({{ 'official-process-cb15a5/' | relative_url }}).

The published official archive is also structured around processed incidents rather than regional folklore. Its tables usually record fields such as place, date, time, shape, colour, apparent speed, direction, estimated altitude, weather and the identity or institutional category of the reporter. The assessment then states whether the event could be associated with known flight activity, weather balloons or another recognised cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1991-2000.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

Several Apulian entries show how this differs from a private narrative archive. In September 1994, an oval white light reported over Bari by private citizens was formally catalogued as an unidentified flying object after examination of the archived data. A second Bari report in November 1994 came from Carabinieri and police personnel and was likewise retained in the official record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1991-2000.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

The same official file contains other regional cases, including a 1993 Galatina report made by Air Force personnel and 1994 incidents at Presicce and Oria. Their presence is important because they demonstrate that the state archive does contain Apulian cases reported by both civilians and uniformed witnesses. It does not, however, follow that the archive contains every case known to local researchers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1991-2000.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

Official wording also requires care. A finding that an event could not be matched to known flying activity is not the same as evidence of an extraordinary vehicle. In the June 2009 event seen from Conversano and other parts of southern Italy, the Air Force found no association with known flight or radiosonde activity, yet its published note also raised a possible meteor explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/OVNI-2010.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

This is a useful illustration of the difference between **unidentified within a specific check** and **unexplained under every plausible hypothesis**. An operational investigation may establish that no authorised aircraft or known balloon accounts for a report while still leaving an astronomical or observational explanation open.

## How definitions change the numbers

The simplest way to understand the mismatch is to imagine three different questions.

**How many unusual reports have been found in Apulia?**

A historical archive is best placed to answer this. Its total may be large because it combines contemporary and retrospective material, strong and weak cases, duplicates awaiting reconciliation, and reports later identified.

**How many reports entered the official state procedure?**

The Air Force archive answers this narrower question. Its total depends on witnesses using the formal reporting channel and on the case being processed and published.

**How many Apulian incidents remain genuinely difficult to explain?**

Neither raw total answers this by itself. That requires case-by-case evaluation of witness quality, timing, independent corroboration, photographs, radar or aviation data, astronomical conditions and alternative explanations.

These categories overlap, but they are not interchangeable. A newspaper clipping may belong in a historical catalogue but never have reached the Air Force. A single bright object seen from several towns may produce several private entries, one official incident or no formal record at all. A case identified as a meteor may remain in the private archive for research purposes but disappear from a list restricted to unresolved events.

Date rules also alter totals. Private investigators can add an incident decades later after discovering an old newspaper or interviewing an elderly witness. Official annual records normally reflect reports received and processed through the institutional system. A private catalogue is therefore cumulative and revision-friendly, whereas an annual government series is more closely tied to administrative intake.

Geography can create further discrepancies. A light seen from Bari, Brindisi and another region might be catalogued under each witness location, under the object’s apparent route, or as one multi-regional event. The 2009 Conversano entry, for example, explicitly covers observations across Apulia, Basilicata and Calabria. Whether that counts as one Apulian case, several local reports or one southern Italian event depends on the database design.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/OVNI-2010.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

Even the word “case” is unstable. It can mean a witness statement, an event, a file, a newspaper item or a completed investigation. Unless a source explains its unit of counting, comparisons can be misleading.

## Large totals are not stronger evidence

A regional archive approaching 1,000 entries is historically significant. It allows researchers to examine where reports concentrate, how waves develop and which descriptions recur. It can also recover incidents that would otherwise vanish with old newspapers or personal correspondence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/ufo-in-puglia-geolocalizzazione-degli-avvistamenti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciUFO in Puglia: geolocalizzazione degli avvistamenti - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

The total does not, however, measure the number of credible unexplained objects. CISU’s national data indicate that more than 70 per cent of catalogue entries are distant night lights, the class most vulnerable to mistakes involving stars, planets, aircraft and other luminous objects. CISU also states directly that only a small part of its complete collection remains unidentified after appropriate analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/ufo-in-italia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Ufo in Italia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciUfo in Italia - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

This changes how Apulia’s map should be read. It is best treated as a map of reported and archived aerial anomalies, not a map of confirmed mysteries. Its value lies in preserving source trails and permitting comparison. The decisive evidence remains inside each case file.

Nor should official status automatically be mistaken for exceptional quality. Some Air Force entries are brief, depend on estimates made by observers and contain no publicly presented radar or photographic support. An official classification establishes that the report entered a recognised procedure and was not matched to a known cause on the available information. It does not prove that the witness’s estimates of size, height or speed were accurate.

The official Bari entry of 2 September 1994, for example, records an oval white source, apparently moving rapidly towards the south-east at low altitude, seen by private citizens in clear weather. The formal table preserves useful facts, but the public summary alone does not show how distance or speed were determined, how many witnesses were interviewed, or whether astronomical and aviation alternatives were reconstructed in detail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1991-2000.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

Official and private records should therefore be regarded as different layers of evidence. The official record can establish institutional receipt and the outcome of particular checks. The private archive may supply press clippings, witness follow-up, local context and later identification proposals. Neither layer should be accepted uncritically.

## Where each system is strongest

Private archives are strongest at **historical reach**. They can search backwards, recover forgotten local reports and preserve material regardless of whether the witness followed a state procedure. In Apulia, this makes it possible to trace reports town by town across many decades and to examine the regional impact of national sighting waves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/ufo-in-puglia-geolocalizzazione-degli-avvistamenti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciUFO in Puglia: geolocalizzazione degli avvistamenti - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

Their weakness is uneven quality control. Sources can range from direct interviews to short press items, and older cases may lack original documentation. Collector enthusiasm also affects regional totals. Unless duplicate events, later explanations and evidential grades are clearly marked, the headline count can exaggerate the scale of the unresolved residue.

The Air Force system is strongest at **institutional checking**. It can consult military and aviation bodies, compare incidents with known flight activity and preserve a standardised official record. Reports from service personnel or law-enforcement witnesses may enter the same framework as civilian accounts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/en/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica MilitareOVNI - Aeronautica Militare...</span></span></span>

Its weakness is intake bias. The reporting route requires awareness, effort and contact with the Carabinieri. Many witnesses will instead speak to family, journalists or UFO groups, particularly when an event seems curious but not threatening. The official archive therefore measures formal reporting behaviour as much as the underlying frequency of unusual observations.

There is also a transparency limit. Publication of a table is useful, but a short result does not always expose the complete investigative file, interviews, calculations or correspondence behind the judgement. Readers should distinguish between a public summary and the full evidential record, which may be more extensive but not openly accessible.

## A better way to compare Apulian records

The most reliable comparison begins with matching individual events, not headline totals. A researcher should first determine whether a private and official entry refer to the same date, time and location. Only then is it meaningful to ask why one archive contains or reaches a different conclusion.

For each matched case, five questions are especially useful:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **What triggered inclusion?** Was it a formal report, a newspaper article, a direct interview or a later recollection?
2. **What is the counting unit?** Does one entry represent one witness, one locality or one physical event?
3. **Was the case investigated?** A source citation is not the same as a technical inquiry.
4. **Was an explanation recorded later?** Historical catalogues often retain solved cases, while unresolved-only lists may remove them.
5. **What primary material survives?** The strongest files include contemporary statements, exact timings, independent witnesses and relevant aviation or astronomical checks.

</div>

Applying these questions prevents two common errors. The first is to dismiss private collections because their numbers are high. Their breadth is precisely what makes them useful for regional history. The second is to treat official files as a complete census of everything seen over Apulia. The Air Force never claims to collect every informal story; it describes a formal procedure for reports submitted to it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/en/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica MilitareOVNI - Aeronautica Militare...</span></span></span>

## What the mismatch really tells us

The disagreement between Apulia’s UFO totals is not itself mysterious. It reflects different systems of governance. Private researchers seek to preserve the widest possible historical record. The Air Force seeks to assess reports relevant to flight and national safety through a defined administrative channel.

As a result, the regional private catalogue is much larger and more varied than the official list. That does not make it false, and it does not make every entry unexplained. The official archive is smaller and more selective, but it is not necessarily more complete or conclusive in every individual case.

For readers trying to judge Apulia’s UFO history, the useful distinction is not “private versus official” as though one must be trusted and the other rejected. It is **archive versus procedure**. The private archive shows what has been reported, remembered and preserved. The official record shows what entered a state investigation and what its available checks could or could not identify.

The strongest regional cases are those in which the two systems can be brought together: a well-preserved witness account, contemporary local documentation, a traceable official file and enough technical information to test ordinary explanations. Where those elements are absent, a high case count mainly demonstrates the richness of Apulia’s reporting history, not the presence of hundreds of unexplained aircraft.
