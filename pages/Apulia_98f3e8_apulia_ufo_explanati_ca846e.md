## Introduction

Most Apulian UFO reports are not descriptions of structured craft seen at close range. They are reports of distant lights whose size, altitude and speed were unknown to the observer. In those conditions, satellites, aircraft, lanterns, balloons, meteors, planets, reflections and, increasingly, drones can all appear stranger than they really are. Apulian investigator Arcangelo Cassano has said that recent cases largely reduce to coloured or white lights, often attributable to drones, satellites, lanterns or illuminated balloons; a 2026 regional report stated that roughly 90 per cent of the cases examined had a logical explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.telebari.it/cronaca/253890-ufo-in-puglia-online-la-piattaforma-degli-avvistamenti-il-90-per-cento-ha-una-spiegazione-logica-video.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: telebari.it">[Telebari]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">telebari.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ufo in Puglia, online la piattaforma degli avvistamenti25 May 2026 — Molti casi, come spiega Cassano, si rivelano droni, satellit...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2026</span></span></span>

This does not mean that every unexplained report is trivial or that witnesses are dishonest. It means that identification depends on information that is often missing: an exact time, viewing direction, duration, angular movement, weather conditions, original photographs and independent witnesses. Without those details, “unidentified” describes the limits of the record, not the discovery of an extraordinary vehicle.

## The most common mistaken identities

[Apulia]({{ 'why-apulias-ufo-record-still-resists/' | relative_url }})’s long Adriatic coastline, busy airspace, holiday resorts and broad rural horizons create excellent conditions for seeing distant lights. They also make those lights difficult to judge. Over dark sea or countryside there may be no buildings, hills or other familiar objects against which to estimate distance. A light five kilometres away and a bright object hundreds of kilometres away can therefore seem to occupy the same blank sky.

The regional case archive assembled by Cassano contains nearly 1,000 reports, with locations, dates, documentary sources and, where available, proposed identifications. The collection includes photographs or drawings for only some cases and spans material of very different quality, from contemporary records to newspaper summaries. Its value lies partly in showing how frequently familiar objects reappear under unfamiliar viewing conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici -Geo-localizzazione degli avvistamenti: l&#x27;esempio della Puglia. **Satellites and rocket activity.** A satellite usually appears as a steady point of light moving smoothly across the stars. It may brighten, fade or vanish when it enters the Earth’s shadow. Groups of newly launched Starlink satellites can form a striking procession, while rocket stages may produce expanding clouds, spirals or luminous plumes that look nothing like an ordinary spacecraft.

A particularly useful example occurred on 23 June 2024. Witnesses across southern and central Italy, including Apulia, reported an unusual luminous phenomenon over the Mediterranean. The Italian Centre for UFO Studies, CISU, compared the timing and path with the launch of Starlink Group 10-2. Space-tracking specialist Marco Langbroek identified the display as an exhaust cloud and fuel venting from the Falcon 9 upper stage during its de-orbit manoeuvre. Aerospace engineer Claudio Paris reached a similar conclusion, explaining that the low Sun illuminated the plume even though observers below were already in evening darkness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/il-flap-del-23-giugno-2024/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">il flap del 23 giugno 2024</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il flap del 23 giugno 2024June 24, 2024 — 24 Jun 2024 —... That incident demonstrates why a spectacular appearance is not, by itself, evidence against an ordinary explanation. A rocket-related cloud may expand, change colour and remain visible longer than an aircraft. It can also generate reports from many widely separated places, which may initially make the event seem more mysterious. In fact, that geographical spread is a clue: a phenomenon seen simultaneously from Apulia, Sicily, Greece and North Africa is probably very high and very distant rather than hovering over one locality.

**Aircraft.** Aeroplanes approaching head-on can appear almost stationary because most of their movement is towards the observer rather than across the field of view. Their landing lights may dominate until the aircraft turns, whereupon the light can dim abruptly or separate into red, green and white navigation lights. Flashing anti-collision lights add to the impression of changing shape or colour. Aircraft used in night operations carry conspicuous lighting systems precisely so that they can be seen, but from an unknown angle those lights do not always resemble the familiar outline of an aeroplane.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/Night_Ops_Ch13.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Aviation Administration Chapter 13: Night Operations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationChapter 13: Night OperationsMarch 8, 2019 — While anticollision lights are required for night visual fligh...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 8, 2019</span></span></span>

Apulia contains major civil and military aviation routes around Bari, Brindisi, Taranto and Foggia. That does not explain any particular report automatically, nor should “probably an aircraft” replace a proper check. Investigators need the precise time, direction and duration so that flight-tracking or airport information can be compared with the observation. A vague account written down days later may no longer contain enough detail to make that comparison.

**Lanterns, illuminated balloons and drones.** Sky lanterns rise slowly, drift with the wind and may seem to manoeuvre when different air currents carry them in slightly different directions. Several released together can form triangles or loose formations. Their flames flicker, brighten and disappear when the fuel is exhausted. Illuminated balloons can produce similar effects, especially during celebrations in coastal tourist areas.

Drones add a newer source of confusion. They can hover, reverse direction, change altitude and carry powerful or multicoloured lights. Unlike a distant aircraft, a drone may genuinely remain above one area for several minutes. Cassano has identified small drones, lanterns and illuminated balloons among recurring explanations for modern Apulian reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/author/admin/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">admin, Autore presso CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO sulla Puglia, Edizioni UPIAR). Una quar...</span></span></span> The difficulty is that recognising the category does not necessarily identify the operator. A report can therefore remain administratively unresolved even when its behaviour is fully compatible with an ordinary drone.

**Planets and bright stars.** Venus is one of the classic sources of UFO reports because it can appear exceptionally bright near the horizon, where atmospheric turbulence makes it shimmer or change colour. NASA’s Night Sky Network notes that amateur astronomers regularly receive questions from people who have mistaken Venus or another conspicuous celestial object for an unusual light.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nightsky.jpl.nasa.gov/news/39/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nightsky.jpl.nasa.gov">[Night Sky Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nightsky.jpl.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Night Sky NetworkIdentifying UFOs and UAPs - Night Sky Network - NASA1 Dec 2013 — If you&#x27;re an amateur astronomer, you are probably asked...</span></span></span>

A stationary light may also seem to drift when stared at against a dark, featureless background. This visual effect, called autokinesis, is well known in aviation: small involuntary eye movements can make an isolated star, planet or distant lamp appear to move.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faasafety.gov/files/events/SO/SO15/2024/SO15134204/YourSensesInTheShadows.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faasafety.gov">[FAA Safety]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faasafety.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">FAA Safety Your Senses in the Shadows</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nighttime Visual Illusions...January 24, 2025 — Autokinesis This illusion happens when a pilot stares at a bright, stationary light set...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 24, 2025</span></span></span> Over the Adriatic at night, where the horizon may offer few reliable reference points, the effect can be especially persuasive.

**Meteors and fireballs.** A bright meteor can produce an intense flash, fragmentation and a changing trail. ## How photographs and distance create errors

A photograph seems more objective than testimony, but a small light in a dark sky is one of the hardest subjects for a phone camera. The image usually contains no dependable scale, depth or horizon. A bright point may be enlarged by digital zoom, blurred by hand movement, distorted by poor focus or spread across several pixels by overexposure. The result can look like a disc, diamond, ring or multi-lobed object even when the source was a star or aircraft light.

Low-light phone photography is not a simple record of one instant. Modern night modes may capture several frames, align them and combine them to reduce noise. If the camera moves or the light changes position during that process, the final image can contain smearing, duplication or an altered shape. Research on mobile low-light imaging describes the need to compensate for hand movement, moving subjects, sensor noise and limited light, often by merging multiple exposures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11336" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Handheld Mobile Photography in Very Low Light</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXiv Handheld Mobile Photography in Very Low Light</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 24, 2019</span></span></span>

Internal reflections are another problem. A bright lamp, the Moon or a light just outside the frame can reflect between lens elements and produce a secondary spot elsewhere in the photograph. Technical research on smartphone flare describes these “ghost” images as reflections inside the lens system, often positioned in relation to the optical centre of the image.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15046" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 27, 2023</span></span></span> Such a spot can be mistaken for an object that the photographer did not notice at the time.

That distinction matters in Apulian archives because some recent reports begin only after an unusual mark is found in a photograph. CISU has noted this wider pattern in regional cataloguing: modern cases may involve an apparent UFO discovered during later inspection rather than something seen directly by the photographer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/author/admin/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">admin, Autore presso CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO sulla Puglia, Edizioni UPIAR). Sono quasi 1.000 i casi pugliesi per... lanterne cinesi o, più recentemente, piccoli droni. Una quar...</span></span></span> An image-only anomaly is evidentially weaker because there is no visual observation with which to compare its position, movement or appearance.

Video can help, but only when it preserves context. A tightly zoomed clip of a pulsating dot may reveal less than a wide shot containing the horizon, stars, buildings and ambient sound. Automatic focus repeatedly searching for a distant light can make the object appear to expand and contract. Camera shake can create sudden apparent manoeuvres. Compression applied by messaging platforms and social networks may remove the faint details needed to distinguish a fuselage, navigation lights or a meteor trail.

Distance produces a related problem. Witnesses often describe an object as “large”, “low” or “moving very fast”, but those judgements require a known distance. A light’s apparent size can be estimated, but its physical size cannot be calculated without knowing how far away it was. The same angular movement may represent a nearby drone travelling slowly or a distant aircraft travelling hundreds of kilometres per hour.

This is why responsible case analysis separates what was directly observed from what was inferred. “A white light crossed ten degrees of sky in twenty seconds” is potentially testable. “A huge craft accelerated over the coast” already assumes a size, distance and mechanism that the witness may not have been able to determine.

## Checking an Apulian mystery light

An ordinary explanation becomes persuasive when it matches several independent features rather than merely naming something that can fly. A useful investigation begins with the exact date and clock time, preferably recorded immediately. It then asks where the observer stood, which direction they faced, how high the light appeared, how long it remained visible and whether it passed fixed landmarks or known stars.

The strongest checks usually include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* **Astronomical position:** Was Venus, Jupiter, a bright star or the Moon in the reported direction?
* **Satellite and launch data:** Did the International Space Station, a Starlink train, a rocket stage or a re-entry cross the area at that time?
* **Air traffic:** Did a scheduled aircraft approach, depart or turn along the same line of sight?
* **Weather and wind:** Would lanterns, balloons, cloud reflections or airborne material have moved as described?
* **Meteor records:** Were fireballs reported by observers elsewhere along a compatible trajectory?
* **Original media:** Does the unedited file retain time, exposure and device information, and is the alleged object visible in consecutive frames?
* **Independent testimony:** Did other witnesses describe the same direction, timing and movement before seeing one another’s accounts?

</div>

The Italian Air Force’s formal procedure follows the same basic distinction between unexplained and extraordinary. Reports are submitted through the Carabinieri and assessed for technical or natural causes. Only when no such justification can be established is the episode classified as an unidentified flying object within the official system. The purpose is aviation and national security, not confirmation of an extraterrestrial interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span></span></span>

Official statistics and private regional catalogues should therefore not be compared as though they were identical samples. The Air Force records cases entering a specific institutional process. A regional archive may also preserve press cuttings, informal enquiries, retrospective memories, photographs and cases solved before they ever reached official channels. A high number of archive entries means that reports were collected, not that an equally high number resisted explanation.

## Why unidentified does not mean extraordinary

Some Apulian reports remain unresolved, but unresolved cases are not all of equal quality. A detailed, promptly recorded observation by several independent witnesses is different from a single sentence in a newspaper or an enlarged photograph with no original file. Both may be labelled “unidentified”, yet only the first offers much material for investigation.

There are three broad reasons why a case may retain that label.

**The evidence is insufficient.** The observer may not remember the precise time, or the report may contain no direction, duration or weather information. A likely aircraft or satellite cannot be confirmed because the details needed for comparison were never recorded. This is probably the most common route to a permanently unresolved file.

**Several ordinary explanations remain possible.** A distant orange light might have been a lantern, drone, aircraft seen through haze or celestial object distorted near the horizon. Investigators may be unable to choose confidently among them. That uncertainty does not make an exotic explanation more probable; it simply prevents a narrower identification.

**The report contains genuinely unusual features.** A small minority of cases describe movements, proximity or interactions that do not fit an obvious conventional source. These deserve careful preservation and renewed examination. Even then, extraordinary conclusions require stronger evidence than an unexplained narrative alone: original records, reliable timing, independent observations, radar or instrument data, and a clear chain of custody for photographs or physical traces.

The regional evidence currently points towards a large explained majority and a smaller, unevenly documented residue. The most useful lesson is therefore not that every mystery light has already been solved. It is that ordinary objects become difficult to recognise when seen briefly, remotely and without scale. Apulia’s skies contain aircraft, satellites, meteors, planets, balloons, lanterns and drones, all capable of producing sincere reports. Cases that survive those checks remain worth documenting, but their unresolved status measures the limits of the available evidence. It does not, on its own, tell us what crossed the sky.
