## Introduction

[Sicily]({{ 'why-sicilys-ufo-stories-still-resist/' | relative_url }})’s 1978 UFO wave was real as a reporting phenomenon, but not as evidence of one clearly defined aerial mystery. Regional researchers have catalogued 223 Sicilian reports for the year, far above the totals recorded during earlier local surges. The most important episodes included a bright fireball seen across much of the island in September, repeated reports by police and Carabinieri in December, a photographed light over Palermo, and several dramatic claims involving landed objects or humanoid figures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/2018/11/londata-del-1978-in-sicilia.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: L’ondata del 1978 in Sicilia...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Sicily_77b465_sicily_1978_ufo_wave_d9ca00-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for 1978 Wave" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The evidence, however, is sharply uneven. Some sightings had multiple trained witnesses or photographs, but still lacked the measurements needed to establish distance, size and movement. Others depended on children, isolated motorists or narratives published after UFO coverage had become intense. At least one large cluster was subsequently attributed to a meteor, showing why a high report count cannot be treated as a count of genuinely unexplained objects. Sicily’s wave matters chiefly because it reveals how unusual lights, sincere testimony, press attention and public expectation combined during Italy’s greatest modern UFO year.

## Where and when the reports clustered

The Sicilian wave was not evenly spread through 1978. Although cases were recorded throughout the year, the clearest concentrations came in September and December. The island was part of a much larger national surge: the Italian Centre for UFO Studies, or CISU, has catalogued more than 2,300 Italian reports from 1978, representing about 28 per cent of all cases then held in its historical database. Its press archive contains more than 2,200 newspaper cuttings from that year alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/2018/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: 2018...</span></span></span>

Sicily contributed 223 catalogued reports. For comparison, the same regional catalogue lists 95 in 1973, 63 in 1974 and 40 in 1977. Those figures depend on what investigators chose to record and should not be read as a scientifically controlled measurement of aerial activity. They nevertheless demonstrate an exceptional rise in public reporting and local newspaper coverage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/2018/11/londata-del-1978-in-sicilia.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: L’ondata del 1978 in Sicilia...</span></span></span>

The geographical spread was also broad. Reports came from the provinces of Palermo, Messina, Agrigento and Catania, while the event of 14 September generated twelve reports across seven of Sicily’s provinces. Such wide distribution can initially look impressive because it suggests independent witnesses observing the same phenomenon from different places. In this instance, however, the pattern helped point towards an astronomical explanation rather than an unknown craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/2018/11/londata-del-1978-in-sicilia.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: L’ondata del 1978 in Sicilia...</span></span></span>

December produced the most striking concentration of apparently stronger witnesses. Police officers and Carabinieri reported nocturnal lights on 11, 12, 19 and 20 December in several provinces. Among those named in the surviving regional account was Boris Giuliano, then head of Palermo’s flying squad and a highly experienced police investigator. A light photographed by the State Police over Palermo on 12 December became one of the best-known Sicilian images from the wave.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/2018/11/londata-del-1978-in-sicilia.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: L’ondata del 1978 in Sicilia...</span></span></span>

These clusters did not necessarily share one cause. A meteor may be visible across the whole island, while a bright planet, aircraft light or misjudged star can generate separate local reports. Media coverage can then encourage additional witnesses to look upwards, report ambiguous objects and interpret later lights through the same UFO framework. The wave is therefore better understood as several overlapping episodes than as a single prolonged incursion.

## The September fireball showed how a wave could form

The twelve reports from 14 September are among the most useful cases because they demonstrate the difference between “unidentified to the witness” and permanently unexplained. Observers in seven provinces reported a conspicuous luminous phenomenon. At the time, speculation included the atmospheric return of the Pegasus satellite, but later Sicilian summaries identify the source as a bolide: an exceptionally bright meteor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/2018/11/londata-del-1978-in-sicilia.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: L’ondata del 1978 in Sicilia...</span></span></span>

A fireball can create exactly the features that make a UFO cluster appear persuasive. It may be visible over hundreds of kilometres, change brightness, fragment into several points and seem much lower or slower than it really is. Witnesses separated by considerable distances can consequently provide sincere but inconsistent estimates of direction, shape, altitude and speed. Modern meteor research illustrates how accurate identification normally requires timed observations, camera records and trajectory reconstruction rather than visual impressions alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.09555" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A Case Study of the May 30th, 2017 Italian FireballNovember 21, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 21, 2019</span></span></span>

The original satellite theory was not absurd. Re-entering spacecraft can also produce slow, fragmented fireballs visible across large areas, and distinguishing natural meteors from artificial re-entries requires reliable timing and orbital information. But the significant point for the Sicilian wave is that one spectacular event produced twelve separate UFO reports. Counting those reports without grouping them would exaggerate the number of independent phenomena.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01004" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Using fireball networks to track more frequent reentries: Falcon 9 upper stage orbit determination from video recordingsSeptember 2...</span></span></span>

This episode also warns against treating geographical breadth as proof of extraordinary technology. A light reported from seven provinces sounds stronger than a solitary sighting, but wide visibility is expected from a high-altitude meteor. In evidential terms, the September cluster is important precisely because it was broadly witnessed and still had a conventional explanation.

## The December police sightings were the strongest reports

The reports involving police and Carabinieri deserve more weight than many other Sicilian cases, though not because officers are incapable of error. Their value lies in the number of witnesses, the recurrence over several nights and the existence of at least one photograph associated with the State Police. According to the CISU Sicily catalogue, observations occurred on 11, 12, 19 and 20 December across the provinces of Palermo, Messina, Agrigento and Catania.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/2018/11/londata-del-1978-in-sicilia.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: L’ondata del 1978 in Sicilia...</span></span></span>

The best-known episode occurred over Palermo on 12 December. The photograph strengthens the documentary record but is less decisive than it may seem. A single image of a light generally cannot establish whether the source was close and small, distant and large, moving rapidly or nearly stationary. Without the original negative, exposure settings, lens details, exact viewing direction and comparison frames, photographic enlargement may add apparent structure that was not resolved by the camera. Modern proposals for serious aerial-phenomena research consequently emphasise simultaneous wide-field and narrow-field cameras, triangulation, spectral data and independent sensors rather than isolated photographs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18566" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Scientific Investigation of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP) Using Multimodal Ground-Based ObservatoriesMay 29, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 29, 2023</span></span></span>

No publicly accessible radar track, measured trajectory or official technical finding has emerged that turns the Palermo photograph into proof of an unconventional aircraft. The surviving account is therefore stronger as evidence that something was genuinely observed than as evidence of what that something was. It remains one of the wave’s better cases because it combines named professional witnesses and photographic documentation, but its unresolved appearance largely reflects missing data.

## The most dramatic close encounters were also the weakest

Sicilian researchers list fourteen alleged third-kind encounters for 1978, including borderline cases whose connection with conventional UFO reporting is uncertain. These accounts supplied the wave’s most memorable stories, but they generally lack the independent documentation found in the December police sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/2018/11/londata-del-1978-in-sicilia.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: L’ondata del 1978 in Sicilia...</span></span></span>

One of the best known was reported on the evening of 4 July near Monte Sona, an extinct crater on Etna. Four people were said to have observed lights on the ground and several entities. Later retellings describe a landed disc and humanoid figures, but readily available summaries do not provide a complete contemporaneous investigation file, physical measurements, photographs or independently preserved statements from each witness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://qds.it/extraterrestri-sicilia-cartoline-dal-1978-etna-teatro-avvistamenti-misteri/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: qds.it">[qds.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">qds.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">extraterrestri sicilia cartoline dal 1978 etna teatro avvistamenti misteri</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">extraterrestri sicilia cartoline dal 1978 etna teatro avvistamenti misteri</span></span></span>

The setting complicates interpretation. Monte Sona already had symbolic importance for followers of Catania contactee Eugenio [Siragusa]({{ 'siragusa/' | relative_url }}), who associated the area with his own alleged extraterrestrial experiences. That does not disprove the four witnesses’ account, but it raises the possibility that local expectations influenced how uncertain lights or figures were interpreted. The case therefore carries cultural and historical interest while remaining weak as physical evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://qds.it/extraterrestri-sicilia-cartoline-dal-1978-etna-teatro-avvistamenti-misteri/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: qds.it">[QdS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">qds.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">extraterrestri sicilia cartoline dal 1978 etna teatro avvistamenti misteri</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">extraterrestri sicilia cartoline dal 1978 etna teatro avvistamenti misteri</span></span></span>

Two more extraordinary stories were reported on 15 December. In Catania, three children reportedly saw a multicoloured object hovering above the ground. They said that a hatch-like opening emitted a beam, down which a being descended and remained suspended roughly ten centimetres above the surface, while a larger figure appeared in the opening. Near Licodia Eubea, a lorry driver claimed to see a luminous object land and two giant beings attempt to communicate in an incomprehensible language.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/2018/11/londata-del-1978-in-sicilia.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: L’ondata del 1978 in Sicilia...</span></span></span>

These claims are vivid but evidentially fragile. The Catania report rests on children’s recollections under circumstances in which misunderstanding, imaginative elaboration and questioning by adults may be especially influential. The Licodia Eubea narrative appears to depend chiefly on one witness, with no widely available photograph, trace analysis or independent observation of the alleged beings. Neither case has developed the corroborating record needed to separate an extraordinary encounter from misperception, storytelling or later amplification.

The timing also matters. By December, UFO stories were appearing frequently in newspapers, while the Italian release of *Close Encounters of the Third Kind*, Joseph Allen Hynek’s visit and extensive publication of humanoid cases had made the imagery of landed craft and beings unusually familiar. ## Why the report count does not prove one cause

The 223 Sicilian entries represent reported experiences, press stories and cases collected by investigators. They are not 223 verified objects. One astronomical event could generate many reports, while a single newspaper story might produce follow-up testimony, duplicated accounts or retrospective claims. Catalogue totals are valuable for studying when and where public attention surged, but they cannot be used alone to measure the number of unexplained physical events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/2018/11/londata-del-1978-in-sicilia.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: L’ondata del 1978 in Sicilia...</span></span></span>

Several processes probably operated at once:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--example" markdown="1">

* **Ordinary celestial and atmospheric phenomena:** the September fireball is the clearest demonstrated example. Bright planets, stars seen through broken cloud, meteors and unusual atmospheric effects can also seem to move or change colour when observed without reference points.
* **Aircraft and artificial objects:** Sicily’s airports, military activity and busy Mediterranean air routes supplied many potential moving lights. Satellite re-entry was considered during the September episode, illustrating how natural and artificial explanations could compete.
* **Expectation and repeated observation:** sustained newspaper and television coverage encouraged people to watch the sky and to report sights they might otherwise have ignored.
* **Case multiplication:** one widely visible event could be entered as numerous geographical reports, making a cluster appear to contain more independent phenomena than it did.
* **Genuinely unresolved observations:** some reports remain difficult to identify because descriptions are incomplete or because no timely investigation recovered the necessary data.

</div>

The media did not simply create the wave from nothing. Police officers, children, motorists and other Sicilians clearly reported experiences they considered unusual. Yet media attention affected which experiences were noticed, how they were described and which stories newspapers selected for publication. CISU’s researchers have consequently characterised the national episode not only as a sighting wave but also as a uniquely powerful media phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.euroufo.net/2018/05/15/project1978/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: euroufo.net">[Euroufo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">euroufo.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The 1978 Great UFO Wave, 40 years later &#124; EuroUFO...</span></span></span>

The film *Close Encounters of the Third Kind* reached Italian audiences in 1978, Hynek visited the country, UFO books sold strongly and newspapers gave unusual prominence to sightings and humanoid encounters. The close match between the stories receiving maximum publicity and the imagery circulating in popular culture is relevant, particularly when assessing the late-year entity reports. It is evidence of a social feedback loop, although not proof that any individual witness was dishonest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/2018/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: 2018...</span></span></span>

## Which reports still deserve attention?

The December police observations, particularly the Palermo photograph, remain the strongest Sicilian reports of the wave. They involved professional witnesses, occurred within a multi-location cluster and left more documentation than most close-encounter narratives. Their limitations are equally important: the publicly available record lacks the calibrated photography, triangulation, radar confirmation and original technical analysis needed to determine the lights’ distance or performance.

The September fireball is the most instructive resolved episode. It shows that a spectacular, island-wide UFO cluster can arise from one natural event, and that initial claims about a falling satellite or unknown craft may change after comparison of reports. Far from being peripheral, explained cases are essential to understanding how the wider wave grew.

Monte Sona, the Catania children’s encounter and the Licodia Eubea lorry-driver case are the most dramatic claims, but not the best evidence. They survive mainly as narratives, with limited accessible primary documentation and little independent physical support. Later repetition has kept them prominent without materially strengthening their factual foundation.

Sicily’s 1978 wave is therefore best described as historically exceptional but evidentially mixed. It contains one clearly instructive misidentification cluster, several respectable yet under-documented observations and a group of extraordinary encounter stories that cannot now be tested adequately. The surviving material supports the conclusion that Sicily experienced an intense wave of reporting and public concern. It does not establish that the island was visited by a single unknown craft, still less by extraterrestrial beings.
