## Introduction

[Sardinia]({{'what-makes-sardinias-ufo-record-so/' | relative_url }})’s UFO archive is not a single public vault with every case neatly verified. It is a patchwork kept alive by national UFO groups, regional investigators, local newspapers, social media pages, private files, and Italy’s official Air Force reporting route. That patchwork matters because many Sardinian cases would otherwise disappear after a short news item or a witness post. It also creates a problem: the same record can contain useful witness detail, uncertain photographs, repeated anecdotes, and cases that later look like aircraft, satellites, flares or other ordinary causes. The best way to read Sardinia’s UFO history is therefore neither to dismiss the archives nor to treat them as proof. They are evidence trails: starting points for checking dates, places, witnesses, media coverage and possible explanations.<img src="{{"/assets/images/Sardinia_83a1a0_local_investigators_ddda8a-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Local Archives" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
## Who actually keeps Sardinia’s UFO record?

The main institutional archive in Italy is not a UFO club but the Italian Air Force’s [official reporting]({{'official-reports/' | relative_url }}) system. Since the wave of sightings in 1978, the Air Force has been the national body designated to collect, verify and monitor reports of unidentified flying objects. Its current process asks witnesses to submit a reporting form through the nearest Carabinieri station, after which a technical assessment can look for correlations with human activity or natural phenomena. Only after checks are completed are cases published, and a case is classed as unidentified when no technical or natural explanation has been found.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica MilitareOVNI - Aeronautica Militare...</span></span></span>

That official channel is important for Sardinia because the island has airports, coastal traffic and military airspace, so an unusual light can be relevant to flight safety even when it has no exotic explanation. But the Air Force archive is not the only place where Sardinian sightings survive. In practice, much of the island’s UFO memory is preserved by private investigators and organisations that collect testimony much faster than an official system can.

Two national groups matter most for the Sardinian record: the Centro Ufologico Nazionale, usually shortened to CUN, and the Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici, usually shortened to CISU. CUN’s public Sardinia page offers a compact catalogue of “principal sightings” from 1954 to 1998, beginning with several reports during the major 1954 Italian UFO wave: Villamassargia, Sorso, Torralba, Sassari and Tavolara all appear in a few days of late October.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/sardegna.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionale</span></span></span> Its national statistics pages also gather annual case tables, provincial data and longer historical datasets, so CUN functions as both a reporting network and a public-facing archive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionale Tabelle Statistiche Avvistamenti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionale Tabelle Statistiche Avvistamenti</span></span></span>

CISU has a different public identity: it describes itself as a private, non-profit cultural association focused on rational and evidence-oriented UFO study. Its stated aims include investigating reports, promoting scientific study, circulating information, coordinating witness collection and preserving Italian UFO documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufo.it/536-2/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo.it">[UFO.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cosa è il CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cosa è il CISU</span></span></span> For Sardinia, CISU is especially important because it has named local representatives who gather reports, digitise material and keep regional cases visible beyond the short life of a newspaper article.<img src="{{"/assets/images/Sardinia_83a1a0_local_investigators_ddda8a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Local Archives illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Sardinia_83a1a0_local_investigators_ddda8a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Sardinia_83a1a0_local_investigators_ddda8a-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## CUN, CISU and local reporting networks

The Sardinian record shows how national archives and local fieldwork depend on each other. A national catalogue gives continuity: it lets a reader see that Sardinian reports stretch from the 1950s into the present, and it places an island case beside comparable reports from elsewhere in Italy. Local investigators add a different value: they receive phone calls, images, videos, drawings and witness descriptions soon after an event, often before there is any formal file.

The clearest example is Antonio Maria Cuccu, listed by CISU as its regional coordinator for Sardinia. CISU says Cuccu joined the organisation in 1987, became regional coordinator the following year, conducted many investigations into Sardinian sightings, and created a broad regional archive of witness reports. It also notes that since 2014 he has written Sardinian UFO chronicle items through the UFOSardegna_news Facebook page.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/antonio-cuccu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Antonio Cuccu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Antonio Cuccu</span></span></span> Local press profiles describe him as a visible Sardinian point of contact, receiving reports through Facebook, WhatsApp and other channels, while deciding not to publish everything that reaches him.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lanuovasardegna.it/regione/2024/03/12/news/il-cacciatore-di-ufo-nei-cieli-della-sardegna-ho-raccolto-migliaia-di-segnalazioni-1.100488525" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lanuovasardegna.it">[La Nuova Sardegna]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lanuovasardegna.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on lanuovasardegna.it.</span></span></span>

## Newspapers turn private files into public memory

Local newspapers and news sites are not neutral scientific archives, but in Sardinia they are often the public bridge between a witness and a case record. A sighting may first appear on an investigator’s page, then be picked up by a local outlet, then enter a national or regional UFO catalogue. This chain gives the case visibility, but it also means readers should distinguish between a reported claim and a verified event.

La Nuova Sardegna’s 2024 profile of Cuccu is useful because it describes the mechanics of collection rather than just repeating a dramatic sighting. It says he catalogues episodes chronologically, uses Facebook as an immediate channel for testimony, and receives material from witnesses including motorists on provincial roads and the SS131. Cuccu is quoted as saying he collects reported cases and gives news of them with photo or video material, but not everything that arrives is published.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lanuovasardegna.it/regione/2024/03/12/news/il-cacciatore-di-ufo-nei-cieli-della-sardegna-ho-raccolto-migliaia-di-segnalazioni-1.100488525" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lanuovasardegna.it">[La Nuova Sardegna]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lanuovasardegna.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on lanuovasardegna.it.</span></span></span>

The same paper’s 2026 interview shows how large a private archive can become in local hands. It describes Cuccu’s files as the product of about forty years of study, and says his archives include more than a thousand videos and images. The article’s opening case, from Villaperuccio in the Sulcis area on 16 March 2026, is a good example of the modern pattern: a witness sees lights, initially considers a satellite, then reports a multiplication of lights and coordinated movement; the investigator takes notes by phone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lanuovasardegna.it/regione/2026/04/18/news/parla-l-esperto-di-dischi-volanti-la-mia-idea-puntare-sul-turismo-ufologico-nell-isola-si-puo-1.100858560" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lanuovasardegna.it">[La Nuova Sardegna]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lanuovasardegna.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on lanuovasardegna.it.</span></span></span>

Other local outlets show how quickly a sighting can become public before any firm conclusion exists. In October 2021, Cagliaripad reported a bright stationary white light near Sorso, said to have intensified, moved downward and vanished. The article noted that experts excluded a satellite but considered a signal rocket or something still unidentified, while seeking further witnesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cagliaripad.it/550108/sorso-segnalato-un-oggetto-luminoso-non-identificato-e-un-ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cagliaripad.it">[Cagliaripad]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cagliaripad.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sorso, segnalato un oggetto luminoso non identificato: è un UFO?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sorso, segnalato un oggetto luminoso non identificato: è un UFO?</span></span></span> That wording is valuable because it does not jump straight to an extraordinary conclusion; it shows the intermediate stage where a case is neither solved nor strong.

A March 2022 Sardegna Live report on Sassari sightings illustrates another common archive problem: a witness may describe a structured object in vivid terms, while the available video shows only a bright light. In that case, the witness described a luminous form “like a jellyfish”, but Cuccu noted that the phone video had not zoomed in and showed only an intense light source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sardegnalive.net/in-sardegna/ufo-avvistamenti-a-sassari-un-testimone-aveva-una-forma-come-la-medusa-px29dvel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sardegnalive.net">[Sardegna Live]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sardegnalive.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sardegnalive.net.</span></span></span> For an archive, that difference matters. ## Why the 2022 southern Sardinia cluster is a useful archive test

The early March 2022 reports around Monastir, San Sperate, Assemini, Serdiana, Uta and nearby areas are one of the best examples of how Sardinian investigators build a case cluster rather than a single anecdote. CISU described an ongoing Sardinian “flap”, meaning a wave of reports over several days, but also warned that it could only judge whether the phenomena were unexplained after studying the reports and looking for rational explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/tag/antonio-cuccu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Antonio Cuccu Archivi</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Antonio Cuccu Archivi</span></span></span>

The case list is revealing because it contains the raw ingredients of local UFO archiving: precise dates and times, locations, direction of movement, witness context, photographs, drawings, and repeated motifs. Reports included a flat circle with green flashing light near Monastir on 22 February, small white spheres near Sant’Andrea Frius on 24 February, a “jellyfish” comparison connected to Sassari and Assemini, circular lights seen from roads near San Sperate, and several reports around 2–4 March involving green or blue lights, helicopters, low altitude impressions and motorists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/tag/antonio-cuccu/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Antonio Cuccu Archivi</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Antonio Cuccu Archivi</span></span></span>

## How to read private Sardinian UFO records cautiously

The most useful attitude is disciplined curiosity. Sardinia’s local UFO archives are worth reading because they preserve witness voices, regional patterns and details that official summaries may omit. They are also fragile evidence because many entries begin as personal recollections, phone calls, social media posts, low-resolution videos or newspaper stories written before investigation is complete.

A cautious reader should ask four questions.

**First, what is the source of the claim?** A case in the Air Force system, a CISU investigation note, a CUN catalogue entry, a local newspaper article and a Facebook post are not equivalent. They may all be useful, but they carry different levels of checking and different incentives. The official Air Force process is designed around technical assessment and flight safety; private investigators may gather richer witness detail; newspapers make cases public but can simplify uncertainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica MilitareOVNI - Aeronautica Militare...</span></span></span>

## Why Sardinia’s archives still matter

Sardinia’s UFO archives matter less as a proof-machine and more as a memory system. They keep track of who reported what, where and when; they preserve older cases that might otherwise survive only as folklore; and they create a basis for comparing local patterns across decades. Without CUN’s historical listings, CISU’s regional collection, Air Force records and local press coverage, Sardinia’s UFO history would become a loose set of rumours rather than a traceable regional record.

The strongest archive practice is the one that keeps uncertainty visible. A good Sardinian UFO file should not merely say “unidentified”; it should show why it remained unidentified, what ordinary causes were considered, what evidence exists, and what evidence is missing. In that sense, private investigators and local archives are at their best when they slow the story down: from spectacle to testimony, from testimony to documentation, and from documentation to a careful judgement about whether the case is unresolved, weak, explained or still worth investigating.
