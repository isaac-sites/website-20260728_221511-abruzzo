## Introduction

[Abruzzo]({{ 'why-abruzzo-became-italys-ufo-hotspot/' | relative_url }})’s fishermen were important witnesses during the central Adriatic sightings of autumn 1978, but their testimony is stronger as evidence of a genuine coastal alarm than as proof of extraordinary craft. Experienced mariners reportedly described lights near the water, sudden mist, disturbed seas, unusual currents and apparent instrument problems. Their familiarity with local conditions deserves respect: these were working observers who routinely navigated at night and depended on recognising weather, vessels and navigation lights.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Abruzzo_4c71bb_fishermen_testimony_ab93e4-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Witnesses" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Yet the surviving record rarely preserves the details needed to test what they saw. Precise positions, observation times, bearings, distances, weather readings, instrument logs and independently recorded statements are generally absent. Later accounts also merge different witnesses, dates and phenomena into an increasingly dramatic narrative. The most defensible conclusion is therefore that fishermen encountered events they could not readily identify, while the available documentation remains too fragmented to establish whether those events shared one cause—or involved anything genuinely anomalous.

## What coastal witnesses reportedly described

Fishing communities between northern Abruzzo and the southern Marche supplied many of the reports that drove public concern in October and November 1978. Accounts associated with Martinsicuro, Giulianova, Tortoreto, Ortona and waters farther north described reddish or violet lights, luminous bodies apparently close to the surface, localised mist, unusual noises and patches of agitated or “boiling” sea. Some retellings added large columns of water, abrupt currents, malfunctioning compasses and unexplained returns on fishing-boat radar.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rete8.it/cronaca/378quando-gli-ufo-invasero-labruzzo-ottobre-78/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rete8.it">[Rete8]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rete8.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">378quando gli ufo invasero labruzzo ottobre 78</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1978: quando gli UFO &quot;invasero&quot; l&#x27;Abruzzo - Rete8...</span></span></span>

One frequently repeated account concerns fisherman Antonio Pallesca, who was said to have observed a large luminous globe that appeared to follow his boat. Regional reporting later connected his story with other fishermen who remembered red lights over the sea and anxiety about leaving harbour. However, the accessible retellings do not provide a contemporary signed statement, exact course, estimated range, duration, compass bearing or full crew list. The account is therefore useful for reconstructing the mood of the period, but difficult to analyse as a discrete observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rete8.it/cronaca/378quando-gli-ufo-invasero-labruzzo-ottobre-78/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rete8.it">[Rete8]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rete8.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">378quando gli ufo invasero labruzzo ottobre 78</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1978: quando gli UFO &quot;invasero&quot; l&#x27;Abruzzo - Rete8...</span></span></span>

A better-documented incident occurred on 9 November, when the crew of the patrol boat CP 2018 reportedly observed a pale red light rising from the sea at an angle. A later reconstruction quotes the radio description as resembling a luminous flare and lasting about four seconds. The crew found no boat or person at the apparent point of origin. This is more specific than many fishermen’s stories, but it still leaves ordinary possibilities open, including a flare viewed at an uncertain distance, pyrotechnics, an astronomical object briefly exposed through cloud, or another light whose apparent movement was distorted by the observer’s own vessel.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.crprato.it/sito/index.php?Itemid=329&amp;id=2370&amp;option=com_content&amp;view=article" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: crprato.it">[Centro Ricerche Prato]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">crprato.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dalle comunicazioni VHF L&#x27;ufficiale comunica via radio ”..Sembra un razzo...Read more...</span></span></span>

Later articles often package these reports into a single night of extraordinary activity. Contemporary chronology appears less tidy. Some stories are linked to 14–15 October, others to early or mid-November, and the parliamentary record refers broadly to sustained concern rather than one decisive encounter. Treating every light, wave, patch of fog and instrument complaint as part of one event makes the case appear more coherent than the surviving evidence permits.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/documenti/attiparlamentari.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionale Le interrogazioni parlamentari i</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionale Le interrogazioni parlamentari i</span></span></span>

## Why local experience matters

Fishermen were not random observers unfamiliar with the sea. Their livelihoods required them to judge wind, swell, current, visibility, vessel lights and the changing appearance of the coast. A mariner who had spent years working the same waters would have been better placed than most visitors to notice something genuinely unusual.

That experience gives particular weight to reports of abnormal sea behaviour. An unexpected current, unusually concentrated disturbance or sudden reduction in visibility could affect navigation directly. Fishermen also had practical reasons to report hazards even when they had no theory about their cause. The Defence Ministry’s later response acknowledged “vivid concern” among fishermen between San Benedetto del Tronto and Pedaso and confirmed that minesweepers and patrol vessels were initially sent to investigate. The Navy subsequently conducted 20 surveillance missions amounting to 230 hours at sea.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/documenti/attiparlamentari.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionale Le interrogazioni parlamentari i</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionale Le interrogazioni parlamentari i</span></span></span>

Experience, however, is specialised rather than universal. A fisherman may be highly skilled at recognising local weather and normal maritime traffic without being able to identify every astronomical, atmospheric, military or optical phenomenon. Night-time observation at sea is especially difficult because an isolated light has few reference points. Without a visible horizon, known distance or fixed background, observers can misjudge a light’s size, speed, direction and height.

Modern marine-safety findings illustrate the broader problem. Restricted visibility can quickly damage orientation and situational awareness, increase stress and affect decision-making even among trained crews. Navigation instruments are valuable, but investigators also emphasise that crews must understand their limitations and compare instrument information with other evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/news/red-falcon-and-greylag-report-published" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Falcon and Greylag report published</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">&gt; Our investigation highlighted how quickly restricted visibility can negatively affect individuals’ awareness and orientation, which inc...</span></span></span>

Fishermen’s expertise therefore strengthens the claim that something seemed outside their normal experience. It does not, by itself, identify the cause. The appropriate evidential question is not whether experienced mariners could ever be mistaken, but whether their observations were recorded in enough detail to distinguish among competing [explanations]({{ 'explanations/' | relative_url }}).

## What the official response confirms

The surviving parliamentary material is the strongest evidence that the fishermen’s concern was real and taken seriously. On 20 November 1978, MP Falco Accame asked the government about alarm in Abruzzo and the Marche following unexplained phenomena reported by the press and allegedly seen by a naval patrol. He requested further investigation and measures to reassure the affected population.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/documenti/attiparlamentari.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionale Le interrogazioni parlamentari i</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionale Le interrogazioni parlamentari i</span></span></span>

Defence Minister Attilio Ruffini replied on 29 January 1979 that the authorities had been informed of serious concern among fishermen in the San Benedetto del Tronto–Pedaso area. Minesweepers and patrol boats were deployed, followed by the organised surveillance programme of 20 missions. The crucial sentence in the response is that nothing unusual was detected during those operations. Surveillance ended on 21 November as public anxiety subsided.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/documenti/attiparlamentari.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionale Le interrogazioni parlamentari i</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionale Le interrogazioni parlamentari i</span></span></span>

This finding neither proves that every report had a conventional explanation nor shows that the witnesses invented their experiences. Patrols beginning after reports had accumulated could easily miss short-lived events. They may also have operated under different weather, traffic or sea conditions. Nevertheless, 230 hours of unsuccessful observation weakens claims of a persistent physical source regularly producing spectacular columns of water, instrument failures or luminous objects in the area.

The official record is also narrower than later folklore. It documents concern, deployment and a negative result. It does not validate the more dramatic claims found in anniversary articles, such as enormous water walls, systematic compass failures, underwater vehicles or a common mechanism connecting every report.

## The details that prevent firm verification

A strong maritime sighting report should allow investigators to reconstruct where the observer was, where the phenomenon appeared and what else was happening nearby. Most surviving fishermen’s accounts from 1978 fail this test because they reach the present through retrospective journalism, television programmes or UFO literature rather than accessible original case files.

The principal gaps are:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Exact time and position.** A date without a precise time, coordinates or distance from shore cannot be reliably compared with shipping movements, celestial objects, military activity or weather records.
* **Bearing and elevation.** “Over the sea” could describe something close to the surface, near the horizon or high in the sky. A compass bearing and angular height would sharply narrow the possibilities.
* **Duration and movement.** Accounts need to distinguish seconds from minutes and apparent motion from changes caused by the fishing boat’s own course, pitch and roll.
* **Weather and visibility.** Cloud, haze, fog, moonlight and the condition of the horizon materially affect the appearance of distant lights.
* **Independent testimony.** Crew members should ideally be interviewed separately and promptly. Statements repeated after newspaper coverage or harbour discussion are more vulnerable to unconscious convergence.
* **Instrument records.** Claims of radar contacts or compass disturbance require settings, timestamps, maintenance history and, where available, recorded traces. A witness remembering that an instrument “went mad” is not equivalent to a preserved technical anomaly.

</div>

Research on eyewitness evidence more generally shows why early, verbatim documentation matters. Human perception and memory are reconstructive rather than perfect recordings, and later information can alter both recollection and confidence. This does not imply dishonesty; sincere witnesses can become more certain as a shared narrative develops around an initially ambiguous event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/18891/chapter/8" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalacademies.org">[nationalacademies.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalacademies.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The committee further recommends that the Initiative support research to better understand the following: (1) the variables that affect t...</span></span></span>

The Abruzzo–Marche reports are particularly vulnerable to that process. Fishermen discussed the events within close working communities, journalists arrived while anxiety was high, and later programmes revisited the story through the established idea of an “[Adriatic Triangle]({{ 'triangle-legend/' | relative_url }})”. Details reported by one witness could consequently migrate into summaries of the entire wave.

## Natural explanations remain possible, but unproven

Several conventional mechanisms could account for parts of the testimony without explaining every report in the same way. Flares, fishing lights, coastal beacons, aircraft, bright astronomical objects and distant vessels can all appear strange when viewed across a dark, featureless horizon. Mist or low cloud can diffuse light, alter colour and make an object appear larger or closer than it is.

Sudden fog and localised poor visibility are also credible maritime hazards. Their presence would not explain every reported light, but they could account for disorientation, apparent changes in motion and some claims of unexpectedly hazardous conditions. Modern accident investigations demonstrate that rapidly restricted visibility can impair even experienced crews’ orientation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gov.uk/government/news/red-falcon-and-greylag-report-published" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: GOV.UK">[GOV.UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">GOV.UK</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Red Falcon and Greylag report published</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">&gt; Our investigation highlighted how quickly restricted visibility can negatively affect individuals’ awareness and orientation, which inc...</span></span></span>

Gas seepage has become a popular explanation in later accounts. Methane seeps and gas-related seabed features are scientifically documented in parts of the Adriatic, including central and northern sectors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.italianjournalofgeosciences.it/297/article-989/the-origin-of-gas-seeps-in-the-northern-adriatic-sea.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: italianjournalofgeosciences.it">[Italian Journal of Geosciences]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">italianjournalofgeosciences.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on italianjournalofgeosciences.it.</span></span></span> However, that geological fact does not validate the specific claim that methane produced the Abruzzo lights, large water columns or apparent instrument failures in autumn 1978. One anniversary article presents escaping gas and mud as the established solution, but it does not identify contemporary samples, measurements, survey reports or a traceable investigation demonstrating that mechanism at the reported times and locations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.italiani.it/ufo-1978-la-notte-degli-avvistamenti-in-abruzzo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.italiani.it">[italiani.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.italiani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">it UF Os in Abruzzo: Forty Years Ago, the Night of the Sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">it UF Os in Abruzzo: Forty Years Ago, the Night of the Sightings</span></span></span>

The gas hypothesis is therefore plausible only at a general level. It should not be presented as a confirmed explanation for the fishermen’s observations. The same caution applies to waterspouts, unusual waves and other atmospheric or marine processes: each may explain a portion of the testimony, but the necessary event-specific data are missing.

## How later reporting changed the story

Later reporting preserved the fishermen’s voices, but it also made the episode more dramatic and less precise. Regional retrospectives openly acknowledge that exaggeration and imagination entered some versions, yet repeat claims about boiling water, huge columns, failing radar and frightened bodies without consistently separating contemporary documentation from harbour lore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rete8.it/cronaca/378quando-gli-ufo-invasero-labruzzo-ottobre-78/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rete8.it">[Rete8]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rete8.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">378quando gli ufo invasero labruzzo ottobre 78</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1978: quando gli UFO &quot;invasero&quot; l&#x27;Abruzzo - Rete8...</span></span></span>

The deaths of brothers Vittorio and Gianfranco De Fulgentiis in the October 1978 sinking of their small fishing vessel became especially entangled with the UFO narrative. The loss itself is part of local maritime history, and modern reporting still records the two brothers among Martinsicuro’s fishing fatalities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ilcentro.it/teramo/per-martinsicuro-%C3%A8-la-27%C2%AA-vittima-dal-1948-1.3327422" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilcentro.it">[il Centro]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilcentro.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">per martinsicuro è la 27ª vittima dal 1948 1.3327422</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">per martinsicuro è la 27ª vittima dal 1948 1.3327422</span></span></span> Claims that the sinking was caused by unidentified phenomena, or that post-mortem findings proved an extraordinary death, survive mainly in later mystery-oriented accounts. No accessible official casualty investigation, medical report or contemporary technical finding has emerged to substantiate that connection. The tragedy should therefore not be treated as evidence for the sightings.

Later coverage has neither decisively debunked nor strengthened the fishermen’s core testimony. It has strengthened the historical case that coastal communities were genuinely alarmed and that numerous mariners reported unusual experiences. At the same time, embellishment, repetition and the loss of original records have weakened the evidential value of the individual stories.

## The fairest credibility assessment

Abruzzo’s fishermen should not be dismissed as fantasists simply because their observations became part of UFO folklore. Their reports prompted real concern and a substantial naval response. Their professional familiarity with the central Adriatic makes their perception that conditions were unusual historically significant.

Their testimony nevertheless falls short of proving a structured craft, an underwater vehicle or a single extraordinary phenomenon. The Navy’s surveillance detected nothing abnormal, most accounts lack reconstructable observational data, and later retellings often blur first-hand evidence with speculation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/documenti/attiparlamentari.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionale Le interrogazioni parlamentari i</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionale Le interrogazioni parlamentari i</span></span></span>

The most balanced classification is **credible concern supported by weak technical evidence**. Some witnesses probably observed genuine lights, weather changes or sea disturbances that they could not identify. Different reports may have had different causes. A smaller number may remain unresolved because essential information was never recorded—not because the evidence positively indicates an exotic explanation.

Within Abruzzo’s UFO history, the fishermen matter less as flawless identifiers of unknown objects than as the community whose repeated reports transformed ambiguous events at sea into a regional public issue. Their testimony explains why the authorities acted. Its limitations explain why, nearly half a century later, the episode remains historically important but scientifically inconclusive.
