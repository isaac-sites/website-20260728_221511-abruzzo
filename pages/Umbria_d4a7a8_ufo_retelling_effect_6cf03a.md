## Introduction

Retelling did not create [Umbria]({{ 'why-umbrias-ufo-stories-still-resist/' | relative_url }})’s strangest UFO cases, but it changed how later readers encounter them. Reports that began as local newspaper items, witness statements or investigators’ files were compressed into conference presentations, magazine summaries and online articles. In that process, memorable details survived while uncertainty about their origin often disappeared. Approximate movements became measured jumps; impressions became firm descriptions; and separate stages of reporting were blended into a single, smooth narrative.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Umbria_d4a7a8_ufo_retelling_effect_6cf03a-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Retelling" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The effect is clearest in the 1977 [Nocera Umbra]({{ 'nocera-1977/' | relative_url }}) encounter, now commonly described as a railway worker pursuing a metallic, triangular figure on thin legs. The surviving public account is vivid, yet it is largely a later summary of an earlier newspaper story rather than the complete contemporary report. Other Umbrian cases, including the 2001 Polino observation and sightings around [Orvieto]({{ 'orvieto/' | relative_url }}), reveal the same problem in milder form. Retelling preserved incidents that might otherwise have been forgotten, but it also made some of them sound more precise and evidentially complete than the surviving record warrants.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tuttoggi.info/ufo-in-umbria-4-avvistamenti-in-30-anni-i-lavori-del-25-convegno-nazionale-ufologia-terni-video-e-foto-tuttoggi-info/85047/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tuttoggi.info">[tuttoggi.info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tuttoggi.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF O IN UMBRIA, 4 AVVISTAMENTI IN 30 ANNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">I LAVORI...November 14, 2010 — 14 Nov 2010 — L&#x27;uomo avrebbe visto uno strano essere di forma triangolare che correva con sottilissimi ga...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 14, 2010</span></span></span>

## From local report to polished case history

The best-known version of the Nocera Umbra case comes from coverage of a national UFO conference held in Terni in November 2010. According to that account, a railway worker driving near Nocera Umbra on 11 August 1977 saw a shining triangular being or object moving along the road. It appeared metallic, travelled on extremely thin legs and advanced in small leaps. When the driver accelerated, it supposedly lengthened and quickened its jumps, made a sudden right-angle turn and vanished.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tuttoggi.info/ufo-in-umbria-4-avvistamenti-in-30-anni-i-lavori-del-25-convegno-nazionale-ufologia-terni-video-e-foto-tuttoggi-info/85047/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tuttoggi.info">[Tuttoggi.info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tuttoggi.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF O IN UMBRIA, 4 AVVISTAMENTI IN 30 ANNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">I LAVORI...November 14, 2010 — 14 Nov 2010 — L&#x27;uomo avrebbe visto uno strano essere di forma triangolare che correva con sottilissimi ga...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 14, 2010</span></span></span>

That is a striking narrative, but the published article was written more than 33 years after the alleged encounter. It says the contemporary local press was the documentary source, yet it does not reproduce the original cutting, identify every stage through which the testimony passed, or separate the newspaper’s wording from later investigators’ reconstruction. The reader therefore receives a finished story rather than a visible chain of evidence.

There are signs that a fuller investigative file once circulated. A 1985 Italian UFO bulletin listed 18 pages of documentation concerning close-encounter cases at Sturno and Nocera Umbra, supplied by researcher Solas Boncompagni. That reference is important because it shows that the case was not invented for the 2010 conference. It also exposes the archival gap: the existence of a file is documented, but much of the material on which modern retellings depend is not readily available alongside those retellings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufomagazines.com/notizie-cun-notizie-cun-no-08/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufomagazines.com">[UFO Magazine Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufomagazines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Magazine Archive NOTIZIE C</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U. The result is an uneven evidential inheritance. The public can easily find the spectacular outline but not necessarily the witness’s earliest recorded words, the interviewer’s questions, a map of the road, lighting and weather checks, or competing explanations considered at the time. Without those materials, it is difficult to tell which details were original observations and which were later clarifications, calculations or interpretive language.

## How precise details emerge over time

Later summaries often contain numbers because numbers make a report easier to picture. In the Nocera Umbra story, the figure is said to have moved in jumps of roughly 20 to 30 centimetres before increasing their length and frequency. The turn is described as 90 degrees. These details may faithfully reflect the witness’s first statement, an investigator’s later interview or an estimate made while presenting the case. The accessible summary does not establish which.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tuttoggi.info/ufo-in-umbria-4-avvistamenti-in-30-anni-i-lavori-del-25-convegno-nazionale-ufologia-terni-video-e-foto-tuttoggi-info/85047/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tuttoggi.info">[Tuttoggi.info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tuttoggi.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF O IN UMBRIA, 4 AVVISTAMENTI IN 30 ANNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">I LAVORI...November 14, 2010 — 14 Nov 2010 — L&#x27;uomo avrebbe visto uno strano essere di forma triangolare che correva con sottilissimi ga...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 14, 2010</span></span></span>

That distinction matters. A frightened driver at night is unlikely to have measured the vertical rise of a moving shape or its change of direction with instruments. Such values are ordinarily visual estimates. Repeating them without qualifiers can transform “small hops” into a seemingly measured gait, or “turned sharply” into a geometrically exact manoeuvre. Nothing needs to be deliberately fabricated for the account to become more definite.

The 2001 [Polino]({{ 'polino/' | relative_url }}) case shows a similar process. The 2010 conference report emphasised that three people were present and that they used binoculars. Those are potentially valuable details because multiple observers and optical aid may improve a sighting’s evidential value. Yet the concise public summary gives little information about whether the witnesses independently described the object, whether they discussed it while observing, what magnification was used, how long it remained visible or how investigators excluded aircraft and astronomical objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tuttoggi.info/ufo-in-umbria-4-avvistamenti-in-30-anni-i-lavori-del-25-convegno-nazionale-ufologia-terni-video-e-foto-tuttoggi-info/85047/amp/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tuttoggi.info">[Tuttoggi.info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tuttoggi.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF O IN UMBRIA, 4 AVVISTAMENTI IN 30 ANNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">I LAVORI DEL 25° CONVEGNO NAZIONALE UFOLOGIA TERNI (Video e Foto TuttOggi.info) - Tuttoggi.info# UFO IN UMBRIA, 4 AVVISTAMENTI IN 30 ANNI...</span></span></span>

A later writer may therefore inherit two strong-looking facts — three witnesses and binoculars — while losing the information needed to assess them. Shared observation can provide corroboration, but it can also produce a single negotiated description if witnesses talk to one another before their statements are recorded. Binoculars can reveal shape, but they can also magnify hand movement, atmospheric shimmer and difficulty tracking a distant light. The shortened retelling preserves the features that strengthen the story while omitting the procedural details that would show how strong they really are.

## Orvieto shows how headlines shape the memory

Reports around Orvieto illustrate a less dramatic but more visible form of narrative shaping. In July 2009, a local news report described two lights seen near the cathedral by several people. One appeared yellow-gold; the other was brighter, whitish and pulsating. Witnesses thought the lights seemed to chase one another for about 15 minutes before disappearing. The headline asked whether a UFO had appeared over Orvieto, while the body remained more cautious and described unidentified lights rather than a structured craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.orvietonews.it/index.php?anno=2009&amp;categoria=cronaca&amp;giorno=10&amp;mese=07&amp;nomepagina=ufo-su-orvieto-luci-strane-in-cielo-sopra-al-duomo-21374" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: orvietonews.it">[Orvietonews]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">orvietonews.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ufo su Orvieto?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Luci strane in cielo sopra al Duomo &#124; Orvietonews.itJuly 10, 2009 — # Ufo su Orvieto?... That difference between headline and body matters when stories are remembered or copied. “Two lights whose nature was not established” can gradually become “the Orvieto UFO”, even though the original report supplied no radar confirmation, photographs, measured trajectory or official conclusion. The location beside a famous cathedral also gives the story a strong visual identity. Later references may remember the dramatic setting more readily than the limited evidence.

A separate 2010 report near Porano, outside Orvieto, was publicised through a civilian investigation network. The accessible retelling says a witness saw an unidentified object at low altitude and found it directly in front of him. A later specialist magazine reference indicates that investigators received the report in March 2010 and examined matters such as weather information. However, shortened online versions tend to detach the initial claim from the later investigative process, leaving readers with the close-range impression but not a clear account of the checks, uncertainties or final assessment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lombardimistero.wordpress.com/category/ufologia/avvistamenti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lombardimistero.wordpress.com">[David Lombardi LUMOS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lombardimistero.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wordpress.com.</span></span></span>

The Orvieto examples show that retelling need not add an invented technical detail to change a case. Selection alone can do it. A summary may retain colour, low altitude, unusual movement and a picturesque location while dropping the absence of photographs, uncertain distance, possible conventional explanations and the fact that “unidentified” may simply mean that no firm identification was reached.

## Memory changes before publication too

The problem begins before a journalist or UFO writer produces a later summary. Human recollection is reconstructive rather than a fixed recording. Research on the misinformation effect shows that information encountered after an event can alter what witnesses later report, particularly when people have discussed the event, read media coverage or answered suggestive questions. Source-monitoring errors can then make a person remember a later suggestion as something personally seen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11680610/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed Central (PMC)]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PubMed Central (PMC)Do cognitive abilities reduce eyewitness susceptibility to the...by M Brassil · 2024 · Cited by 15 — The fact that m...</span></span></span>

Repeated interviewing is not automatically harmful. Carefully conducted follow-up interviews can recover additional accurate information that a witness omitted initially. Experimental work has found that people may provide during a second structured interview without necessarily reducing overall accuracy. The difficulty is that investigators need dated records showing when each detail first appeared and what information the witness encountered between interviews.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24098471/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nih.gov.</span></span></span>

For Umbrian UFO cases known mainly through later accounts, that chronology is often missing from public view. A detail appearing in a 2010 article about a 1977 event may have been present from the first night, recalled during a later interview, inferred by an investigator or adopted from an earlier published retelling. Without versioned statements, all four possibilities can look identical on the page.

Repetition can also raise confidence without improving the underlying evidence. Once a witness or writer has told a coherent version many times, the narrative may feel stable because its wording has become familiar. Psychological research does not justify dismissing every old witness statement as false, but it does show why narrative confidence should not be treated as a substitute for a contemporary record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dr.lib.iastate.edu/bitstreams/5c3f871c-6edc-4e59-bd26-74b056816646/download" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dr.lib.iastate.edu">[iastate.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dr.lib.iastate.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on iastate.edu.</span></span></span>

## What retelling preserved — and what it obscured

Retelling has had genuine historical value in Umbria. Conference reports and specialist catalogues kept regional incidents in circulation even where the original newspapers were difficult to search or had not been digitised. They connected isolated local stories with civilian case files and with Italy’s wider history of UFO reporting. Without this work, the Nocera Umbra and Polino cases might now be little more than names in an inaccessible index.

The cost is that later summaries often flatten different levels of evidence. Several distinctions can disappear:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* **Witness wording versus investigator wording.** A comparison or impression may be presented as a literal physical description.
* **Estimate versus measurement.** Approximate height, angle, distance or duration may acquire numerical precision.
* **Initial report versus final assessment.** The fact that investigators examined a case can be mistaken for confirmation that it resisted explanation.
* **Multiple witnesses versus independent witnesses.** A group sighting may sound like several separate confirmations even when accounts were formed collectively.
* **Unidentified versus extraordinary.** Failure to identify a light does not establish unusual technology or an unknown craft.

</div>

This flattening is especially important when cases are grouped together in anniversary articles or conference coverage. The 2010 Terni report presented four notable Umbrian incidents across roughly three decades. That format was useful for regional history, but it placed very different reports — a close roadside encounter, a multi-witness binocular observation and distant aerial lights — within one narrative category. Readers may remember them as four comparable mysteries even though their evidence, documentation and possible explanations differ considerably.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tuttoggi.info/ufo-in-umbria-4-avvistamenti-in-30-anni-i-lavori-del-25-convegno-nazionale-ufologia-terni-video-e-foto-tuttoggi-info/85047/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tuttoggi.info">[Tuttoggi.info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tuttoggi.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF O IN UMBRIA, 4 AVVISTAMENTI IN 30 ANNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">I LAVORI...November 14, 2010 — 14 Nov 2010 — L&#x27;uomo avrebbe visto uno strano essere di forma triangolare che correva con sottilissimi ga...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 14, 2010</span></span></span>

## Separating testimony from added interpretation

A careful reading of an old Umbrian case should treat the story as a sequence of documents, not as one timeless account. The most useful questions are straightforward: What is the earliest surviving source? Was it written during or soon after the event? Does it quote the witness directly? Which details appear only in later versions? Were witness statements taken independently? Did investigators publish their unsuccessful checks as well as their preferred interpretation?

Applied to Nocera Umbra, this approach produces a restrained conclusion. There is evidence that an unusual report was circulating in 1977 and that civilian researchers held a substantial file by the mid-1980s. The later conference account may accurately preserve its essential content. What cannot presently be established from the widely accessible sources is whether every striking measurement and movement description belonged to the earliest testimony or gained definition as the case passed through investigators, speakers and journalists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufomagazines.com/notizie-cun-notizie-cun-no-08/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufomagazines.com">[UFO Magazine Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufomagazines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Magazine Archive NOTIZIE C</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U. ## Did retelling make the cases stranger?

In presentation, often yes. Retelling gave Umbria’s cases cleaner plots, more memorable figures and sharper movements. It encouraged readers to imagine a metallic road creature making measured jumps, an optically confirmed object above Polino, or purposeful lights pursuing one another over Orvieto. Those images are easier to remember than incomplete files and qualified witness estimates.

In evidential terms, however, later reporting generally did not strengthen the cases. It preserved claims and sometimes pointed towards archives, but it rarely supplied the missing contemporary documents, instrument records or independent corroboration needed to test them. In several instances, the passage of time increased narrative detail while making the origin of that detail harder to verify.

The fairest description is therefore not that Umbria’s strangest stories were exposed as inventions, nor that repeated publication confirmed them. They became culturally stronger and evidentially less transparent. Their survival is valuable to regional UFO history, but their most polished versions should be read as maps leading back towards the original evidence — not as replacements for it.
