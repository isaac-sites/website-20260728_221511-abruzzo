## Introduction

Italy’s 1978 UFO wave changed official policy by turning scattered sighting stories into a standing air-safety and national-security reporting responsibility. The Italian Air Force says that Prime Minister Giulio Andreotti designated it as the institutional body responsible for collecting, checking and monitoring UFO reports after the wave. That responsibility survives today, with reports channelled through the Carabinieri and assessed for possible technical, human or natural [explanations]({{ 'explanations/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/en/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica MilitareOVNI - Aeronautica MilitareFollowing the wave of Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) sightings in 1978, the then Prime M...</span></span></span>

[Abruzzo]({{ 'why-abruzzo-became-italys-ufo-hotspot/' | relative_url }}) matters to this policy shift because alarm along the central Adriatic coast was not confined to newspaper columns. Reports from coastal communities prompted a parliamentary question, military surveillance and demands for a government explanation. The resulting policy did not confirm extraterrestrial craft, nor did it create an Italian equivalent of a large scientific UFO project. It established something more limited but durable: a national procedure for ensuring that unusual aerial reports entered the aviation and security system rather than being left entirely to journalists, local police or private investigators.

## Why the 1978 wave demanded a national response

Italy had experienced prominent UFO reports before 1978, but that year presented a problem of scale and administration. Reports arrived from many parts of the country, varied widely in quality and involved civilians, pilots, military personnel and law-enforcement bodies. In the Air Force’s surviving table for 1972–90, 69 cases are listed for 1978, compared with two in 1977 and ten in 1979. The table records eight of those 1978 cases in Abruzzo and three in the neighbouring Marche. These are Air Force archive figures, not a count of every claim published that year, but they show how sharply the official caseload rose.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/INT.Dperiodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

Civilian UFO organisations collected much larger totals because they included press reports and testimony that never became an Air Force file. The Centro Ufologico Nazionale database, for example, records more than a thousand reports for 1978. That difference is important: there was no single, complete national census. The military archive represents cases received and retained through official channels, while civilian catalogues cast a wider and less uniform net.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/Avvistamenti1978.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico NazionaleAvvistamenti1978.keyJune 11, 2026 — CodH 0 &#124; Descrizione Evento non rilevante --- &#124; --- CodH 1 &#124; Descrizione Lu...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 11, 2026</span></span></span>

Abruzzo’s coastal episode added a governance problem to the volume of reports. On 20 November 1978, parliamentarian Falco Accame asked whether the government knew of the alarm spreading through Abruzzo and the Marche over phenomena reported in the press and allegedly observed by a Navy patrol boat. He also asked what measures would be taken to establish their nature and reassure residents. A Defence Ministry reply followed in January 1979.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/documenti/attiparlamentari.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionale Le interrogazioni parlamentari i</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Per conoscere, se è al corrente dello stato di allarme creato nelle popolazioni dell’Abruzzo e Marche a seguito del manifestarsi di fenom...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 14, 2024</span></span></span>

The significance of that exchange was not that [Parliament]({{ 'parliament/' | relative_url }}) endorsed the [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses/' | relative_url }})’ interpretations. It showed that unexplained aerial or maritime observations could generate public anxiety, involve military assets and require an answer across several levels of government. Local reports could no longer be treated simply as odd stories from individual witnesses. Officials needed a body able to compare them with aircraft movements, weather, astronomical objects, military activity and other information unavailable to most civilians.

## The Air Force became the reporting authority

The Italian Air Force’s own account is direct: following the [1978 wave]({{ '1978-wave/' | relative_url }}), Andreotti designated the service as the institutional organisation charged with collecting, verifying and monitoring UFO reports. The present responsibility rests with the General Security Department of the Air Staff.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/en/ufficio-relazioni-con-il-pubblico/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufficio relazioni con il pubblico</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica MilitareUfficio relazioni con il pubblicoFollowing the wave of UFO sightings in 1978, the then Prime Minister Giulio Andreott...</span></span></span>

This choice was practical. The Air Force already had responsibility for surveillance and control of national airspace, along with access to military units and aviation information. A later parliamentary answer described its UFO role as collecting reports and checking their truthfulness from the standpoint of general security. That wording places the activity within airspace protection rather than speculative research into extraterrestrial life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://legislature.camera.it/_dati/leg13/lavori/stenografici/sed653/s110.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legislature.camera.it">[Camera dei Deputati]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legislature.camera.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">dei Deputati Sten</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">653 s110January 18, 2000 — In merito alla problematica relativa agli oggetti volanti non identificati, cosiddetti UFO, si rappresenta che...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 18, 2000</span></span></span>

The policy introduced three connected functions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* **Collection:** reports could be preserved in a common official system rather than remaining in local notebooks or newspaper archives.
* **Verification:** investigators could compare a witness account with known aircraft, balloons, celestial bodies, atmospheric conditions and military activity.
* **Monitoring:** repeated reports could be examined for patterns relevant to flight safety or national security.

</div>

The available official material does not present the post-1978 arrangement as a separate, heavily funded scientific programme with dedicated observatories or public research teams. Nor does the Air Force webpage identify a published decree, budget or detailed founding order. It describes an assigned institutional responsibility. That distinction guards against two opposite exaggerations: Italy did not simply ignore UFO reports after 1978, but neither did it create proof that the government regarded them as alien spacecraft.

## How the reporting system works

The policy’s lasting effect can be seen in the present procedure. A person who wishes to report a UFO-related event is directed to complete an Air Force form and submit it to the nearest Carabinieri station. The report can then be passed into the military assessment chain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/en/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica MilitareOVNI - Aeronautica MilitareFollowing the wave of Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) sightings in 1978, the then Prime M...</span></span></span>

The form-based approach matters because eyewitness memories are easily altered by time, discussion and media coverage. A structured report seeks details that can be checked: date, time, location, direction, apparent motion, colour, shape, weather and information about the observer. The historical Air Force archive uses broadly similar fields, recording who made the report and the result of later checks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

Archived cases show that the process can produce different outcomes. Some entries are attributed with reasonable confidence to bright astronomical objects or ordinary aircraft. Others state that, after examination of the information held in the archive, the event remained catalogued as a UFO.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

This is an identification system, not a machine for validating extraordinary claims. Its useful question is: can the report be matched to a known cause with the information available? A poor-quality account may remain unidentified simply because the time, direction or duration was recorded imprecisely. Conversely, a detailed and sincere report can still have an ordinary explanation.

## What an officially unidentified case means

The Air Force defines a UFO case operationally. Once checks have been completed, an episode is classified as an unidentified flying-object sighting when no technical or natural justification can be established. The stated purpose of the activity is flight safety and national security.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span></span></span>

“Officially unidentified” therefore means that the available investigation did not establish an explanation. It does **not** mean that investigators proved the object was a spacecraft, demonstrated impossible performance or excluded every conventional possibility. The label describes the result of the identification process, not the physical nature of the reported object.

Several evidence gaps can lead to that result:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* the observation was brief or made at night;
* distance and altitude could not be judged reliably;
* no photograph, radar record or physical trace survived;
* aircraft or weather data were incomplete;
* the report reached investigators too late for effective checking;
* witness descriptions conflicted or lacked precise timings.

</div>

The official archive itself illustrates this caution. Its entries often preserve basic descriptive information but little supporting material. A file may record colour, movement and witness type without providing raw radar plots, calibrated photographs or a full investigative narrative. Such records prove that a report entered the system; they do not necessarily establish that an unusual physical craft was present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

This is particularly relevant to the Abruzzo wave. Later accounts contain vivid claims about luminous objects over the Adriatic, disturbed water, fog and malfunctioning equipment. Yet the policy legacy rests on firmer evidence than many of those details: public alarm was documented, Parliament intervened, military authorities responded, and the government assigned the Air Force a continuing reporting role. The nature of the original phenomena remained much less certain.

## What changed — and what did not

Before 1978, responsibility for unusual aerial reports was less visible and could appear fragmented between police, military commands, local authorities and private UFO groups. After the wave, citizens and officials had a recognised national destination for reports. The change made the subject bureaucratically legible: an incident could be recorded, checked against aviation information and retained in an archive.

The policy also reframed the issue. UFO reports became relevant not because “flying saucers” had been accepted as real vehicles, but because any unexplained activity in controlled or sensitive airspace could deserve examination. A misidentified aircraft, balloon, atmospheric phenomenon or unknown object can matter to aviation even when it has no exotic origin.

What did not emerge was equally important. The designation did not guarantee that every Italian sighting would be investigated in depth. It did not eliminate uneven reporting, missing records or differences between civilian and military case totals. It did not make the complete evidential basis for every conclusion public. Later parliamentary discussion continued to describe the Air Force role primarily in terms of collecting reports and assessing them for general security, rather than conducting broad scientific research into the UFO phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://legislature.camera.it/_dati/leg13/lavori/stenografici/sed653/s110.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legislature.camera.it">[Camera dei Deputati]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legislature.camera.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">dei Deputati Sten</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">653 s110January 18, 2000 — In merito alla problematica relativa agli oggetti volanti non identificati, cosiddetti UFO, si rappresenta che...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 18, 2000</span></span></span>

The official statistics also warn against treating the archive as a complete picture of public experience. The Air Force table contains 208 sightings for the whole 1972–90 period, while civilian organisations record far more claims. The official system is therefore best understood as a filtered administrative record shaped by which reports reached the authorities, which were retained and which remained unidentified after assessment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/INT.Dperiodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

## Abruzzo’s place in the policy shift

Abruzzo was one part of a nationwide wave, not the sole reason for the government’s decision. Nevertheless, the region provided a particularly clear example of why a national reporting structure was needed. Coastal testimony crossed regional boundaries, involved the Adriatic maritime environment, generated claims of military observation and produced enough alarm to reach Parliament.

That combination linked local UFO history to questions of public reassurance and security. A fisherman’s report off the Abruzzo coast could not be assessed properly by newspaper comparison alone. Investigators might need information from naval patrols, aviation authorities, military radar, meteorological services and neighbouring regions. The Air Force assignment supplied a national point through which such checks could, at least in principle, be coordinated.

The most defensible conclusion is therefore narrower than the region’s later legends but more significant than a single unresolved sighting. Abruzzo’s 1978 alarm helped demonstrate that mass UFO reporting could become an institutional problem. Italy’s response was to place it within the structures already responsible for airspace and security.

## The policy’s lasting legacy

The system created after 1978 remains publicly visible decades later. The Air Force still provides a reporting form, publishes statistics and explains that cases are checked for technical or natural causes before any unidentified classification is made.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span></span></span>

That continuity is the strongest evidence that 1978 produced more than a temporary reaction to sensational headlines. It established a lasting division of responsibility. Civilian researchers and journalists could continue gathering testimony and debating interpretations, but the Air Force became the official repository and assessor for reports relevant to flight and national security.

For readers examining Abruzzo’s UFO history, this policy legacy helps separate three questions that are often blurred together. People unquestionably reported unusual phenomena in 1978. The authorities unquestionably treated the wave seriously enough to investigate and reorganise reporting responsibility. But the surviving official record does not prove that the phenomena were extraterrestrial or even that every celebrated coastal claim entered the military archive.

The change of 1978 was therefore administrative rather than revelatory. Italy did not announce that it had discovered alien craft. It accepted that unexplained aerial reports required a permanent route into government, a method of checking ordinary explanations and an official meaning for the word “unidentified”.
