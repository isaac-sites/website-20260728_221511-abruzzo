## Introduction

Eugenio Siragusa was not simply a witness who reported an unusual light over [Sicily]({{ 'why-sicilys-ufo-stories-still-resist/' | relative_url }}). He claimed continuing communication, face-to-face meetings and journeys with extraterrestrial beings, then built a public mission around the messages he said they had entrusted to him. From Catania and communities on the slopes of Mount Etna, he became Italy’s most prominent “contactee”: a person who presents alleged extraterrestrials as teachers or spiritual authorities rather than merely unidentified visitors.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Sicily_77b465_siragusa_contactee_m_21f46f-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Siragusa" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

His importance to Sicilian UFO history lies less in the strength of his evidence than in his cultural influence. Siragusa gave the island a ready-made language of benevolent “space brothers”, cosmic warnings, prophetic disasters and human spiritual transformation before the large sighting waves of the 1970s. His lectures, publications and followers helped blur the boundary between investigating objects in the sky and accepting a religiously charged story about extraterrestrial guidance. The claims themselves remain almost entirely testimonial, with no independently verified physical evidence establishing that contact occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/p/gli-ufo-in-sicilia.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: Gli Ufo in Sicilia...</span></span></span>

## The rise of a Catania contactee

Siragusa was born in Catania in 1919 and initially entered Sicily’s small flying-saucer scene as an enthusiast rather than as its leading visionary. In 1958 he was among the founders of an early local study group alongside Sicilian researchers including Franco Brancatelli, Alfredo Scalia, Giuseppe Pappalardo and Aldo Turiano. That group belonged to the first organised phase of UFO interest on the island, when enthusiasts held meetings, collected press reports and hosted visiting personalities associated with the international contactee movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU Sicilia CISU</span></span></span>

One influential visitor was George Hunt Williamson, an American writer linked to George Adamski, the best-known of the early post-war contactees. Adamski claimed that he had met a man from Venus in the Californian desert in 1952, and his books established many of the themes later found in contactee narratives: human-looking visitors, warnings about nuclear weapons, advanced spiritual teaching and a special messenger chosen to address humanity. Williamson spoke in Catania in August 1958, and Siragusa reportedly welcomed him enthusiastically. The encounter placed Sicily’s emerging UFO community in direct contact with an already developed international mythology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/p/gli-ufo-in-sicilia.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: Gli Ufo in Sicilia...</span></span></span>

Siragusa’s own public career began decisively in 1962. He said that he had previously experienced unusual inner communications, but that in April of that year he was drawn towards Mount Etna and met beings associated with a luminous craft. Published versions differ in detail, yet the central account describes two human-like figures wearing close-fitting or diving-suit-like clothing, illuminated belts and other technological equipment. They allegedly spoke Italian and delivered a message of peace and warning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.org/stream/PassportToMagonia--UFOsFolkloreAndParallelWorldsJacquesVallee1993/Passport%20to%20Magonia%E2%80%94UFOs%2C%20Folklore%2C%20and%20Parallel%20Worlds%2C%20Jacques%20Vall%C3%A9e%20%281993%29_djvu.txt" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.org">[Internet Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eugenio Siragusa, 43, claimed to have met two men of normal height, clad in diving suits, with belts emitting...Read more...</span></span></span>

The Catania newspaper *La Sicilia* brought the story to a much wider audience on 19 September 1962, reproducing Siragusa’s narrative and early messages. Its headline framing was openly sceptical, effectively suggesting that imagination had been allowed to run freely. The mockery did not prevent the article from giving Siragusa the publicity he needed. Within a few years, his reputation had spread beyond Sicily and eventually beyond Italy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/p/gli-ufo-in-sicilia.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: Gli Ufo in Sicilia...</span></span></span>

That sequence is important. Siragusa did not become prominent because investigators first established an exceptional physical case. He became prominent because a vivid personal narrative entered the regional press, attracted curiosity and developed into a continuing body of teachings. The movement grew through repetition, personal charisma and the promise of privileged knowledge, not through the accumulation of independently confirmed observations.

## Messages, followers and media attention

Siragusa founded the Centro Studi Fratellanza Cosmica, or Centre for the Study of Cosmic Brotherhood, which became the organisational centre of his mission. Its activities included lectures, circulars, pamphlets, private meetings and the circulation of messages attributed to extraterrestrial or higher intelligences. A community of followers formed around him near Catania, including at locations on the lower slopes of Etna such as Santa Maria della Stella, Valverde and Nicolosi.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/p/gli-ufo-in-sicilia.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: Gli Ufo in Sicilia...</span></span></span>

The movement’s title captured its central promise. Siragusa did not portray the occupants of UFOs primarily as hostile invaders or detached scientific observers. They were presented as members of an advanced cosmic order concerned about humanity’s violence, materialism, environmental destruction and misuse of nuclear technology. Earth, in this account, occupied a morally dangerous stage of development and faced catastrophe unless human beings changed their behaviour.

This framework combined several traditions that were already familiar to European audiences:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Flying-saucer contact stories**, including encounters with attractive, human-like visitors and voyages aboard advanced craft.
* **Christian and apocalyptic language**, with extraterrestrials sometimes portrayed as agents of divine justice or beings connected to biblical history.
* **Esoteric teachings**, including reincarnation, hidden dimensions, lost civilisations and claims about humanity’s cosmic origins.
* **Contemporary anxieties**, particularly nuclear war, pollution, political conflict and the possibility of planetary disaster.

</div>

The result was closer to a spiritual movement than to a conventional UFO investigation society. Siragusa claimed not only meetings with non-human visitors but access to cosmology, prophecy and moral instruction. Accounts circulated by supporters also attributed to him extraordinary journeys, including travel beyond Earth and knowledge about hidden extraterrestrial bases. These stories expanded over time, but the supporting material remained largely Siragusa’s testimony and documents produced within his own circle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eugeniosiragusa.it/la-storia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eugeniosiragusa.it">[eugeniosiragusa.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eugeniosiragusa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Eugenio Siragusa – Biografia – Eugenio Siragusa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eugenio Siragusa – Biografia – Eugenio Siragusa</span></span></span>

His message travelled effectively because it addressed more than curiosity about strange objects. Followers were offered an explanation for war, social disorder and the perceived spiritual emptiness of modern life. They were also given a role: to spread warnings, prepare morally and recognise extraterrestrial intervention as part of a larger sacred history. Studies of Italian alternative religion have consequently treated organisations descended from Siragusa’s work as part of the country’s new religious landscape rather than merely as clubs studying aerial anomalies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.academia.edu/30440890/New_Religious_Movements_in_Italy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: academia.edu">[Academia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">academia.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New Religious Movements in Italy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Religious Movements in Italy</span></span></span>

During the 1970s, Siragusa travelled widely. Supporter accounts list appearances in several European countries and visits to South America and Canada, while independent Sicilian UFO historians likewise acknowledge that his fame crossed national borders. Regional importance therefore became international reach: a contactee identity formed in Catania was exported through conferences, personal networks and translated publications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/p/gli-ufo-in-sicilia.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: Gli Ufo in Sicilia...</span></span></span>

## Why his story changed Sicily’s UFO culture

Siragusa’s most lasting influence was to make a particular interpretation of UFOs culturally available. Before a witness had finished describing a light, a disc or an apparent figure, audiences already possessed a story into which it could be placed: visitors were watching humanity, Etna was a possible contact zone, advanced beings could look human, and their appearance carried a moral warning.

That did not mean later Sicilian witnesses were copying Siragusa consciously. Nor does similarity prove that every report was imaginary. It does mean that testimony developed within an environment already rich in familiar imagery. Investigators therefore have to consider cultural influence alongside astronomical, meteorological and aviation explanations.

Mount Etna was especially powerful as a setting. Its height, isolation, changing weather, volcanic activity and dramatic night skies made it a natural stage for stories of luminous objects and secret meetings. Siragusa’s alleged encounters tied that landscape to a mythology of cosmic visitation. When later close-encounter reports appeared around Etna, readers and journalists encountered them in a region where the volcano had already been represented as a meeting place between humanity and beings from elsewhere.

The influence became particularly relevant during Italy’s enormous UFO wave of 1978. Sicilian researchers catalogued 223 regional reports that year, including lights, photographs and alleged encounters with humanoid figures. Siragusa did not originate those individual reports, and they must be examined separately, but his decades of publicity had helped prepare audiences to interpret ambiguous experiences in extraterrestrial and sometimes spiritual terms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/2018/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU Sicilia CISU</span></span></span>

This is why Siragusa belongs in Sicily’s UFO history even when his own contact claims are judged weak. A sighting can matter because it has good observational data; a contactee can matter because he changes what later witnesses, journalists and readers expect an unexplained event to mean. Siragusa’s influence was primarily of the second kind.

## The 1978 crisis and its aftermath

Siragusa’s public standing was severely tested at the end of the 1970s. He dissolved the Centro Studi Fratellanza Cosmica in 1978, according to both movement histories and later regional accounts. In November that year he was arrested after allegations by an American couple formerly associated with his circle. Contemporary accusations reportedly included fraud, unlawful medical activity, sexual violence and the now-abolished Italian offence commonly translated as psychological domination or “plagio”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thebongiovannifamily.it/messaggi-celesti/persecuzione-e-liberazione-di-un-gigante-del-cielo-seconda-parte/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thebongiovannifamily.it">[The Bongiovanni Family]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thebongiovannifamily.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on thebongiovannifamily.it.</span></span></span>

The case received heightened attention because it unfolded during a period of intense fear about coercive religious groups. Only days earlier, more than 900 members of the Peoples Temple movement had died in Guyana. Italian reporting was also covering a separate child-death case initially and sometimes inaccurately connected in the press with Siragusa’s organisation. Later reconstruction found that the family involved may only have attended some of his conferences rather than belonging formally to the Cosmic Brotherhood.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/2018/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU Sicilia CISU</span></span></span>

Siragusa was acquitted of the charges in 1982. That legal outcome must be stated clearly: arrest and accusation are not proof of guilt, and the prosecution did not produce a conviction. Accounts maintained by his supporters present the case as a campaign of persecution and emphasise the full acquittal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eugeniosiragusa.it/la-storia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eugeniosiragusa.it">[eugeniosiragusa.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eugeniosiragusa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Eugenio Siragusa – Biografia – Eugenio Siragusa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eugenio Siragusa – Biografia – Eugenio Siragusa</span></span></span>

Regional UFO historians have remained strongly critical of his leadership and of the way followers’ commitment supported his activities, while devotees reject that portrayal and describe participation as voluntary, unpaid spiritual work. The available online record is heavily polarised between former or continuing believers and sceptical UFO researchers, with limited neutral documentation of everyday life inside the group.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/p/gli-ufo-in-sicilia.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: Gli Ufo in Sicilia...</span></span></span>

Siragusa remained the founding reference point, but control of his legacy fragmented.

## Contactee testimony is not sighting evidence

The central evidential problem is straightforward. Siragusa’s extraordinary claims depended overwhelmingly on Siragusa himself. His alleged meetings were not documented under controlled conditions, his messages were not received through independently monitored instruments, and the journeys described in movement literature produced no publicly verified object or measurement demonstrating extraterrestrial origin.

This makes his case fundamentally different from a well-recorded aerial incident. A conventional sighting investigation can compare witness positions, timings, photographs, radar data, weather records, aircraft movements and astronomical objects. Even when no explanation is found, the inquiry concerns an event that may have left several independent traces.

A contactee narrative is harder to test because its most important elements occur privately:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--caution" markdown="1">

* The claimant says a voice or telepathic message was received.
* The claimant identifies its source as extraterrestrial.
* The claimant supplies information about the visitors’ intentions.
* Followers may accept later events as confirmation of earlier warnings.
* Failed or unclear predictions can be reinterpreted symbolically or postponed.

</div>

The existence of documents does not solve that problem. Siragusa’s archives contain many messages, statements and recollections, but these establish what he and his followers published, not who or what originated the material. A large archive can be historically valuable while remaining evidentially weak as proof of alien contact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.famigliafideus.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/RACCOLTA-COMUNICAZIONI-VOL.1-E.-Siragusa.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: famigliafideus.com">[Famiglia Fideus]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">famigliafideus.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Famiglia Fideus RACCOLTA COMUNICAZIONI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Famiglia FideusRACCOLTA COMUNICAZIONI - VOL.1 - Eugenio SiragusaEugenio Siragusa, nato a Catania il 25 marzo 1919, Da quel giorno di Apri...</span></span></span>

Some of Siragusa’s teachings also included claims that conflict with established science, including hidden lunar bases, vanished moons, a hollow Earth and cosmic accounts of lost continents. Such statements make independent corroboration especially important, yet the movement did not provide testable evidence proportionate to the claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eugeniosiragusa.it/la-storia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eugeniosiragusa.it">[eugeniosiragusa.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eugeniosiragusa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Eugenio Siragusa – Biografia – Eugenio Siragusa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eugenio Siragusa – Biografia – Eugenio Siragusa</span></span></span>

Supporters often point instead to moral consistency, apparent predictions, personal experiences around Siragusa or the emotional force of his teaching. These may explain sincere belief, but they do not identify an extraterrestrial source. Broad warnings about war, environmental decline, water scarcity or technological danger can remain relevant for decades without demonstrating privileged information; they concern risks already discussed by scientists, governments and campaigners.

## How Siragusa should be assessed

Siragusa’s place in Sicilian history is best understood by separating three questions that are often merged.

**Did he become an influential public figure?** Yes. Contemporary press coverage, regional UFO archives, movement publications and the survival of successor groups all show that he attracted followers and became Italy’s best-known contactee.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisusicilia.it/p/gli-ufo-in-sicilia.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisusicilia.it">[CISU Sicilia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisusicilia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CISU Sicilia CISU</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CISU SiciliaCISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici - SICILIA: Gli Ufo in Sicilia...</span></span></span>

**Did his ideas shape Sicilian interpretations of UFOs?** Very probably. He linked Catania and Etna to an internationally recognisable contactee tradition and popularised the image of spiritually advanced extraterrestrials warning humanity. That influence preceded the island’s largest reporting wave and helped entrench contact narratives within regional popular culture.

**Is there persuasive evidence that he communicated with extraterrestrials?** No publicly available evidence establishes that conclusion. The claimed encounters rest mainly on personal testimony, publications created by his movement and later accounts from committed followers. Neither the volume of the material nor Siragusa’s acquittal in the 1982 criminal case turns those claims into independently verified events.

His legacy is therefore double-edged. To followers, he was a moral messenger who urged humanity away from self-destruction. To sceptical investigators, he exemplified how personal revelation, international saucer folklore and charismatic authority could overtake evidence-based inquiry. For Sicily’s wider UFO history, the most defensible conclusion lies between those positions: Siragusa was culturally important but evidentially unconvincing.

Understanding that distinction helps readers approach later Sicilian cases more carefully. Reports involving pilots, police officers, photographs or multiple witnesses should be judged on their own records, not accepted because they resemble Siragusa’s teachings and not dismissed merely because contactee folklore existed. His real, demonstrable impact was not proof that extraterrestrials visited Etna. It was the creation of a durable interpretive world in which many Sicilians could imagine that they had.
