## Introduction

Terni’s importance in Italy’s 1978 UFO wave rests on one unusually strong feature: the Umbrian observation was not an isolated story from a single member of the public. On 9 March, a luminous phenomenon was reported across a wide stretch of central and northern Italy, with military personnel and civilian airline crews among the witnesses. The Italian Air Force archive groups Terni with Bologna, the Gran Sasso area, Vicenza and Ancona, describing an elongated red-and-green object and retaining the event as unidentified after reviewing the available records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Umbria_d4a7a8_terni_1978_flap_216229-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for 1978 Flap" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That official classification makes the case more significant than most Umbrian reports, but it does not establish that an extraordinary craft crossed Italy. The surviving public record contains important gaps, including conflicting times, no published radar plot, no photographs and no complete reconstruction of the observers’ sightlines. Terni therefore matters less as proof of alien visitation than as a rare Umbrian point within a national aviation incident that was documented, investigated and never publicly given a firm conventional explanation.

## The multi-location sighting of 9 March

The Italian Air Force’s published historical catalogue treats the episode as a single multi-location event. Its summary lists Terni, Bologna, Gran Sasso, Vicenza and Ancona; gives the date as 9 March 1978; describes the reported form as elongated and the colours as red and green; and identifies the witnesses collectively as Air Force personnel and civilian pilots. The fields for speed, direction, altitude and weather are left unspecified. Its conclusion is carefully limited: after examination of the data in the archive, the event was catalogued as an unidentified flying object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

This wording is easy to overread. In the Air Force system, “unidentified” means that investigators could not establish a technical or natural explanation from the information available. It is not a finding that the object was extraterrestrial, nor even confirmation that every observer saw one physical object. The Air Force states that its role is to collect, verify and monitor reports relevant to flight and national security, classifying an episode as unidentified when its checks do not yield a satisfactory explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica MilitareOVNI - Aeronautica Militare - Ministero della DifesaSegnalazioni O.V.N.I., Oggetti Volanti Non Identificati. More detailed later accounts, apparently drawing on aviation communications, place an important part of the event near the Terni area. At about 19:34, a military aircraft identified as Mix 368 reportedly informed the Potenza Picena control facility of a bright green phenomenon resembling a rocket or signal flare. It was said to oscillate, climb and descend close to the aircraft, creating the impression of an attempted interception. The aircraft was reported near “Monte Gargiano” in the Terni area at flight level 110, roughly 11,000 feet under standard pressure settings. A subsequent message placed it near the Gran Sasso sector, where the crew again described a green rocket-like or flare-like light moving vertically near its position.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ilsud-est.it/attualita/inchiesta/2022/10/31/9-marzo-1978-il-prologo-della-strage-di-ustica/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilsud-est.it">[IlSudEst]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilsud-est.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">9 marzo 1978 il prologo della strage di ustica</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">IlSudEst9 MARZO 1978, IL PROLOGO DELLA STRAGE DI USTICA? - IlSudEst...</span></span></span>

The location name “Monte Gargiano” is itself uncertain. It may be a transcription or reporting error, and the publicly accessible Air Force summary does not repeat it. That small problem illustrates a wider limitation: much of the detailed narrative reaches the public through later transcription and journalism rather than through a complete, facsimile set of contemporaneous operational records.

At approximately the same stage of the incident, civilian crews were also reporting green lights. One Itavia captain flying from Rome towards Treviso described a very bright green illumination to the right of his aircraft, initially comparing it with a signal rocket and later with an aircraft afterburner. Air traffic control reportedly told him that no known aircraft was within about 40 miles of his position. The controller then advised that a Malta Airways aircraft and other crews near Ancona had reported a similar green glow at estimated distances of seven to fifteen miles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ilsud-est.it/attualita/inchiesta/2022/10/31/9-marzo-1978-il-prologo-della-strage-di-ustica/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilsud-est.it">[IlSudEst]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilsud-est.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">9 marzo 1978 il prologo della strage di ustica</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">IlSudEst9 MARZO 1978, IL PROLOGO DELLA STRAGE DI USTICA? - IlSudEst...</span></span></span>

A regional news account likewise records a military report at 19:34 near Potenza Picena, followed at about 19:40 by reports from three commercial aircraft of a large green luminous object over Ancona. It says that the sighting material was passed by the Defence Staff to Italian civilian UFO organisations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.picusonline.it/it/pagine/3FF1114A-07D4-4ED0-988B-45AF9A9AE3F7%2CB6A14620-EA5F-11EB-A0DC-FE7BA12B91FC%2C17030/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: picusonline.it">[Picus Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">picusonline.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Picus Online Ufo, su Rai Due gli avvistamenti nelle Marche</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Picus OnlineUfo, su Rai Due gli avvistamenti nelle Marche - Picus Online - IT...</span></span></span>

The reports therefore form a geographically broad chain rather than a single close encounter over Terni. The Umbrian connection comes from the military aircraft’s position in or near the Terni sector and from Terni’s inclusion in the Air Force’s consolidated list of locations. The phenomenon was also reported farther north and east, particularly around the Adriatic aviation corridor.

## Military and aviation witnesses

Pilot testimony gives the case more evidential weight than an unsupported ground report, chiefly because trained crews understand ordinary aircraft lights, flares and common flight conditions. The 9 March reports were also made through operational channels while the observations were unfolding, reducing the risk that the entire story developed only through later UFO publicity. The Air Force’s own catalogue confirms that both military personnel and civilian pilots contributed to the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

Even so, professional status does not solve the central measurement problems. A pilot may describe a light accurately while still being unable to determine its distance, size or true motion. Against a dark sky, a bright object with no visible background reference can appear to approach, pace or manoeuvre near an aircraft when it is actually much farther away. Different crews may also assign very different distances to the same high-altitude phenomenon.

The Itavia pilot’s language is revealing because it was cautious rather than sensational. He first compared the light to a green signal rocket, considered an afterburner, and reportedly stated that he was not calling it a UFO. Only after being told that other crews had seen a similar phenomenon did he describe the impression that it had moved towards his aircraft before turning away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ilsud-est.it/attualita/inchiesta/2022/10/31/9-marzo-1978-il-prologo-della-strage-di-ustica/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilsud-est.it">[IlSudEst]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilsud-est.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">9 marzo 1978 il prologo della strage di ustica</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">IlSudEst9 MARZO 1978, IL PROLOGO DELLA STRAGE DI USTICA? - IlSudEst...</span></span></span> This strengthens the case that something unusual was genuinely observed, while weakening claims that the witness positively identified a structured or intelligently controlled vehicle.

The military crew’s reported description presents the same ambiguity. A light moving up and down “like an interception” sounds dramatic, yet the crew also compared it with a rocket or green flare. Such comparisons keep several ordinary categories open:

* a bright meteor or fragmenting fireball;<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.facebook.com/EdPiotrowskiWPDE/videos/meteor-over-italy/2425088154429214/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[facebook.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

A bright meteor can appear green because of its composition, speed and atmospheric interaction, and can be visible across a very large area. Modern instrumented Italian fireballs demonstrate that intense green events can travel tens of kilometres through the upper atmosphere and be observed from widely separated locations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://neo.ssa.esa.int/search-for-fireballs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: neo.ssa.esa.int">[NEO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">neo.ssa.esa.int</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NEOCC - European Space AgencyFireballs and bright meteors are small-sized NEOs, typically centimetres to metres in diameter, that c...</span></span></span> Yet a straightforward meteor explanation faces difficulties here: the reported duration appears longer than a typical brief fireball, and some witnesses described vertical or apparently responsive motion. Without exact bearings and timings from each aircraft, however, those movements cannot be reconstructed reliably.

The absence of known traffic near one civilian aircraft is also less decisive than it sounds. Air traffic control could exclude aircraft represented in its operational picture, but that does not automatically exclude all military activity, small objects, distant lights or phenomena without a usable radar return. Conversely, no publicly available radar track confirms a solid object performing the manoeuvres perceived by the crews.

## Why the published record remains unresolved

The strongest evidence is the combination of simultaneous aviation reports, broad geographical coverage and official preservation. The weakest point is the lack of detailed, mutually consistent data needed to test possible explanations. The Air Force’s public table records no speed, direction, altitude or weather for the phenomenon itself. It provides no angular measurements, duration for each witness, radar imagery, photographs, physical traces or complete witness statements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

There is also a conspicuous timing conflict. The published Air Force catalogue gives an approximate interval of 20:30 to 20:40, whereas accounts based on aviation communications place the key reports between about 19:34 and 19:41.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[difesa.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span> A one-hour difference might reflect a clerical mistake, different time conventions or later transcription, but the open record does not settle the issue. That discrepancy matters because precise timing is essential for deciding whether reports from distant cities concerned one phenomenon or several unrelated ones.

The source record also changes in specificity. The Air Force table calls the object elongated and red-and-green, while the aviation accounts concentrate mainly on an intense green glow resembling a flare, rocket or afterburner. These descriptions are not necessarily incompatible: separate observers may have seen different phases or portions of the same event. They could equally show that several reports were grouped together because they occurred within a narrow period and shared a dominant colour.

Later reporting has therefore strengthened the historical importance of the case more than its physical interpretation. The release of the Air Force catalogue confirms that Terni was genuinely part of an official multi-witness file and that the episode remained unidentified in the archive. Detailed reproductions of radio exchanges add immediacy and show that the crews actively considered ordinary aviation explanations. They do not, however, supply the missing geometry or sensor data required to prove that a single object travelled from [Umbria]({{ 'why-umbrias-ufo-stories-still-resist/' | relative_url }}) towards the Adriatic or deliberately approached aircraft.

## Terni’s place in the 1978 wave

Italy’s 1978 wave was exceptional in scale. The Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici later counted about 1,800 sighting reports for the year and digitised roughly 2,400 related newspaper articles. Its researchers have also stressed that 1978 was partly a media wave: extensive press attention amplified public interest and created an atmosphere sometimes described at the time as UFO mania.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.euroufo.net/2018/05/15/project1978/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: euroufo.net">[EuroUFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">euroufo.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The 1978 Great UFO Wave, 40 years later &#124; EuroUFO...</span></span></span>

The 9 March incident occurred before the most intense later phases of that national excitement and differs from many sensational cases associated with the year. It involved no claimed beings, abduction, landing or recovered material. Its core is narrower and more credible: several aircrews reported unusual coloured lights, controllers compared reports, and the military retained the episode without a published solution.

Within Umbria’s UFO history, this gives Terni a distinctive role. The city was not necessarily the centre of the phenomenon, and the evidence does not establish that an object hovered over urban Terni itself. Rather, the Terni sector was one node in a fast-moving network of aviation observations extending across central Italy. It linked Umbria to the country’s largest twentieth-century UFO wave through a case involving professional witnesses and an official record, not merely local folklore.

The fairest verdict is therefore **unresolved but evidentially limited**. Something unusual was reported by several military and civilian observers on 9 March 1978. The reports were serious enough to enter the Italian Air Force archive and remain classified there as unidentified. Yet the surviving public evidence is insufficient to determine whether all witnesses saw one object, whether its apparent manoeuvres were real, or whether a flare, meteor, aircraft-related effect or unrecorded military activity could explain some or all of the observations. Terni’s lasting significance lies precisely in that boundary between a credible report and an unproven interpretation.
