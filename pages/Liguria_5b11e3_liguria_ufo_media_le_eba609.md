## Introduction

[Liguria]({{ 'why-liguria-became-italys-ufo-coast/' | relative_url }})’s reputation as a centre of UFO mystery was not created by a large body of strong sightings. It was created mainly by the repeated public retelling of one extraordinary and disputed story: security guard Pier Fortunato Zanfretta’s alleged encounters near Torriglia from December 1978 onwards. Newspapers turned an alarming local incident into national news; television supplied faces, voices, reconstructions and hypnotic testimony; later films, talk shows, podcasts and social media kept the story available to new audiences.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Liguria_5b11e3_liguria_ufo_media_le_eba609-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Media Legacy" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

This matters because the media did more than report the case. Coverage gradually expanded its meaning. A confused night-time episode became an abduction narrative, Torriglia became a recognised UFO location, and almost any later photograph of an uncertain object over Genoa could be linked back to Zanfretta. Repetition increased the story’s familiarity, but not necessarily its evidential strength. Later broadcasts added testimony and spectacle, yet they produced little new independently testable evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rinodistefano.com/it/articoli/zanfretta-rivista-gente.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rinodistefano.com">[RinoDiStefano.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rinodistefano.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rino Di Stefano.com ADESS O GLI UFO MI VOGLIONO RAPIRE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rino Di Stefano.com ADESS O GLI UFO MI VOGLIONO RAPIRE</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 20, 1979</span></span></span>

## National coverage made Zanfretta the face of Ligurian UFOs

The first reports contained ingredients that were unusually attractive to journalists: an ordinary working man, an isolated mountain road, a frightened radio call, colleagues who found him distressed, police involvement and reports of strange lights in nearby communities. These details gave the story a firmer news framework than an unsupported claim made years later. They did not prove an alien encounter, but they allowed editors to present the episode as a genuine mystery rather than simply a piece of fantasy.

Journalist Rino Di Stefano’s article for the weekly magazine *Gente*, published on 20 January 1979, was especially important. It placed Zanfretta’s account alongside statements from his colleagues and descriptions of the security company’s response. The magazine format gave room for narrative detail, photographs and dramatic quotations. Di Stefano later stated that the article was translated and sold internationally, helping a Ligurian incident move beyond its regional setting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rinodistefano.com/it/articoli/zanfretta-rivista-gente.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rinodistefano.com">[RinoDiStefano.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rinodistefano.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rino Di Stefano.com ADESS O GLI UFO MI VOGLIONO RAPIRE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rino Di Stefano.com ADESS O GLI UFO MI VOGLIONO RAPIRE</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 20, 1979</span></span></span>

The story’s appearance on *Portobello*, Enzo Tortora’s hugely popular television programme, marked another step. A witness who had previously existed for most Italians as a name in print now appeared before a mass audience. Television could make his anxiety seem immediate, but its entertainment setting also encouraged audiences to treat him as an unusual character. Zanfretta later complained that he felt mocked by the programme and described receiving nuisance telephone calls from strangers. The publicity therefore strengthened the legend while imposing a considerable personal cost on the man at its centre.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier_Fortunato_Zanfretta" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pier Fortunato Zanfretta</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pier Fortunato Zanfretta</span></span></span>

This tension followed the case throughout its media life. Zanfretta was often presented simultaneously as a sincere, troubled witness and as a figure of spectacle. Viewers could sympathise with his visible distress without accepting his account, laugh at the more extravagant details or remember only the image of the “alien abductee”. In this way, national television made the case culturally durable even among people who did not believe it.

## Hypnosis turned a local mystery into an abduction saga

The greatest change in the story came not from a new physical discovery but from hypnotic sessions organised after journalists and UFO investigators became involved. During [hypnosis]({{ 'hypnosis/' | relative_url }}), Zanfretta gave increasingly elaborate accounts of being taken aboard a craft, examined and confronted by giant non-human beings. The media now had far more vivid material than the original report of lights, fear and unusual figures.

A surviving 1979 recording of one session demonstrates why the material was so suitable for television. Instead of a dry summary, audiences could hear a distressed witness apparently reliving an encounter. The performance carried emotional force, even though emotional intensity is not a measure of factual accuracy. The session was also conducted in the presence of interested observers, including journalists and UFO researchers, rather than under conditions designed to avoid suggestion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rinodistefano.com/it/video/zanfretta-ipnosi-1979.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rinodistefano.com">[RinoDiStefano.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rinodistefano.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rino Di Stefano.com«NON SIETE ESSERI UMANI…»</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gli incontri ravvicinati di Piero Zanfretta” della docuserie “COME SIAMO” (RAI, 1984)... * ADESSO GLI UFO MI VOGLIONO RAPIRE (20 Gennaio...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 8, 1979</span></span></span>

This distinction is central to understanding how Ligurian UFO lore grew. The original event had uncertainties: something frightened Zanfretta, colleagues responded, and authorities recorded information. The later alien-abduction narrative was much more detailed, but those details emerged through a technique that can increase confidence and narrative richness without reliably recovering accurate memories. Even figures within Italian ufology later argued that intervention during the sessions had irreparably contaminated the material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier_Fortunato_Zanfretta" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pier Fortunato Zanfretta</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pier Fortunato Zanfretta</span></span></span>

Media accounts often combined the early investigation and the later hypnotic narrative as though they formed one continuous body of evidence. This created a powerful but misleading impression. Police reports concerning distress, lights or ground marks did not independently confirm the spacecraft interiors, medical procedures or alien messages described under hypnosis. The older, better-supported part of the case lent authority to the newer, weaker part.

The process also illustrates how reporting can change the object being reported. Journalists were no longer simply investigating what had happened on 6 December 1978. Their involvement helped create the circumstances in which the fullest version of the story emerged. The case became partly a history of an alleged encounter and partly a history of its own investigation, publication and performance.

## Television gave the legend a second life

By the early 1980s, the Zanfretta story might have faded into the archive of sensational weekly magazines. Instead, it was consolidated through a book and a major regional television production. Di Stefano’s book appeared in 1984 and went through several editions. In December of the same year, RAI broadcast the two-part production *UFO a Genova? Gli incontri ravvicinati di Piero Zanfretta*, made for the series *Come siamo*. Contemporary UFO press also recorded the RAI Liguria broadcast, showing that it was recognised at the time as a significant media treatment rather than merely a later internet curiosity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufomagazines.com/notizie-cun-notizie-cun-no-08/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufomagazines.com">[ufomagazines.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufomagazines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Magazine Archive NOTIZIE C. U. N. — Notizie CUN &#124; UFO Magazine Archive</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Magazine Archive NOTIZIE C. U. N. — Notizie CUN &#124; UFO Magazine Archive</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 1985</span></span></span>

The title itself widened the geographical claim. The events had been associated chiefly with Marzano, Torriglia, Propata and the mountainous hinterland, yet “UFO in Genoa?” connected the case to the region’s major city. For viewers outside Liguria, the simpler geographical label was easier to remember. Over time, “the Zanfretta case”, “the Genoa UFO case” and “the Torriglia abduction” became overlapping versions of the same cultural story.

Television reconstruction also gave the landscape a starring role. Dark roads, wooded slopes, isolated houses and mountain weather supplied visual atmosphere even when no direct record of the original event existed. Such images do not fabricate the location, but they can make uncertainty feel ominous. A difficult night-time environment becomes, through editing and narration, an apparently natural setting for an extraordinary encounter.

Later appearances kept renewing the claim. Zanfretta returned on programmes including the *Maurizio Costanzo Show*, *Il bivio*, *Rebus*, *Ricomincio da qui* and *Mistero*. These broadcasts mixed retrospective testimony, studio argument, location reconstruction and new claims about an alleged object or “sphere” connected with the encounters. In a 2007 programme, photographs and video offered as possible evidence were reportedly too dark to establish anything. The broadcasts therefore extended the narrative without resolving its original evidential weaknesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier_Fortunato_Zanfretta" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pier Fortunato Zanfretta</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pier Fortunato Zanfretta</span></span></span>

One later dispute showed how much control over presentation mattered. After a 2007 edition of *Il bivio*, Di Stefano challenged the programme’s editing, alleging that its contents had been altered. An agreement led to the transmission of a fuller version. Whatever one thinks of the underlying UFO claim, the episode demonstrated that television editing could materially influence how witnesses, investigators and disagreements were understood.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ilgiornale.it/news/telespettatori-rivolta-zanfretta-ruggeri-trasmette-puntata.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilgiornale.it">[il Giornale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilgiornale.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ilgiornale.it.</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 13, 2007</span></span></span>

## From disputed case to regional folklore

The Zanfretta narrative gradually moved beyond factual reporting into fiction, tourism, local events and popular culture. This did not necessarily mean that audiences believed every detail. Folklore can remain attractive precisely because it sits between history, humour, personal testimony and uncertainty.

The 2004 science-fiction film *InvaXön – Alieni in Liguria* was an especially clear example. It converted the alleged beings associated with Zanfretta into the basis of a fictional invasion of Liguria, while Zanfretta appeared as himself. The film did not merely repeat the case; it turned its characters and vocabulary into recognisable regional cultural material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier_Fortunato_Zanfretta" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pier Fortunato Zanfretta</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pier Fortunato Zanfretta</span></span></span>

Torriglia also hosted UFO meetings that drew enthusiasts and speakers to the place most closely associated with the story. Local coverage of a 2015 convention presented the Zanfretta case as part mystery, part historical attraction and part continuing investigation. Once a location hosts commemorative events, lectures and reconstructions, the relationship between story and place becomes self-reinforcing: visitors arrive because of the legend, and their arrival confirms the legend’s cultural importance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.corfole.it/public/pagine_gdm/168_1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: corfole.it">[corfole.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">corfole.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on corfole.it.</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 31, 2015</span></span></span>

The result is a form of regional branding that does not require official promotion or widespread belief. Torriglia remains a real town with a much broader history, but online descriptions, audio guides and paranormal programmes repeatedly identify it through Zanfretta. Genoa and the Ligurian hinterland become searchable destinations within international UFO culture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rsi.ch/play/tv/storie/video/pier-fortunato-zanfretta-lattesa?urn=urn%3Arsi%3Avideo%3A1506152" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rsi.ch">[rsi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rsi.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pier Fortunato Zanfretta: l&#x27;attesa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pier Fortunato Zanfretta: l&#x27;attesa</span></span></span>

This helps explain why the case overshadows almost every other Ligurian report. A weak photograph of a light has little narrative structure. Zanfretta’s story has a protagonist, repeated encounters, named investigators, official documents, dramatic creatures, recordings, locations and decades of media conflict. It is easier to dramatise, package and remember.

## Social media revives weak sightings

Digital platforms changed the mechanics of Ligurian UFO reporting. Earlier stories usually passed through an editor, broadcaster or organised UFO group before reaching a large audience. Today, a short video or enlarged photograph can circulate before anyone establishes its date, direction, distance, camera settings or relationship to known aircraft and wildlife.

A revealing example occurred in July 2023, when an object photographed over Genoa Airport generated debate and was discussed as a possible UFO. The local broadcaster Primocanale explicitly connected the excitement to the regional memory of Zanfretta. Later coverage reported that the object was a gull rather than an unidentified craft. The case is important not because the image was extraordinary, but because it shows how quickly the old legend supplies a frame for interpreting a new ambiguity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.primocanale.it/commenti/28918-da-zanfretta-a-boldi-quanti-avvistamenti-a-genova.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: primocanale.it">[Primocanale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">primocanale.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">28918 da zanfretta a boldi quanti avvistamenti a genova</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Non era un gabbiano. The sequence is now familiar:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* an indistinct object is photographed or filmed;
* the most puzzling frame is isolated from the wider sequence;
* captions ask whether a UFO has appeared over Genoa;
* users invoke Zanfretta or Torriglia;
* reposting spreads faster than identification;
* the correction reaches a smaller or less engaged audience.

Research on Italian social-media communities has found that users often gather around information matching an existing narrative and pay less attention to contrary material. Applied cautiously to UFO content, this helps explain why an exciting first interpretation may survive even after a mundane explanation appears. The emotional value lies in participating in the mystery, whereas “it was probably a bird” brings the story to an abrupt end.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.09494" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Misinformation spreading on Facebook</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">arXiv Misinformation spreading on Facebook</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 28, 2017</span></span></span>

Digital retellings also detach claims from their source hierarchy. A police document, a journalist’s reconstruction, a hypnotic statement, a fictional scene and an anonymous social post may appear together in a single video as though all carry equal weight. Each repetition tends to preserve the most dramatic detail while dropping qualifications about how that detail was obtained.

At the same time, digital archives have one genuine advantage: they make old broadcasts, articles and disagreements easier to compare. Viewers can examine the 1979 hypnosis recording, read Di Stefano’s original articles and study later programmes rather than relying solely on second-hand summaries. The internet can preserve folklore, but it can also expose how the story changed.

## Repetition strengthened the legend, not the proof

Later media coverage has strengthened several limited conclusions. It confirms that the Zanfretta case had substantial contemporary publicity, that named colleagues and officials were involved in recording aspects of the affair, and that Zanfretta maintained his broad account over many years. It also documents the social consequences he described, including ridicule and professional difficulty. These are legitimate parts of the historical record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rinodistefano.com/it/articoli/zanfretta-rivista-gente.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rinodistefano.com">[RinoDiStefano.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rinodistefano.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rino Di Stefano.com ADESS O GLI UFO MI VOGLIONO RAPIRE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rino Di Stefano.com ADESS O GLI UFO MI VOGLIONO RAPIRE</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 20, 1979</span></span></span>

Coverage has not produced equally strong support for the extraterrestrial interpretation. The most elaborate claims remain dependent on Zanfretta’s testimony, especially statements developed during hypnosis. Frequently cited physical details, such as disturbed ground, do not establish their cause. Later television demonstrations and images of the alleged sphere failed to provide clear, independently examinable proof.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier_Fortunato_Zanfretta" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pier Fortunato Zanfretta</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pier Fortunato Zanfretta</span></span></span>

The story’s durability can therefore be explained without assuming that journalists invented the original incident or that Zanfretta was knowingly deceptive. A disturbing event may have occurred; the witness may have been sincere; colleagues may have observed genuine distress; and people in the area may have reported unusual lights. Media processes then selected the most memorable interpretation, encouraged further narrative development and repeatedly returned the resulting account to the public.

For readers assessing Liguria’s wider UFO history, this is the key lesson. Public prominence is not the same as evidential quality. A case can become culturally stronger while remaining factually unresolved. In Liguria, decades of newspaper reports, television appearances, fictional adaptations and online revivals transformed Zanfretta from the witness in one disputed incident into the central character of a regional UFO mythology. The media legacy is real and well documented; the alien encounter remains unproven.
