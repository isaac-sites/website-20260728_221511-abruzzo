## Introduction

Between 1985 and 1988, reports of unusual lights and objects repeatedly appeared along the coast around [Falconara]({{ 'falconara/' | relative_url }}), Ancona, Portonovo, Numana, Sirolo and Monte Conero. The concentration was real at the level of recorded testimony: dozens of entries describe formations over the Adriatic, objects apparently approaching from the sea, hovering lights and, in a few dramatic accounts, shapes entering or emerging from the water. The busiest period was 1987, when reports recurred from winter through late summer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/marche.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Marche_113fca_ancona_coastal_wave_7ad705-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Ancona Wave" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Yet the Ancona wave is not a single well-documented incident. It is a family of short, unevenly preserved reports, largely known through a retrospective catalogue rather than complete contemporary case files. Exact witness statements, weather data, photographs, radar traces and investigation records are absent from most surviving entries. Ancona therefore became [Marche]({{ 'how-marches-ufo-legends-took-shape/' | relative_url }})’s most persistent coastal UFO hotspot in popular memory, but not its strongest body of proof.

## The main coastal locations and dates

The wave began conspicuously in 1985. A catalogue published by the Italian National UFO Centre records a series of reports extending from Falconara in the north to Monte Conero and Portonovo south of Ancona. These were not identical observations, but their closeness in time and geography encouraged investigators and enthusiasts to treat them as a connected coastal sequence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/marche.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span></span></span>

One early entry, dated 5 May with the date itself marked as uncertain, concerns a night watchman in Ancona. Between about 4.40 and 5.10 in the morning, he reportedly used binoculars to watch six unidentified objects manoeuvring just above the sea. The summary gives no name, precise observing position, angular measurements or information about boats and aircraft in the area. The use of binoculars sounds evidentially useful, but without the original interview it is impossible to know whether the instrument clarified the objects or merely enlarged distant points of light.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/marche.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span></span></span>

Falconara produced two striking reports on 16 August. At 4.45 a.m., two luminous spheres were said to perform manoeuvres, merge and descend into the sea. Roughly ninety minutes later, another report described a disc estimated at 80 metres across, carrying an intense light that periodically went out. The enormous size estimate is one reason for caution: without a known distance, an observer cannot reliably calculate the physical diameter of a light or silhouette in the sky. The two Falconara entries may represent separate events, repeated observation of one stimulus, or unrelated reports later placed together in the chronology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/marche.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span></span></span>

The catalogue also records an aviation-connected observation on 21 August. A helicopter pilot associated with the Falconara air base and another person reportedly watched two joined yellow lights with a smaller flashing red light below them for approximately twenty minutes near Paterno di Fabriano. That description is among the more interesting in the sequence because the witness was reportedly familiar with aircraft. Even so, the surviving summary does not state whether the helicopter pilot was flying or on the ground, whether air-traffic control was contacted, or whether the lights were checked against known aircraft movements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/marche.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span></span></span>

Portonovo supplied one of the wave’s most spectacular numerical claims. During the night of 21 August, between midnight and 1 a.m., witnesses allegedly observed 28 drop-shaped objects, described as golden and pale blue, moving in formation. No original count sheet, photograph or list of named witnesses accompanies the catalogue entry. “Twenty-eight UFOs” is therefore best understood as the reported interpretation of a formation of lights, not as independently established evidence of 28 structured vehicles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/marche.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span></span></span>

The year ended with a reported formation over Ancona on 16 November. Several witnesses, including a former municipal police officer, were said to have watched ten red lights surrounding a larger light. The formation allegedly remained stationary for twenty minutes before departing. Multiple witnesses and a long duration could have made this a valuable case, but the record does not preserve their separate descriptions. It is consequently impossible to test whether they agreed before discussing the event with one another or whether the final account combined their impressions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/marche.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span></span></span>

Reports continued in 1986 but were more scattered. A brilliant silvery light containing what appeared to be a large globe was reported at Sirolo in late February. An object with white and red lights was seen at low altitude over Ancona on 28 February, and a whitish light was observed flying over the sea from Torrette on 12 June. On 17 September, a brown disc with a dome was reportedly seen near Monte Conero and, an hour later, at Mazzangrugno. Another metallic ovoid was reported at Poggio on 23 September.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/marche.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span></span></span>

The two brown-disc reports are notable because the descriptions resemble one another and occur only an hour apart. They may indicate the same object travelling through the province, but that conclusion cannot be demonstrated from the catalogue. There are no bearings, flight direction, witness locations or reliable times with which to reconstruct a route. Similar wording may also reflect the way an investigator or compiler summarised originally different accounts.

### The 1987 peak

The density of reports increased sharply in 1987. On 5 January, a red globe was said to move in a zigzag pattern over Torrette. The following morning brought three entries: a low-flying whitish cylinder over Ancona, a disc carrying apparent markings near Poggio, and a white sphere seen by a night watchman in Ancona. Their timing, between approximately 6.30 and 7.20 a.m., raises the possibility of one stimulus being reported from different positions, although the recorded shapes differ considerably.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/marche.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span></span></span>

Torrette then became the centre of a particularly active sequence in May:

* On 10 May, an object resembling two orange lenses divided by a dark central strip reportedly approached from the sea while producing a humming sound.

## Recurring shapes and movements

There was no stable “Ancona object” reported throughout the four years. The descriptions include spheres, discs, cylinders, ovoids, triangles, drops, crescents and formations of simple lights. Colours ranged from white and silver to red, orange, yellow, green, blue and brown. Some objects allegedly hovered; others zigzagged, dived, accelerated, joined together or appeared to enter the Adriatic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/marche.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span></span></span>

This diversity weakens the idea that witnesses were repeatedly observing one recognisable type of craft. A coherent technological phenomenon might reasonably be expected to produce some recurring structural features. Instead, the most consistent feature is geographical: observers were often looking out over the sea from populated coastal settlements.

The apparent water connection is also less decisive than it first appears. From a beach, harbour, cliff or coastal road, a light descending towards a distant horizon can seem to enter the sea even when it remains far above it. The absence of depth cues at night makes height and distance particularly difficult to judge. A light moving away from an observer may dim or disappear below haze, giving the impression of submergence. None of the published summaries records a recovered object, measurable disturbance of the water or simultaneous confirmation from a vessel at the apparent entry point.

Some descriptions are compatible with ordinary aviation lights. White, red and occasionally green lights, especially when arranged in triangles or groups, can belong to aircraft seen head-on, during a turn or in a holding pattern. A flashing red light beneath two steady yellowish lights, as described in the Falconara pilot case, may suggest an airborne object, but the summary contains too little positional information for a secure identification.

Other entries resemble celestial or atmospheric phenomena. Bright meteors can appear as coloured balls, ovals or cylinders and may seem to plunge into the sea. Stars and planets close to the horizon can flash, change colour and appear to move because of atmospheric turbulence and small involuntary eye movements. These explanations are not sufficient for every report, particularly alleged close, structured or long-duration objects, but they show why the visual description alone cannot establish exotic technology.

The mid-June 1987 claim of more than ten reports between Portonovo and Falconara may also illustrate social amplification. Once local people and newspapers become alert to a supposed wave, observations that would normally be ignored are more likely to be noticed, discussed and submitted. This does not imply dishonesty. It means that the number of reports can rise because public attention has changed, even when the number of unusual stimuli has not.

## Why Ancona became the focal point

Geography made the Ancona coast a natural observation corridor. Falconara, Torrette and central Ancona face a busy maritime and aviation environment, while Monte Conero, Portonovo, Sirolo and Numana provide elevated or open views across a broad sea horizon. A light seen from several points along this coast could generate separate reports without being directly overhead at any of them.

The area also contained features that encouraged military interpretations. Falconara had an aviation presence, while military installations and restricted areas on Monte Conero were the subject of intense public interest during the 1980s. The Conero park’s own historical account records political controversy in 1984 over the installations, official secrecy surrounding their functions and public fears about possible missiles or weapons beneath the mountain. The defence minister assured Parliament that the installations posed no danger to local health or safety, but the limited public information left ample room for rumours.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://giornale.parcodelconero.com/old/it/giornale/anno-2013---n4-e-5/pagine-di-memoria-1--cosa-c-sotto-il-conero/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: giornale.parcodelconero.com">[giornale.parcodelconero.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">giornale.parcodelconero.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pagine di memoria 1 – Cosa c’è sotto il Conero?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Parco del Conero News...</span></span></span>

That atmosphere matters because the UFO wave followed immediately afterwards. Strange lights near a secretive military site could readily be interpreted as experimental aircraft, surveillance activity or something even more extraordinary. However, proximity does not prove a connection. No surviving evidence shows that the 1985–88 sightings were detected by Conero installations, involved military exercises, or prompted an official alert at the base.

Falconara’s airport and the coastal flight corridor present a more ordinary but potentially relevant connection. Aircraft approaching or turning at night can appear nearly stationary when travelling towards an observer, then move abruptly when the viewing angle changes. Landing lights can dominate the outline of an aircraft so completely that its body remains invisible. Unfortunately, the published reports have not been matched against period flight logs or air-traffic records.

Local history also contributed to the hotspot’s reputation. Ancona and the wider Marche coast already possessed a substantial record of reported aerial phenomena, including the widely observed luminous passage of October 1954 and Adriatic stories from the national wave of 1978. Contemporary coverage of the 1954 event described observations across a long stretch of coastline and treated Ancona as a central reporting point.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://anconarivistaacolori.it/la-volta-che-gli-u-f-o-attraversarono-le-marche-a-1-000-km-h/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: anconarivistaacolori.it">[Ancona ri&#124;vista a colori]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">anconarivistaacolori.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ancona ri&#124;vista a coloriLa volta che gli U.F.O. attraversarono le Marche a 1.000 km/h - Ancona ri&#124;vista a colori...</span></span></span> By the mid-1980s, new coastal lights were therefore entering an established regional narrative rather than being interpreted in isolation.

## How strong was the investigation?

Italy had an official procedure for unidentified-object reports throughout this period. ## Why the evidence remains inconclusive

The Ancona wave is stronger as evidence of a sustained reporting pattern than as evidence of a single unexplained vehicle or extraordinary operation over the Adriatic. Several factors prevent a firmer conclusion.

First, the chronology depends heavily on one compressed secondary catalogue. Its entries contain uncertain dates, inconsistent detail and occasional interpretive phrases such as “probably the same object”. Those comments may be reasonable, but the underlying testimony needed to test them is not supplied.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/marche.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionalewww.centroufologiconazionale.net</span></span></span>

Second, the accounts do not form a consistent physical profile. The reported objects vary dramatically in shape, colour, behaviour and apparent size. This is more readily compatible with a mixture of stimuli and perceptions than with repeated observation of one machine.

Third, the coastal setting creates predictable visual ambiguities. Distant aircraft, vessels, astronomical objects, meteors and lights distorted by haze can all appear unusual over a dark, largely featureless sea. Claims of objects entering or emerging from the water are especially dependent on accurate distance and horizon judgement, neither of which can be reconstructed from the surviving summaries.

Fourth, the strongest-sounding witnesses do not come with the strongest surviving files. A helicopter pilot, night watchmen, fishermen and a former police officer are all mentioned. Their occupations may suggest attentiveness or familiarity with the local environment, but professional status cannot replace measurements, independent statements or technical checks.

Finally, later reporting has not materially strengthened the 1985–88 cases. The Conero continues to be described in local press as an area associated with unusual lights, but repetition of the hotspot label is not new corroboration. A 2015 newspaper report, for example, referred broadly to decades of lights around the mountain without supplying fresh evidence for the mid-1980s incidents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ilrestodelcarlino.it/ancona/cronaca/ufo-sopra-loreto-942855c9" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilrestodelcarlino.it">[Il Resto del Carlino]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilrestodelcarlino.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">soprattutto alle pendici del</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Resto del Carlino“Ho visto un ufo sui cieli di Loreto”February 18, 2015 — 18 Feb 2015 — ANCONA Un avvistamento di un Ufo (Ancona), Stu...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 18, 2015</span></span></span>

The fairest verdict is therefore mixed. It is plausible that some witnesses saw genuinely unfamiliar phenomena and that a small number of reports might have remained unresolved even after competent checking. It is not justified to combine every entry into a single four-year event or to treat the catalogue as proof of craft operating from the Adriatic. The surviving evidence supports a coastal sighting wave: a period when unusual observations, local expectations, military secrecy and difficult viewing conditions converged around Ancona. It does not establish what, if anything, lay behind the wave as a whole.

      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
