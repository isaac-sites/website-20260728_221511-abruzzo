## Introduction

Many strange lights reported from [Abruzzo]({{ 'why-abruzzo-became-italys-ufo-hotspot/' | relative_url }})’s coast could have had ordinary origins: aircraft approaching or crossing the Adriatic, fishing and leisure vessels, navigation lights, low cloud, mist, atmospheric refraction and the human difficulty of judging a lone light over a dark sea. None of these explanations proves that every report was mistaken. They do, however, provide credible alternatives for sightings that lacked photographs, radar data, precise bearings or independent measurements.

This matters especially when assessing the celebrated coastal wave of autumn 1978. [Witnesses]({{ 'witnesses/' | relative_url }}) undoubtedly reported something, and the concern was serious enough for the Italian Navy to conduct 20 surveillance missions totalling 230 hours. Yet those patrols detected nothing unusual. That result does not retrospectively identify every light, but it weakens claims that a large, persistent or mechanically disruptive phenomenon was operating openly offshore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/documenti/attiparlamentari.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico NazionaleLe interrogazioni parlamentari iSuccessivamente la marina militare disponeva un organico servizio di sorveglian...</span></span></span>

The most sensible approach is therefore not to choose between “alien craft” and “nothing happened”. It is to ask which familiar coastal stimuli could have appeared unfamiliar under the conditions described.

## Aircraft and lights over the Adriatic

Aircraft are among the strongest candidates for reports involving bright lights that appear stationary, move slowly and then accelerate or vanish. Abruzzo Airport lies beside Pescara, close to the coast, while the region’s skies also form part of the wider network of civil and military movements across central Italy and the Adriatic. A witness looking out to sea may see an aircraft approaching almost directly along the line of sight, with its landing lights facing towards the observer. For several minutes, there may be little obvious sideways movement. Only when the aircraft turns does its speed and direction become clear.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abruzzoairport.com/en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abruzzoairport.com">[abruzzoairport.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abruzzoairport.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeroporto Internazionale d&#x27;Abruzzo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">### MANIFESTAZIONE DI INTERESSE PER IL REPERIMENTO DI OPE-RATORI ECONOMICI INTERESSATI ALLA STIPULA DI UN CON-TRATTO DI SUB CONCESSIONE D...</span></span></span>

Aviation authorities treat such visual errors as real safety concerns, not as signs of foolishness or unreliable character. The US Federal Aviation Administration warns that a stationary light against a dark background can appear to move, an effect known as autokinesis. It also notes that haze can make objects seem farther away and that isolated bright lights can distort perceived distance. SKYbrary, an aviation-safety reference, similarly describes how shorelines, cloud banks and scattered lights can create false horizons at night.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aip_html/chap8_section_1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[faa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">False horizon.Sloping cloud formations, an obscured horizon, a dark scene spread with ground lights and stars, and certain geometric patt...</span></span></span>

These effects are particularly relevant to Abruzzo because the offshore view often supplies almost no fixed reference points. An aircraft seen over the land can be compared with hills, buildings and roads. The same aircraft seen beyond the beach may be reduced to one light against black sky and water. Its height, heading and distance then become largely matters of impression.

Aircraft do not comfortably explain every dramatic retelling of the [1978 wave]({{ '1978-wave/' | relative_url }}). They cannot by themselves account for reports of disturbed water or mist rising from the sea. But those added details are often preserved through later journalism rather than through complete contemporary logs. For the simpler and more common reports of moving or hovering lights, aircraft remain a leading possibility.

## Boats can resemble lights in the sky

Abruzzo’s coastline is not an empty viewing platform facing an empty sea. Pescara has berths for fishing and leisure craft, while ports and smaller landing places at Giulianova, Ortona and Vasto support commercial, fishing and recreational traffic. Along the southern coast, fixed fishing platforms extend over the water. A light seen near the horizon may therefore belong to a vessel, harbour approach, fishing installation or buoy rather than to an aerial object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://old.porto.ancona.it/en/ports/port-of-pescara" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: old.porto.ancona.it">[Porto di Ancona]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">old.porto.ancona.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Porto di Ancona PORT OF PESCARA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Porto di AnconaPORT OF PESCARA - Porto di AnconaThe port of Pescara is located within the main coastal city of Abruzzo. At night, the hull of a distant boat can disappear into the darkness while its lamps remain visible. The observer sees light without a supporting structure and naturally places it in the sky rather than on the water. Waves can intermittently obscure the lamp, making it blink irregularly. A vessel turning, pitching or passing behind mist may appear to rise, descend, change colour or disappear.

Fishing operations add further complications. Bright deck lamps are used in some fisheries to illuminate working areas or attract marine life. Research using satellite sensors has shown that fleets employing strong fishing lights can be detected from space, demonstrating how conspicuous such sources can become against an otherwise dark sea. This does not establish that any particular Abruzzo report involved a light-fishing vessel, but it shows that intense offshore illumination is not inherently extraordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352938516301124" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sciencedirect.com.</span></span></span>

Navigation lights can also create misleading arrangements. Depending on a vessel’s orientation, an observer may see one lamp, a pair of lights or a changing sequence of red, green and white. Two boats at different distances can appear to belong to one large object. Conversely, several lamps on a single vessel can be interpreted as separate objects moving together.

This possibility is especially relevant to reports made by people onshore. A fisherman aboard another boat may recognise maritime lighting patterns more readily, but recognition is not guaranteed. Research on night-time lookout duties shows that identifying vessels depends on background brightness, atmospheric clarity, target distance, lamp intensity and the observer’s level of dark adaptation. Even experienced mariners work under difficult perceptual conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-navigation/article/nighttime-lookout-duty-the-role-of-ambient-light-levels-and-dark-adaptation/6EA9B79C787F32DA9978A095701CD654" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cambridge.org.</span></span></span>

## Cloud, mist and unusual sea conditions

Abruzzo’s geography places the Adriatic beside a steep rise towards the Apennines. Coastal air can therefore be influenced by sea breezes, moist marine layers and weather moving down from higher ground. Low cloud, haze and mist can transform an ordinary light without creating any unusual object at all.

Water droplets scatter light. A lamp seen through mist may acquire a glowing halo, lose its sharp outline or illuminate a patch of cloud around it. When the obscuring layer moves, the light may brighten, dim, enlarge or seem to change direction. Fog can also be transported from the sea onto coastal land, while low cloud may occur close enough to the surface to be experienced as mist.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/weather/types-of-weather/fog" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: weather.metoffice.gov.uk">[Met Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">weather.metoffice.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Met Office What is fog?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Met Office What is fog?</span></span></span>

This offers a modest explanation for some accounts of luminous haze over the Adriatic. A boat’s lamp shining into low cloud could create a diffuse illuminated area that appears detached from the source. Aircraft lights behind broken cloud can produce a similar effect. Moving gaps in the cloud may make the glow seem to pulse or travel even when the light source follows an ordinary course.

Mist also obscures boundaries. If the sea horizon disappears, the witness loses the main line separating sky from water. A light on a vessel can then appear airborne, while a light in the sky may seem to touch or enter the sea. Aviation guidance specifically recognises that obscured horizons, sloping cloud and patterns of ground lights can cause serious errors in orientation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/Air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim_html/chap8_section_1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on faa.gov.</span></span></span>

The more dramatic language associated with 1978 — “boiling” water, columns rising from the sea or luminous masses among the waves — is harder to evaluate. Such descriptions could refer to spray, localised rain, foam, illuminated mist or agitated water, but the surviving public accounts generally lack the measurements needed to choose among them. Later articles repeat these details, sometimes alongside openly speculative stories about underwater bases or battles between unknown craft. The growth of those narratives makes it difficult to separate original observation from later interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.italiani.it/ufo-1978-la-notte-degli-avvistamenti-in-abruzzo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.italiani.it">[italiani.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.italiani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo 1978 la notte degli avvistamenti in abruzzo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ufo 1978 la notte degli avvistamenti in abruzzo</span></span></span>

Natural marine phenomena should not be forced into every case. A waterspout, for example, is a real rotating column beneath a cloud, but it would normally be associated with observable weather conditions and does not explain a point of light. The safer conclusion is narrower: changing cloud, mist, spray and visibility can alter how familiar lights appear and can make the sea itself look unusually active.

## Refraction can lift and distort distant objects

Atmospheric refraction is a particularly relevant coastal mechanism because light travelling close to the horizon passes through layers of air at different temperatures and densities. When colder air lies beneath warmer air, a temperature inversion can bend light and produce superior mirages. Distant ships, coastlines or lights may appear raised above their true position, stretched vertically or repeated in broken layers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu/hbase/atmos/mirage.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu">[gsu.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gsu.edu.</span></span></span>

The most elaborate form is often called a Fata Morgana. It can turn a distant vessel into a narrow tower, a floating block or a collection of disconnected shapes. At night, however, the observer may see only distorted lamps rather than the ship itself. A navigation light displaced upwards by refraction can look like an object hovering above the sea. If the air layers change, the image can shift or disappear quickly.

Refraction is strongest as an explanation when several conditions coincide:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the object is close to the visible horizon;
* the sea and air have markedly different temperatures;
* winds are light enough for stable air layers to develop;
* the apparent shape changes while the object remains in roughly the same area;
* distant ships or coastline features also look raised or distorted.

</div>

It is less persuasive for a light reported high overhead or moving across a large part of the sky. Nor should every unusual horizon sighting automatically be labelled a mirage. Confirmation would require weather data, an accurate viewing direction and preferably photographs showing comparable distortion elsewhere along the horizon.

Even so, the mechanism deserves more attention than it usually receives in popular accounts of the Abruzzo wave. Scientific work on extreme visual ranges shows that atmospheric structure can allow distant objects or lights to be seen under conditions that depart significantly from ordinary geometric expectations. A witness may therefore be observing a real light while being badly mistaken about where it is.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://opg.optica.org/ao/abstract.cfm?URI=ao-59-21-F11" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: opg.optica.org">[Optica Publishing Group]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">opg.optica.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on optica.org.</span></span></span>

## Why distance and scale are easily misjudged

The central problem with a nocturnal coastal sighting is not necessarily identifying the colour of a light. It is determining its distance. Without distance, estimates of size, speed and altitude become unreliable.

A small lamp on a nearby boat and a powerful light on a distant aircraft can occupy the same apparent angle in the eye. If the witness assumes that the source is far away, it will seem enormous. If the assumed distance changes during the sighting, the calculated speed can become equally exaggerated. Statements such as “it crossed kilometres in seconds” are meaningful only when the object’s range has been independently established.

Laboratory research confirms that a visible horizon contributes to human judgements of absolute distance. Remove or obscure that horizon and the task becomes harder. Over the dark Adriatic, the witness may have no textured ground, known building or nearby object against which to compare the light.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3315150/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed Central (PMC)]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nih.gov.</span></span></span>

Several perceptual effects can then combine:

**Apparent motion.** A stationary point of light may seem to wander when stared at against a featureless background. Small eye movements are interpreted as movement of the source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skybrary.aero/index.php/articles/autokinetic-effect" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skybrary.aero">[Skybrary]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skybrary.aero</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">To prevent this illusion, focus the eyes on objects at varying distances and avoid fixating on one target...</span></span></span>

**Observer movement.** Someone on a boat is pitching, rolling and drifting. Even a witness standing on a beach may move their head while trying to keep a light in view. Without fixed references, this can make the target appear to dart or oscillate.

**Changing brightness.** An aircraft banking, a boat turning or a lamp passing behind thin cloud may dim suddenly. The brain can interpret fading as rapid recession and brightening as approach.

**Multiple lights.** Separate boats, aircraft or shore lamps may be mentally grouped into one structured object. If one light disappears, the supposed object may seem to rotate or change shape.

**Poor dark adaptation.** Street lighting, torches, vehicle headlights and illuminated cabins reduce sensitivity to faint detail. A bright point remains visible while the surrounding hull, cloud or landscape disappears. Maritime research identifies dark adaptation and ambient lighting as important factors in detecting and recognising navigation lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-navigation/article/nighttime-lookout-duty-the-role-of-ambient-light-levels-and-dark-adaptation/6EA9B79C787F32DA9978A095701CD654" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cambridge.org">[Cambridge University Press]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cambridge.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cambridge.org.</span></span></span>

These are not signs that witnesses invented their experiences. They are normal limits of human vision, strong enough to be included in professional training for pilots and mariners.

## Which explanations fit the 1978 reports best?

The surviving record does not support one universal solution. The coastal wave appears to have combined numerous reports made on different dates, from different positions and under poorly documented conditions. A reasonable assessment therefore works by report type rather than by treating the entire episode as one object repeatedly returning.

For isolated white, red or flashing lights moving slowly offshore, aircraft and vessel traffic are the simplest candidates. For lights described as hovering near the horizon, an approaching aircraft, distant fishing boat or navigation light distorted by autokinesis is plausible. For luminous patches, blurred objects or apparent lights within cloud, mist and reflected illumination deserve consideration. Reports of elongated or elevated structures close to the horizon may be compatible with refraction.

Claims involving physical interaction with the sea are less easily resolved. They may preserve observations of spray, weather or boat activity, but later retellings do not provide enough technical detail to establish that. The absence of unusual findings during the Navy’s extensive patrols is important here. Twenty missions and 230 hours of surveillance did not reproduce the alarming phenomena. That weighs against a continuing offshore process large enough to disturb the sea repeatedly, although it cannot disprove brief events that occurred before or between patrols.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/documenti/attiparlamentari.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico NazionaleLe interrogazioni parlamentari iSuccessivamente la marina militare disponeva un organico servizio di sorveglian...</span></span></span>

The resulting experience could be genuinely striking and difficult to identify even though every component was conventional.

* exact date and time, preferably to the minute;

* photographs retaining original timestamps and metadata;
* aircraft and vessel tracking records;
* accounts from observers at separated locations;
* radar, harbour or patrol records where available.

</div>

Modern scientific proposals for studying unidentified aerial phenomena emphasise the same principle: cameras alone are rarely sufficient. Reliable identification benefits from triangulation, radar-derived range, multispectral imaging, acoustic information and environmental sensors. These methods distinguish a distant light from a nearby object and apparent motion from measured motion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18566" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

Most celebrated Abruzzo accounts do not preserve that level of evidence. Their unresolved status therefore often means “insufficiently documented”, not “shown to defy conventional explanation”. The region’s coastal setting supplies several powerful sources of misidentification, while the 1978 Navy patrols supplied no confirming physical or operational evidence of an extraordinary offshore presence.

Ordinary explanations consequently weaken the more sensational interpretation of the Abruzzo wave without erasing its historical importance. People reported memorable sights, newspapers amplified them and the authorities responded. What remains uncertain is not whether reports occurred, but whether any of them survived the basic tests needed to rule out aircraft, boats, weather, refraction and night-time visual error.
