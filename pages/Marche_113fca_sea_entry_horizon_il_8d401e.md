## Introduction

Reports of luminous objects apparently entering or emerging from the Adriatic form one of the most memorable strands of [Marche]({{ 'how-marches-ufo-legends-took-shape/' | relative_url }})’s UFO history. The strongest conclusion, however, is not that unidentified craft were operating underwater. It is that a light descending towards a dark sea can appear to “submerge” when it merely passes below the observer’s visible horizon, fades in haze or is hidden by waves and atmospheric distortion. Distance, height and direction are exceptionally difficult to judge when the only visible feature is a point of light against a nearly featureless sky and water.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Marche_113fca_sea_entry_horizon_il_8d401e-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Sea Entry" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The regional record contains genuine reports of this kind, including a terse 1954 catalogue entry from Marotta describing a cigar-shaped object that “sank into the sea”. Yet the surviving summaries generally lack the measurements, independent viewpoints, radar data or physical effects needed to distinguish real water entry from disappearance along the line of sight. Sea-entry stories therefore matter less as proof of underwater UFOs than as a clear example of how the Adriatic horizon can turn an ambiguous observation into a dramatic narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/marche.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[centroufologiconazionale.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico NazionaleMarche&gt;MARCHE principali avvistamenti dal 1954. ## Reports of objects descending into water

The clearest Marche catalogue example dates from the morning of 25 October 1954. The Italian National UFO Centre’s regional chronology records that at Marotta, on the coast of what is now the province of Pesaro and Urbino, a cigar-shaped object was seen to “sink into the sea”. The entry appears amid a much larger sequence of luminous-object reports across Marche that day, but it provides no witness names, exact observation point, angular measurements, duration, weather conditions or indication that anyone saw a splash, plume or illuminated water.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/marche.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico NazionaleMarche&gt;MARCHE principali avvistamenti dal 1954. Un sigaro che sprofonda in mare. Sfera lucentissima. Ovale con...</span></span></span>

That absence of detail is crucial. “It entered the sea” may describe what the witness inferred rather than what was directly observed. A distant light seen moving downwards can vanish at the horizon while remaining far above the physical water surface. Unless its range and altitude are known, the observer cannot tell whether it was nearby and low, distant and high, or travelling away over the curvature of the Earth.

The wider events of 1954 make an astronomical explanation particularly relevant. On 25 October, observers across numerous Marche towns reported brilliant spheres, cylinders and objects with coloured trails moving rapidly towards the horizon. A luminous meteor or fireball can appear elongated, descend steeply and disappear beyond the visible skyline, producing descriptions such as “cigar”, “flaming object” or “falling into the sea”. The Marotta report may have been a separate event, but the brief catalogue entry does not provide enough information to separate it confidently from the morning’s broader luminous phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/marche.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico NazionaleMarche&gt;MARCHE principali avvistamenti dal 1954. Maritime claims became more elaborate during the Italian UFO wave of 1978. Reports from the central Adriatic, especially between the southern Marche coast and Abruzzo, included lights over the water, luminous globes, alleged columns of water and suggestions that objects were rising from or descending beneath the sea. Later retellings linked these stories into the so-called “Adriatic Triangle”, sometimes adding claims of submarine conflict or extraterrestrial bases. The Marche–Abruzzo boundary is relevant because San Benedetto del Tronto was a major fishing port and a natural reporting centre for events seen offshore. But later stories often combine observations made on different nights, from different vessels and at different distances. Lights near the horizon, disturbances in the water, fog, compass problems and unrelated maritime accidents can consequently become parts of a single mystery even when no surviving file demonstrates that they had one cause. Retrospective reports about the 1978 wave openly show how speculation expanded from unidentified lights into stories of underwater battles and hidden bases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.italiani.it/ufo-1978-la-notte-degli-avvistamenti-in-abruzzo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: italiani.it">[italiani.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">italiani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UF O in Abruzzo: quarant&#x27;anni fa la notte degli avvistamenti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO in Abruzzo: quarant&#x27;anni fa la notte degli avvistamentiNovember 19, 2018 — 19 Nov 2018 — UFO: misteriose luci sul mare. ## How the horizon creates false submergence

The sea horizon is not a physical line painted on the water. It is the apparent boundary at which the visible surface and sky meet from the observer’s position. An object can pass behind that boundary without touching the sea, just as the Sun appears to sink into the ocean at sunset while remaining far beyond Earth. The United States Naval Observatory describes the horizon as the apparent intersection of the sky with the Earth’s surface around the observer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/RST_defs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aa.usno.navy.mil">[US Naval Observatory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aa.usno.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on navy.mil.</span></span></span>

Observer height controls how far away that boundary lies. Someone standing near beach level has a horizon only a few kilometres away; a person on a cliff, tall building or ship’s bridge can see considerably farther. A distant aircraft, meteor, flare or celestial object may therefore remain well above the sea but disappear below the observer’s personal horizon as it moves away or as Earth’s curvature intervenes.

The illusion becomes stronger at night because most ordinary depth cues vanish. Over open water there may be no trees, buildings, illuminated roads or textured ground against which to judge scale and motion. Aviation authorities specifically warn that flight over water or other featureless terrain can distort perceptions of altitude and distance. Bright isolated lights can seem nearer than they are, while an obscured or false horizon can make their path appear steeper or lower.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aip_html/chap8_section_1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[FAA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bright runway and approach lighting systems, especially where few lights illuminate the surrounding terrain, may create the illusion of l...</span></span></span>

A witness may therefore see the following sequence:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. A light appears above the dark sea.
2. It moves downwards relative to the visible horizon.
3. Its apparent speed or brightness changes as it travels away.
4. It reaches the horizon line and becomes dimmer.
5. It disappears, leading the observer to conclude that it entered the water.

</div>

Every stage can occur without contact with the sea. The light may have passed behind Earth’s curvature, entered haze, moved behind a cloud bank, switched off, become too faint to see or been temporarily obscured by waves. If the object was a meteor, its light may simply have ceased as it broke up or cooled.

### A point of light provides almost no scale

The human visual system normally estimates distance by comparing an object with known surroundings, using its apparent size, surface detail, motion against a background and the slightly different views received by each eye. A small unresolved light offers few of these clues. A bright source several kilometres away can look similar to a weaker source much closer to the observer.

This creates a serious problem for sea-entry testimony. To determine whether a light reached the water, the witness must know both its distance and its angle above or below the true horizon. Without a recognisable structure, radar range or a second observation site, neither value is secure. Descriptions such as “large”, “low”, “just offshore” or “moving towards us” may be sincere impressions rather than measurements.

The problem is well known in night aviation. The United States Federal Aviation Administration warns that darkness, water and isolated lights can create false horizons and errors in perceived altitude and range. These warnings concern pilots with training and instruments, which underlines how vulnerable an unassisted coastal observer can be.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aip_html/chap8_section_1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[FAA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bright runway and approach lighting systems, especially where few lights illuminate the surrounding terrain, may create the illusion of l...</span></span></span>

### Atmospheric refraction moves the apparent horizon

Air over the Adriatic is not optically uniform. Layers of different temperature and density bend light, especially along shallow sight lines close to the sea surface. The World Meteorological Organization notes that mirages can make normally hidden objects visible, cause visible objects to disappear or produce displaced and distorted images. “Looming”, associated with strong refraction, can make an object below the geometrical horizon appear raised above it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/mirage.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cloudatlas.wmo.int">[International Cloud Atlas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cloudatlas.wmo.int</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A mirage may occur as a lower mirage (inferior mirage) over intensely heated water surfaces, soils, beaches, roads, etc., or as an upper...</span></span></span>

The reverse transition can be equally misleading. If a refracted image weakens or collapses as atmospheric conditions change, a distant ship’s light, aircraft or celestial source may seem to descend suddenly or disappear into the water. Haze can also remove the lower part of an object first, reinforcing the impression of submergence.

Refraction should not be invoked automatically for every report. It requires suitable atmospheric conditions and is only one possible mechanism. Its importance is that the apparent horizon is not always identical to the geometrical horizon. A witness may be accurately reporting what the light seemed to do while still being mistaken about its physical path.

### Motion towards the observer can look vertical

A light travelling mainly towards or away from a witness may show little sideways movement. Small changes in altitude can then dominate its apparent motion, making a conventional aircraft appear to hover, climb vertically or descend towards a single point.

Aircraft landing lights are especially deceptive because they can remain bright and nearly stationary when the aircraft is approaching head-on. When the aircraft turns, descends, switches lights or changes direction, the source may suddenly dim or vanish. Over the sea, where the aircraft’s body and navigation lights may be difficult to resolve, the entire event can be interpreted as one luminous object dropping into the Adriatic.

Flares and signal rockets present a different but related problem. A descending flare may genuinely approach the sea, but its light can extinguish before impact. From a distant shore this can be remembered as an object entering the water, even though the witness never observed a physical body or contact point. Marche’s coastal and maritime setting makes aircraft, marine signals, military activity and distant vessel lights necessary checks in any investigation.

## What would prove an underwater entry?

A light disappearing at the horizon is not enough to establish that an object entered the Adriatic. A credible underwater claim needs evidence showing both a controlled trajectory to a known point and a physical interaction with the water.

The most valuable evidence would include several mutually supporting elements:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* **Independent observations from separated locations.** Bearings from two or more known positions could be used to triangulate the object’s range and path.
* **Precise times and directions.** Investigators need the observation point, compass bearing, elevation angle, duration and movement, not simply “offshore” or “towards the sea”.
* **A visible contact event.** A splash, plume, wake, steam cloud or illuminated disturbance should occur at the same place and time as the apparent entry.
* **Radar, sonar or vessel data.** Radar could establish an airborne track, while sonar or hydrophone records might show whether anything continued underwater.
* **Uninterrupted imagery.** Video should include the horizon, fixed landmarks and enough time before and after disappearance to rule out camera movement, loss of focus or an object merely fading.
* **Environmental and traffic checks.** Investigators should compare the report with aircraft routes, shipping positions, flares, military exercises, astronomical events, weather and atmospheric refraction conditions.
* **Prompt original testimony.** Statements recorded separately soon after the event are more useful than recollections reconstructed years later.

</div>

## How the Marche claims should be classified

The available cases are best divided into three levels rather than placed together under a single label.

**Apparent sea entry:** A light descends to the horizon and disappears. This is the category into which the Marotta catalogue entry currently falls. It preserves a witness interpretation but lacks independent proof of water contact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/marche.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico NazionaleMarche&gt;MARCHE principali avvistamenti dal 1954. **Possible interaction with the sea:** Witnesses describe a light and a water disturbance at approximately the same time. Such reports deserve closer examination, particularly when made by experienced fishermen, but coincidence, waves, marine activity, flares and errors in distance must still be excluded. The retrospective 1978 accounts contain claims of luminous water and columns rising from the Adriatic, yet the accessible versions do not provide the contemporaneous measurements needed to connect those effects securely to a particular object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.italiani.it/ufo-1978-la-notte-degli-avvistamenti-in-abruzzo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.italiani.it">[italiani.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.italiani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo 1978 la notte degli avvistamenti in abruzzo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Marche, lived an unforgettable night, the night of the UFO.... **Instrumentally supported transition:** An object is tracked through the air to a measured entry point and then detected beneath the surface, preferably by independent systems. No publicly documented Marche case found in the regional catalogue or official reporting material reaches this standard.

This classification does not imply that witnesses fabricated their experiences. People may accurately remember an unusual light, sudden disappearance or strange-looking patch of water while drawing an incorrect conclusion about distance and depth. The distinction is between the reported observation and the physical interpretation placed upon it.

## The most likely reading of the record

Marche’s sea-entry stories remain culturally important because the Adriatic is central to the region’s landscape and maritime identity. A mysterious light above a dark, open sea naturally invites a more dramatic interpretation than the same light seen above a town. During a major reporting wave such as 1978, repeated accounts can also prime later observers to interpret any descending light as part of an established underwater mystery.

The evidence nevertheless weakens rather than strengthens as the claim becomes more specific. There is good evidence that people reported strange lights over and near the Adriatic. There is weaker evidence that some lights appeared to descend to the sea. There is much weaker evidence that any object physically crossed the water surface, and no accessible regional case provides a documented airborne-to-underwater track supported by instruments.

Horizon geometry is therefore not a complete explanation of every maritime report, but it is the first mechanism that must be tested. A light that vanishes at sea level has not necessarily entered the sea; it has only disappeared from the observer’s view. Until a case supplies range, trajectory, independent viewpoints and a confirmed water interaction, “sea entry” should be treated as an appearance described by witnesses, not an established event.
