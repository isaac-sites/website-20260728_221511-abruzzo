## Introduction

The alleged RS/33 papers are the foundation of the claim that Fascist Italy recovered an unknown aircraft in [Lombardy]({{ 'lombardys-ufo-history-between-evidence/' | relative_url }}) in 1933 and created a secret committee to study it. Without those papers, the story of a crash near Magenta and a hidden programme led by Guglielmo Marconi has almost no contemporary evidential support.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Lombardy_9b46d3_rs33_document_debate_bbfcb0-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for RS 33 Files" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The central problem is not simply that the documents describe something extraordinary. It is that they surfaced anonymously more than sixty years after the supposed events, have no demonstrated route from a Fascist archive to their present custodian, and contain few of the bureaucratic features that would allow historians to verify where they came from. Supporters say examinations showed that the paper and ink were compatible with the 1930s. Critics reply that old materials can be used to create new documents, and that material dating cannot authenticate the words written on them.

On the evidence publicly available, the RS/33 collection is best treated as an unverified private archive, with serious indications of modern fabrication rather than a secure historical record.

## What the documents allegedly contain

The papers began reaching Italian UFO researchers and media organisations by post in the second half of the 1990s. Roberto Pinotti, then president of the Centro Ufologico Nazionale, said the sender was an anonymous person connected to a former member of the supposed RS/33 organisation. The source became known only by a pseudonym, usually rendered as “Mister X”. ## The missing chain of custody

The most serious weakness is provenance: the documented history of who created, held and transferred an object or record. For an archival document, good provenance normally links the paper to a named office, official file, registry entry, collection or identifiable owner.

No comparable chain has been established for the RS/33 papers. The alleged source has not been publicly identified, the supposed original government file has not been located, and there is no known accession record showing that the documents ever belonged to an Italian state, military or scientific archive. Some were reportedly sent to journalists as unsolicited postal packages rather than discovered during systematic archival research. Several recipients apparently declined to publish them because they suspected a hoax.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.luxaliena.it/dossier/ufo-di-mussolini/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: luxaliena.it">[luxaliena.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">luxaliena.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo di mussolini</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">falso. Nel frattempo Mister X inviò copie della documentazione ad altri... Due pagine dei fascist file in cui si menzionano l&#x27;UFO e il G...</span></span></span>

An anonymous source does not automatically make a document false. Whistleblowers and private families sometimes preserve genuine records. But anonymity becomes much more damaging when it is combined with the absence of an independently verifiable source file. Researchers cannot examine the history of the collection, interview its custodian, compare it with neighbouring papers or determine whether pages have been removed, rearranged or manufactured.

The provenance problem also prevents a basic archival test: locating related documents created by other offices. A secret committee studying an aircraft would probably require communications, travel authorisations, security arrangements, personnel instructions, technical reports, storage decisions or expenditure. Not every record would necessarily survive, but a programme involving prominent scientists, police officials, military personnel and a major aircraft factory would normally generate records in more than one administrative system.

No securely catalogued RS/33 appointment order, budget, minutes, technical report or correspondence chain has emerged from the principal Fascist-era collections. The papers therefore remain detached objects rather than part of a recoverable bureaucratic record. Critical accounts contrast them with genuine Milanese prefectural files concerning airports, suspicious aircraft, border violations and aviation accidents. Those authentic files have identifiable archival locations and administrative contexts, but have not produced evidence of an extraterrestrial recovery programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vice.com/it/article/x-files-fascisti-primo-avvistamento-ufo-mussolini/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vice.com">[VICE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vice.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">x files fascisti primo avvistamento ufo mussolini</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gli &#x27;X-Files fascisti&#x27; e la strana teoria del primo...2 Sept 2021 — I fantomatici documenti riservati del “Gabinetto RS/33” sono sta...</span></span></span>

## Stamps, protocols and bureaucratic form

Critics often describe the RS/33 controversy as a problem of bureaucracy rather than UFO theory. The issue is whether the papers behave like genuine records produced by Fascist government offices.

Many of the circulated pages reportedly lack ordinary authentication features such as protocol numbers, incoming or outgoing registry marks, routing annotations, office stamps and filing references. Some resemble private correspondence or drafts rather than completed documents transmitted through an official system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vice.com/it/article/x-files-fascisti-primo-avvistamento-ufo-mussolini/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vice.com">[VICE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vice.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">x files fascisti primo avvistamento ufo mussolini</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gli &#x27;X-Files fascisti&#x27; e la strana teoria del primo...2 Sept 2021 — I fantomatici documenti riservati del “Gabinetto RS/33” sono sta...</span></span></span>

A protocol number is not merely decorative. It links a document to a register that records when it was received or issued, which office handled it and where it was filed. Stamps and handwritten routing marks can likewise reveal the path a paper took through an administration. Even confidential records may bear internal controls, precisely because officials need to track who is permitted to see them.

Secrecy therefore does not resolve the difficulty. A secret service or restricted committee may conceal a file from the public, but it still needs internal procedures. Orders must reach recipients; reports must be returned; documents must be copied, stored or destroyed under authority. A collection that describes an elaborate secret structure while offering almost no verifiable administrative trail is historically weak.

Some individual pieces may have been drafts, personal copies or notes retained outside the normal registry system. That could explain the absence of a stamp on one or two pages. It is less convincing as an explanation for the whole collection, especially when no corresponding registered copies have been found elsewhere.

## Can paper and ink dating prove authenticity?

Supporters of the RS/33 papers have cited an examination commissioned from consultant Antonio Garavaglia. Pro-RS/33 accounts say that the paper and ink were found to be consistent with materials from the 1930s, sometimes describing the process as carbon dating or forensic analysis. This reported finding helped persuade Pinotti to publish material he had initially suspected might be false.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.luxaliena.it/dossier/ufo-di-mussolini/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: luxaliena.it">[Lux Aliena]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">luxaliena.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo di mussolini</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">falso. Nel frattempo Mister X inviò copie della documentazione ad altri... Due pagine dei fascist file in cui si menzionano l&#x27;UFO e il G...</span></span></span>

The public descriptions of this examination raise several problems.

First, the underlying methodology has not been presented with enough detail for independent replication. Critical reporting notes that the techniques, sampling process and limits of the examination have not been adequately explained. It is therefore difficult to know precisely what was tested, which pages were included, whether the ink itself was dated, and what range of dates the result actually allowed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vice.com/it/article/x-files-fascisti-primo-avvistamento-ufo-mussolini/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vice.com">[VICE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vice.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">x files fascisti primo avvistamento ufo mussolini</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gli &#x27;X-Files fascisti&#x27; e la strana teoria del primo...2 Sept 2021 — I fantomatici documenti riservati del “Gabinetto RS/33” sono sta...</span></span></span>

Second, dating a material is not the same as dating a document’s creation. A forger can obtain old blank paper from unused stationery, the back of an existing document or a period notebook. Historically compatible ink can also be found, reproduced or prepared. A finding that paper was manufactured in the 1930s would show only that the sheet was not newer than claimed; it would not demonstrate that the RS/33 text was placed on it in 1933.

Third, authentication requires comparison. Examiners would normally assess typewriter characteristics, typography, ink composition, handwriting, paper fibres, watermarks, official letterheads, administrative language and formatting against securely dated examples from the same organisation. No widely available independent study has shown that the RS/33 pages passed this broader set of tests.

The reported material examination is therefore not worthless, but it has been given more significance than it can bear. At most, it may suggest that some physical materials are compatible with the claimed period. It does not establish the identity of the author, the truth of the contents or the existence of the alleged committee.

## Language and historical compatibility

A convincing forgery usually mixes accurate period detail with mistakes, ambiguities or concepts drawn from later culture. The RS/33 material contains enough authentic-looking names and institutions to create an impression of historical depth. Marconi was a major public figure with close connections to the Fascist state. Italy possessed advanced aeronautical research establishments, heavily controlled newspapers and security organisations capable of suppressing sensitive military information.

Those facts make the scenario imaginable, but they do not prove that the named committee existed. A modern author with access to histories, directories and biographies could assemble a plausible group of scientists. Indeed, selecting famous researchers can make a fabricated document more persuasive to a general reader while making it more vulnerable to specialist checking.

The alleged membership lists have produced chronological questions. Luigi Broglio, later an important Italian aerospace engineer, has appeared in some versions of the RS/33 membership story even though he was born in 1911 and was only in his early twenties in 1933. That does not make his participation impossible in every capacity, but it makes his presentation as an established member of an elite scientific cabinet difficult to accept without contemporary evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incidente_di_Magenta" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Incidente di Magenta</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Incidente di Magenta</span></span></span>

The meaning of “RS” is uncertain as well. It is commonly expanded as “Special Research”, while “33” is interpreted as the year of creation. Yet this explanation comes from the story’s advocates rather than from an independently located founding decree or registry. The title may sound bureaucratically plausible, but plausibility is not authentication.

The narrative structure also resembles later UFO mythology. A crashed disc, a secret recovery team, prominent scientists, controlled press reporting and eventual transfer to the United States closely parallel post-war crash-retrieval stories, particularly the disputed Majestic 12 documents. Critics have therefore suggested that RS/33 is less a surviving Fascist file than a retrospective Italian version of an established UFO legend.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vice.com/it/article/x-files-fascisti-primo-avvistamento-ufo-mussolini/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vice.com">[VICE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vice.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">x files fascisti primo avvistamento ufo mussolini</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gli &#x27;X-Files fascisti&#x27; e la strana teoria del primo...2 Sept 2021 — I fantomatici documenti riservati del “Gabinetto RS/33” sono sta...</span></span></span>

## Genuine records do not confirm RS/33

The Fascist state closely monitored Italian airspace, foreign aircraft, aviation accidents and politically sensitive news. This means that authentic files concerning unusual aerial activity do survive. Their existence is important because it provides a control sample against which the RS/33 papers can be judged.

Research cited in critical reporting identifies genuine telegrams and aviation-related papers in the Milan State Archive’s prefectural collections. The relevant files concern subjects such as airports, flights, accidents and suspicious foreign aircraft. They demonstrate that the authorities recorded and circulated reports about potentially threatening activity in the sky. They do not, however, confirm a crashed disc, an RS/33 committee or a recovery at Magenta or Vergiate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vice.com/it/article/x-files-fascisti-primo-avvistamento-ufo-mussolini/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vice.com">[VICE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vice.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">x files fascisti primo avvistamento ufo mussolini</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gli &#x27;X-Files fascisti&#x27; e la strana teoria del primo...2 Sept 2021 — I fantomatici documenti riservati del “Gabinetto RS/33” sono sta...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters. Ordinary records about unidentified aircraft can sound suggestive when removed from context. In the 1930s, an “unidentified” aeroplane might be a foreign military aircraft, a smuggling flight, an unregistered civil machine or an observation that could not be resolved from the available information. It does not follow that the authorities meant an object of non-human origin.

No verified record from the Regia Aeronautica, the prefecture, the police, the Stefani news agency or SIAI-Marchetti has yet bridged the gap between routine air-security concerns and the extraordinary RS/33 interpretation.

## The case made by supporters

Supporters argue that several features of the collection deserve more than immediate dismissal. The papers use names, offices and scientific figures appropriate to Fascist Italy. Some of the stationery and writing materials have reportedly been judged compatible with the period. The regime was capable of strict censorship, and secret military projects do not always leave complete public records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.scribd.com/document/280073372/2000-LISSONI-PINOTTI-Gli-X-filews-Del-Fascismo-Ricerche-Aerospaziali-Dal-1933" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: it.scribd.com">[scribd.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">it.scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2000 LISSONI PINOTTI Gli X filews Del Fascismo Ricerche Aerospaziali Dal 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">2000 LISSONI PINOTTI Gli X-Filews Del Fascismo....files di Mussolini, Gabinetto RS/33: dagli ufo arriv il raggio della morte... T...</span></span></span>

They also argue that the anonymous sender may have inherited private copies that were never entered into a public archive. In this interpretation, the absence of formal registration marks is explained by the papers being drafts, unofficial duplicates or documents deliberately retained by an insider.

These points keep absolute certainty out of reach. A private collection can contain genuine historical papers, and an incomplete archive can conceal important events. It would be too strong to say that every page has been conclusively proved fraudulent by a definitive laboratory test.

Nevertheless, the supporter’s case relies heavily on possibilities: the originals may have been removed, the committee may have operated outside normal channels, other records may have been destroyed, and the source may have had legitimate reasons to remain anonymous. Each possibility is individually conceivable. Together, they insulate the claim from normal historical testing rather than positively demonstrating it.

## Why the forgery explanation is stronger

The forgery hypothesis accounts for the evidence with fewer assumptions. It explains why the documents appeared only in the 1990s, why the source remained unidentifiable, why no original archive was supplied, why administrative controls are weak or absent, and why the collection so neatly anticipates the needs of a modern UFO crash narrative.

A successful historical forgery need not be technically crude. Old paper, period typewriters, genuine letterheads and correctly chosen public figures can all be obtained. The most revealing weaknesses may instead be archival: no chain of custody, no matching register, no related files and no independent discovery by researchers working in established collections.

The RS/33 papers also lack strong external corroboration. No verified contemporary newspaper report describes the alleged crash. No identified witness testimony recorded in 1933 has been tied securely to the documents. No aircraft wreckage, technical sample, photograph or factory inventory has been authenticated as part of the supposed recovery. The papers are therefore being asked to authenticate both themselves and the event they describe.

That is an evidential circle. The crash is said to be real because the RS/33 papers mention it, while the papers are treated as credible because they appear to concern a real secret crash. Without independent support, neither side of the argument strengthens the other.

## What would change the assessment

The authenticity debate could be moved forward by evidence that is independently testable rather than narratively supportive. The most important development would be the identification of the source and the recovery of a documented chain of possession.

Other significant evidence would include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* original envelopes, postal records and complete correspondence from the anonymous sender;
* high-resolution access to every document, including reverse sides, watermarks and physical measurements;
* independent testing by recognised paper, ink, typewriter and handwriting specialists;
* publication of the full forensic report, methods, samples and uncertainty ranges;
* matching protocol entries or copies in a state, military, university, company or private archive;
* securely dated correspondence from Marconi or another alleged member referring to RS/33;
* SIAI-Marchetti security, storage or transport records consistent with an unusual recovery;
* a contemporary diary or witness account that can be shown to pre-date the 1990s story.

</div>

A single later recollection or another anonymously supplied page would add little unless it could be connected to an established archive. Repetition is not corroboration when every version ultimately traces back to the same disputed collection.

## The most defensible verdict

The RS/33 papers have historical interest because they transformed a poorly evidenced Lombard crash story into an elaborate claim about Fascist science, censorship and secret aviation research. They also show how the appearance of official paperwork can give a modern legend an archival authority it did not previously possess.

Their evidential value is much lower. No secure provenance has been demonstrated, no supporting government file has been located, and the documents lack enough verifiable bureaucratic detail to connect them confidently to the Fascist administration. Reported testing of paper and ink does not resolve these problems and has not been published in a form that allows robust independent evaluation.

The fairest classification is therefore not “proved fake” in the narrow forensic sense, but “probably fabricated and historically unverified”. Until records with a demonstrable archival origin emerge, the RS/33 collection weakens rather than establishes the case for a 1933 recovery in Lombardy.
