## Introduction

The 1978–79 UFO wave brought [Friuli-Venezia Giulia]({{ 'friuli-venezia-giulia/' | relative_url }}) into the largest recorded surge of unidentified-object reports in modern Italian history. Three regional cases survive in the Italian Air Force’s public archive: a fast, luminous object reported at [Feletto Umberto]({{ 'feletto/' | relative_url }}) in September 1978, red spherical lights at Fiume Veneto in January 1979, and a red circular object reported through the Carabinieri at Pulfero four days later. All three were catalogued as unidentified after review of the information held in the archive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Friuli-Venezia_Giuli_608dd8_friuli_1978_79_wave_4f68ed-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for 1978 79 Wave" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Their importance is historical rather than evidentially spectacular. The reports show that Friuli participated in a nationwide phenomenon which changed how Italy received and checked UFO claims. They do not provide photographs, radar tracks, physical traces or detailed interview records. Later archival publication therefore strengthened the case that the sightings were genuinely reported and officially retained, but did not establish what the witnesses saw.

## The national flap and Friuli’s place within it

Italy experienced an exceptional concentration of UFO reports during 1978. Sightings that had normally numbered in the tens per month rose into the hundreds, accompanied by intense newspaper, magazine and television attention. Research based on the archives of the Italian Centre for UFO Studies, or CISU, has described 1978 as both a sighting wave and an unprecedented media wave: its press archive contains more than 2,000 cuttings from that year alone, held in seven boxes plus a separate dossier of illustrated-magazine coverage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iltascabile.com/scienze/dischi-volanti-italia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iltascabile.com">[Il Tascabile]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iltascabile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Tascabile Dischi volanti sull’Italia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il TascabileDischi volanti sull’Italia - Il Tascabile...</span></span></span>

One later CISU-based reconstruction counted 2,345 Italian reports for 1978 when cases with uncertain dates were included. That figure should not be confused with the much smaller number preserved in the Air Force’s summary tables. Civilian researchers attempted to collect newspaper stories, local enquiries and reports sent to private groups, whereas the military archive represents only those cases that entered a particular official reporting channel and survived in its published records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iltascabile.com/scienze/dischi-volanti-italia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iltascabile.com">[Il Tascabile]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iltascabile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Tascabile Dischi volanti sull’Italia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il TascabileDischi volanti sull’Italia - Il Tascabile...</span></span></span>

The regional cases were therefore part of a much larger reporting environment. Feletto Umberto was not an isolated local curiosity: its report fell on 14 September 1978, a date on which the Air Force archive also lists observations at Agro Samassi in Sardinia, Cortona in Tuscany, Cisano sul Neva in Liguria, Santadi in Sardinia and Siena. The descriptions differ considerably in time, direction, colour and apparent form, so the entries do not by themselves prove that all witnesses saw one shared object. They do, however, illustrate how widely distributed the reporting had become.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

The wave also produced an institutional consequence. The Italian Air Force states that, following the 1978 surge, Prime Minister Giulio Andreotti designated it as the official body responsible for collecting, verifying and monitoring reports of unidentified flying objects. The service’s role was not to confirm extraterrestrial visitors, but to compare reported events with known aviation, military, meteorological and other activity where possible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/ufficio-relazioni-con-il-pubblico/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare Ufficio relazioni con il pubblico</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Devo segnalare l&#x27;avvistamento di un oggetto volante non identificatoA seguito dell’ondata di avvistamenti di Oggetti Volanti Non Identifi...</span></span></span>

For Friuli-Venezia Giulia, this makes the surviving reports significant even though they are sparse. They are among the region’s earliest publicly accessible official UFO records and mark the point at which local observations became part of a national system rather than remaining only in conversation, local folklore or newspaper files.

## Feletto Umberto: the clearest early Friuli entry

The first of the three cases occurred at Feletto Umberto, in the municipality of Tavagnacco near Udine, at about 10.30 pm on 14 September 1978. Private citizens reported a circular object with a trail, coloured yellow and red. It was described as moving at high speed from south to north, apparently at low altitude, beneath a clear sky. The Air Force summary concludes that, after examination of the data in its archive, the event was catalogued as an unidentified flying object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

That entry gives the sighting a firm documentary core: there is a named place, date, approximate time, direction, colour, shape, weather condition and category of witness. It is therefore stronger than a recollection published decades later without contemporary documentation.

Yet the apparent precision can be misleading. Terms such as “low altitude” and “high speed” appear to be witness estimates rather than instrument measurements. Against a clear night sky, without a known distance or fixed reference point, a small nearby object and a large distant one can appear similar. Apparent speed is equally difficult to calculate when neither range nor duration is recorded.

The trail is an important detail because it broadens the range of conventional possibilities. Meteors, re-entering space debris, aircraft lighting viewed under unusual conditions and pyrotechnic or balloon-borne objects can all produce combinations of coloured light and apparent trailing. None can be assigned confidently from the summary alone, but neither does the document show that such explanations were systematically eliminated.

The date creates another complication. Several Italian reports were logged on 14 September, but they were not obviously uniform. The Feletto object was reported late in the evening travelling south to north; other entries include early-morning observations, different directions and objects described as triangular, silver or equipped with light beams. The national clustering may indicate an unusual sky stimulus, but it may also reflect intensified attention and a greater readiness to report ambiguous lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

No surviving public entry identifies radar confirmation, aircraft interception, photographs, independent observing sites or a detailed follow-up interview. Feletto Umberto is consequently best classified as a documented but information-poor unresolved sighting: genuine as a report, uncertain as a physical event.

## Fiume Veneto and Pulfero: the January 1979 tail

The two later Friuli reports arrived as the national wave was rapidly subsiding. Contemporary reconstructions describe the number of Italian cases as falling sharply after the New Year, although public discussion continued during 1979.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iltascabile.com/scienze/dischi-volanti-italia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iltascabile.com">[Il Tascabile]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iltascabile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Tascabile Dischi volanti sull’Italia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il TascabileDischi volanti sull’Italia - Il Tascabile...</span></span></span>

At Fiume Veneto, in the province of Pordenone, private citizens reported luminous red spherical bodies at about 5 am on 10 January 1979. Their movement was described as variable and their apparent altitude as very low. The sky was recorded as clear. As with Feletto Umberto, the Air Force summary says the event was catalogued as unidentified following review of the archived data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

The plural “bodies” makes the account memorable, but the entry omits the number of lights, their formation, duration, angular size and whether they moved together. Those missing facts are crucial. Several red lights travelling together could suggest aircraft, flares, balloons, distant vehicles or separate celestial and terrestrial sources; an object repeatedly changing direction would raise different questions. The table’s single word, “variable”, is too compressed to distinguish between these possibilities.

Fiume Veneto’s proximity to aviation and military activity in the wider Pordenone area can naturally encourage speculation, particularly because [Aviano]({{ 'aviano/' | relative_url }}) Air Base has strongly influenced later regional UFO narratives. The 1979 archive entry, however, records no connection with Aviano, no military aircraft encounter and no radar observation. Treating the sighting as an air-base case would therefore go beyond the evidence.

The Pulfero report followed on 14 January at about 7.20 am. It concerned a red circular object said to move at variable speed from north towards west at an estimated altitude of roughly 3,000 metres under a clear sky. Unlike the other two Friuli entries, the report came through personnel of the Carabinieri. It too was retained as an unidentified event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

A report routed through the Carabinieri has a stronger administrative chain than an unattributed story, but it does not automatically make the visual interpretation more reliable. The summary does not specify whether Carabinieri personnel witnessed the object themselves or transmitted information gathered from someone else. Nor does it preserve the observing duration, angular elevation, sound, apparent size or any comparison with known aircraft.

Pulfero lies in the Natisone valleys near the border with what was then Yugoslavia. In the Cold War setting of 1979, an unfamiliar aerial observation in such an area had legitimate security relevance even if its ultimate cause was ordinary. That helps explain why military authorities would record and compare reports without implying an exotic conclusion.

## What “catalogued as unidentified” actually means

The repeated wording in the Air Force archive is easy to overread. In all three cases, the conclusion is essentially that, on the basis of the data held in the archive, the event was catalogued as an unidentified flying object. This is a filing outcome, not a declaration that investigators proved the existence of an extraordinary craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

An unresolved report can remain unresolved for two very different reasons. It may contain strong evidence that resists careful explanation, or it may contain too little information to test any explanation properly. The Friuli entries fit the second category more closely. They preserve basic descriptive fields but not the underlying statements, calculations or investigative exchanges that would allow an independent reassessment.

The absence of an identified explanation therefore has limited weight. A meteor hypothesis, for example, requires a sufficiently accurate time, path, duration and comparison with astronomical records. An aircraft explanation requires flight information and a reliable estimate of direction and elevation. A balloon explanation requires wind data, launch records and observation duration. The published tables do not show whether these checks were performed in detail or whether the surviving material was simply inadequate.

The distinction matters because the word “official” is often treated as though it were synonymous with “confirmed”. Here it means that the report entered a state archive and received an administrative classification. That makes the record historically valuable, but it does not upgrade the witness description into sensor evidence.

## Why heightened attention complicates the evidence

A sighting wave is not merely a large pile of independent observations. Reports can influence later reports. Newspaper headlines teach readers which shapes and behaviours to notice; radio and television programmes make unusual lights socially discussable; organised UFO groups provide reporting channels; and repeated stories encourage people to watch the sky more closely.

Italy in 1978 contained all these ingredients. UFO material circulated heavily in the press, local enthusiast groups had expanded, and Steven Spielberg’s *Close Encounters of the Third Kind* had popularised both the subject and the language of the “close encounter”. CISU’s later historical work treats the year not simply as a possible physical mystery but also as a mass-media and social phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iltascabile.com/scienze/dischi-volanti-italia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iltascabile.com">[iltascabile.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iltascabile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Tascabile Dischi volanti sull’Italia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il TascabileDischi volanti sull’Italia - Il Tascabile...</span></span></span>

A particularly revealing episode occurred at Jesolo in August 1978, when hot-air balloons made by psychiatric patients were launched after invented letters had primed newspapers and the public to expect unusual aerial objects. Some observers interpreted the balloons as UFOs, demonstrating how prior expectation could shape what an ambiguous stimulus became in retelling. This does not explain the Friuli cases directly, but it shows why the surrounding reporting climate cannot be ignored.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iltascabile.com/scienze/dischi-volanti-italia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iltascabile.com">[Il Tascabile]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iltascabile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Tascabile Dischi volanti sull’Italia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il TascabileDischi volanti sull’Italia - Il Tascabile...</span></span></span>

Heightened attention cuts both ways. It may increase false identifications, but it may also bring genuine anomalies to official notice that would otherwise never be recorded. The correct response is therefore neither to dismiss every report as social contagion nor to treat the volume of reports as proof of an unknown technology. Each case must stand on the quality of its own evidence.

For Feletto Umberto, Fiume Veneto and Pulfero, the records are too compressed for that individual testing. Their strongest collective value is that they document how the national wave reached different parts of Friuli: the Udine plain, the Pordenone area and the eastern valleys.

## Did later reporting strengthen the original claims?

Later publication strengthened the provenance of the reports. Readers no longer have to rely solely on UFO books or remembered newspaper stories: the Italian Air Force has made a substantial historical archive publicly accessible and continues to explain the institutional system created after the 1978 wave.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/category/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare OVNI Archives</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare OVNI Archives</span></span></span>

It did not, however, strengthen the cases in the more important evidential sense. No publicly available later material located for these three events adds radar data, photographs, named witness interviews, physical evidence or a convincing reconstruction of the observing geometry. Nor has a later investigation produced a well-supported common explanation linking all three.

The passage of time has instead made their limitations more serious. Aircraft logs, weather observations, astronomical comparisons and witness memories become harder to recover or verify after several decades. Even when the original witnesses were sincere and careful, a table containing only a few descriptive words cannot preserve everything needed to distinguish an unusual aircraft, meteor, balloon, planet or perceptual error.

The most defensible assessment is therefore graduated:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Feletto Umberto** is a clearly documented official report with a reasonably detailed visual description, but no corroborating instrumentation.
* **Fiume Veneto** is intriguing because several red spherical bodies were apparently involved, yet the summary lacks the number, spacing, duration and precise motion needed for analysis.
* **Pulfero** has an official law-enforcement reporting route and a stated direction and altitude, but it remains unclear who directly observed the object and how the estimates were derived.

</div>

None is demonstrably debunked. None provides strong evidence for an extraordinary craft. All three remain unresolved chiefly because the surviving information is too thin.

## The wave’s lasting place in Friuli’s UFO history

The 1978–79 episode matters because it established the documentary starting point for much of Friuli-Venezia Giulia’s later UFO history. The region’s later reports around Pordenone, [Cordenons]({{ 'cordenons/' | relative_url }}) and other locations would enter an institutional framework created in response to this national surge. The early cases therefore connect local witness testimony with the development of Italy’s official collection system.

They also offer a useful standard for reading subsequent claims. An entry can be authentic, contemporary and officially archived while still being weak evidence for what physically occurred. Conversely, an ordinary explanation should not be declared merely because it appears plausible; it must fit the reported time, direction, duration and conditions.

Seen in that light, the Great Wave reached Friuli not as one decisive encounter but as three slender records embedded in a far larger national moment. Their historical status is secure. Their causes are not. The surviving evidence supports a conclusion of documented uncertainty rather than confirmation, exposure or simple dismissal.
