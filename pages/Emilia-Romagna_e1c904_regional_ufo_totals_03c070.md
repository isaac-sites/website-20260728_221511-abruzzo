## Introduction

[Emilia-Romagna]({{ 'why-emilia-romagna-became-a-ufo-hotspot/' | relative_url }}) was a regular source of Italian UFO reports, but the official figures do not support the idea that it was consistently the country’s leading hotspot. Italian Air Force tables record 17 regional cases between 1972 and 2000, about 5.3% of the national total. That placed Emilia-Romagna jointly eighth among Italy’s regions, well behind Lazio, Tuscany and Puglia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/INT.Dperiodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Emilia-Romagna_e1c904_regional_ufo_totals_03c070-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Regional Totals" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The picture changes when later years or civilian archives are included. By the end of 2013, an Air Force-derived national ranking credited Emilia-Romagna with 32 of Italy’s 445 reports, moving it to sixth place. The much larger archive of the Italian Centre for UFO Studies, or CISU, meanwhile places the region among only three with more than 1,000 historical cases. These totals are not contradictory measurements of the same thing: they cover different periods, accept reports through different channels and apply different cataloguing rules.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rainews.it/archivio-rainews/articoli/ufo-avvistamenti-segnalazioni-italia-aeronautica-218a7f1a-d128-4494-b464-066f409c5400.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rainews.it">[RaiNews]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rainews.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dal 1972, 445 avvistamenti di Ufo in Italia29 Mar 2014 — Seguono: Toscana, con 43 segnalazioni classificate dall&#x27;Aeronautica, Lomb...</span></span></span>

## Regional totals from 1972 to 2000

The Air Force divides its older statistics into two periods. For 1972–1990 it lists 208 sightings nationally, of which 11 were assigned to Emilia-Romagna. For 1991–2000 it records another 112 nationwide and six in the region. Combined, the tables produce the following result:

PeriodEmilia-RomagnaItalyRegional share1972–1990112085.3%1991–200061125.4%**1972–2000****17****320****5.3%**

The near-identical percentages are revealing. Emilia-Romagna’s contribution remained remarkably stable even though reporting across Italy fluctuated sharply from year to year. It accounted for 5.3% of cases in the first period and 5.4% in the second. This looks less like a continuous regional flap than a persistent background level of reporting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/INT.Dperiodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span></span></span>

The yearly table reinforces that interpretation. Emilia-Romagna produced reports in scattered years rather than through an uninterrupted run: one each in 1972 and 1973, two in the national peak year of 1978, and smaller entries during the 1980s. From 1991 to 2000, its six cases were concentrated in 1995, 1997, 1998 and 2000. There were several years with no listed regional event at all.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/INT.Dperiodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span></span></span>

Italy as a whole followed a much more dramatic cycle. The Air Force table records 69 cases in 1978 and 32 in 1980, compared with none in 1976 and only one in 1975. Emilia-Romagna participated in the 1978 wave, but with two reports it was not its principal centre. Sicily registered 13 that year, Abruzzo eight, Puglia seven, and several other regions four or five. The famous national flap was therefore geographically broad, with especially strong concentrations elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/INT.Dperiodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span></span></span>

These figures should not be read as a count of alien craft, nor even as a complete count of everything residents saw. The Air Force explains that its role is to collect and check reports for possible connections with human activity or natural phenomena, principally in support of flight and national security. An event is classified as unidentified when investigators cannot establish a technical or natural explanation from the information available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">2022... 2020... ## How Emilia-Romagna compares with other regions

Across the full 1972–2000 tables, Emilia-Romagna’s 17 cases put it jointly eighth with Sardinia. The national ranking was:

RankRegionReports1Lazio392Tuscany373Puglia304Sicily235Lombardy226Marche207Campania18=8**Emilia-Romagna****17**=8Sardinia17

Emilia-Romagna therefore sat in the upper half of the national table, but not among its dominant regions. Lazio recorded more than twice as many cases, while Tuscany had 20 more. Even the neighbouring Marche, much smaller in population and area, registered three more in the same dataset.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/INT.Dperiodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span></span></span>

The region’s position was not fixed. During 1972–1990, its 11 cases placed it behind Lazio, Tuscany, Sicily, Puglia, Marche, Sardinia, Campania and Lombardy, while level with Liguria. During 1991–2000 it recorded six cases, equal to Abruzzo and ahead of several regions, but far below Tuscany’s 16 and the 13 each attributed to Lazio and Puglia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/INT.Dperiodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span></span></span>

A later national summary shows Emilia-Romagna climbing. Figures presented in 2014, covering Air Force records from 1972 through 2013, listed 445 Italian sightings. Lazio remained first with 53, followed by Tuscany with 43, Lombardy and Campania with 36, Puglia with 34 and Emilia-Romagna with 32. Sicily followed closely with 31. Emilia-Romagna then represented about 7.2% of the total and ranked sixth rather than joint eighth.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rainews.it/archivio-rainews/articoli/ufo-avvistamenti-segnalazioni-italia-aeronautica-218a7f1a-d128-4494-b464-066f409c5400.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rainews.it">[RaiNews]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rainews.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dal 1972, 445 avvistamenti di Ufo in Italia29 Mar 2014 — Seguono: Toscana, con 43 segnalazioni classificate dall&#x27;Aeronautica, Lomb...</span></span></span>

Comparing the two sets implies that 15 Emilia-Romagna cases were added from 2001 to 2013, against 125 additional cases nationally. The region therefore supplied roughly 12% of the newer entries, more than double its share during 1972–2000. This does not by itself demonstrate increased anomalous activity: it could reflect a genuine reporting cluster, better public awareness of official channels, uneven investigation, or changes in how cases were received and retained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/INT.Dperiodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span></span></span>

The official totals also contain no adjustment for population, land area, air traffic, visibility or time spent outdoors. A populous, highly connected region with Bologna’s metropolitan area, an Adriatic tourist coast and busy civil and military airspace offers many opportunities for people to notice aircraft, satellites, balloons, meteors and other unfamiliar lights. Raw counts measure reports entering a system, not the underlying frequency of unexplained objects.

## Why civilian catalogues give a different ranking

The strongest apparent contradiction comes from CISU’s national catalogue. Its historical summary says that Piemonte, Tuscany and Emilia-Romagna each have more than 1,000 recorded cases, placing Emilia-Romagna among the three richest regional archives. That sounds incompatible with an Air Force total of only 17 cases through 2000 or 32 through 2013, but CISU is counting a much broader body of material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/ufo-in-italia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Ufo in Italia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">C.I.S.U.. CISU’s Regional Catalogues Project began in 1981 and builds local archives from witness reports, press stories, historical research and investigations by a network of regional collaborators. Its national collection extends back before the Air Force series, embracing the major waves of 1954, 1973 and 1978 as well as less dramatic years. The Air Force tables begin in 1972 and represent reports that reached a specific official process. A sighting found retrospectively in a 1954 Bologna newspaper can therefore enter a civilian historical catalogue but cannot appear in a 1972-onwards military table.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/ufo-in-italia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Ufo in Italia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">C.I.S.U.. The civilian archive also warns that regional differences partly reflect the presence or absence of active investigators. A well-organised local group can uncover old press reports, encourage witnesses to come forward and document cases that would otherwise remain unknown. High catalogue totals may therefore reveal strong archival coverage as much as an unusually active sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/ufo-in-italia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Ufo in Italia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">C.I.S.U.. Different datasets may also count related observations differently. A bright meteor visible from several provinces might be entered as several local reports, one multi-witness incident or a single explained event, depending on the catalogue’s purpose. Duplicates, uncertain dates and reports later linked to known phenomena may be retained for historical study in one archive but omitted from a narrower list of officially unresolved incidents.

Modern civilian totals illustrate the same problem. A published summary of reports received in 2022 placed Emilia-Romagna third nationally with 18, behind Lombardy’s 25 and Lazio’s 19. It also identified six reports from Piacenza and several from Modena. Yet contemporary reporting systems receive many observations later associated with Starlink satellites, aircraft, the International Space Station, lanterns, drones and powerful ground lights. A high preliminary total is therefore not equivalent to 18 strong unexplained cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://initalia.virgilio.it/ufo-italia-nuovi-avvistamenti-misteriosi-tutti-luoghi-69524" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: initalia.virgilio.it">[Virgilio InItalia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">initalia.virgilio.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">In Italia Ufo in Italia, 319 nuovi avvistamenti misteriosi: tutti i luoghi</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">In Italia Ufo in Italia, 319 nuovi avvistamenti misteriosi: tutti i luoghi</span></span></span>

## Was it really a national hotspot?

The fairest answer depends on what “hotspot” means. Emilia-Romagna was clearly not marginal: it appeared repeatedly in Air Force records, maintained a stable share of national reports from 1972 to 2000, rose to sixth in the longer official ranking, and holds one of Italy’s largest civilian historical catalogues.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/INT.Dperiodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[difesa.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span></span></span>

It was not, however, the unchallenged centre of Italian UFO activity. In the official 1972–2000 data it supplied just over one report in twenty and ranked behind seven regions. Its pattern was persistent but intermittent, without a decades-long sequence suggesting a continuous local flap. The national peaks of 1978 and 1980 were much larger than anything attributable to Emilia-Romagna alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/INT.Dperiodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare Presentazione standard di Power Point</span></span></span>

The most defensible description is that Emilia-Romagna was **one of Italy’s recurring reporting regions rather than its foremost hotspot**. Its importance grows when the [1954 wave]({{ '1954-wave-227217/' | relative_url }}), newspaper archives and later civilian submissions are included. It diminishes when attention is restricted to the narrower Air Force record. Neither result establishes that unusual craft visited the region more often than elsewhere; both show how strongly a UFO map depends on who collected the reports, during which years and under what rules.
