## Introduction

The Carabinieri sightings at San Tifeo and Corigliano Calabro are among the clearest examples of official witnesses entering [Calabria]({{ 'why-calabria-has-many-ufo-reports-but-no/' | relative_url }})’s UFO record without producing decisive physical evidence. In December 1978, officers reported two very different aerial phenomena: a low red circular object travelling across the night sky at San Tifeo, and a slow white phenomenon of changing form seen in clear morning conditions at Corigliano. Both reports were retained in the Italian Air Force archive as unidentified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Calabria_663de5_calabria_police_ufo_38cc8e-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Police Reports" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Their significance lies less in what they prove than in what they preserve. These were not anonymous anecdotes collected decades later. They passed through a recognised police reporting chain and entered an official national file. Yet the surviving summaries contain no photographs, radar returns, instrument readings, measured distances or detailed witness statements. The cases therefore deserve more weight than an unsupported rumour, but not the certainty sometimes attached to sightings made by uniformed personnel.

## The San Tifeo and Corigliano observations

The first report is dated 12 December 1978 and numbered 35 in the Italian Air Force’s historical archive. At about 3 a.m., Carabinieri personnel at San Tifeo, in the province of Cosenza, observed something described as circular and red. It reportedly moved from north to south and appeared to be at low altitude. The archive gives no known speed and no weather information. Its concluding entry says that, after examination of the information held on file, the event was catalogued as an unidentified flying object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

That brief description immediately raises questions which the published record cannot answer. “Circular” may describe a structured disc, but it may equally describe the apparent outline of a distant light. “Low” is an estimate rather than a measured height. No angular size, duration, sound, brightness comparison, horizon position or number of individual witnesses appears in the summary. It is also unclear whether the reported north-to-south movement was tracked against fixed landmarks or simply judged by eye.

The Corigliano Calabro sighting followed two days later, on 14 December, at approximately 7.15 a.m. Carabinieri personnel reported a white phenomenon whose form was described as variable. Its speed was considered low, its altitude variable, and its direction of travel was not specified. The sky was recorded as clear. This event, numbered 43, was also retained as unidentified after review of the archived data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

Corigliano is in some respects the more intriguing report because it occurred in daylight or early morning light under clear skies. Those conditions can allow an observer to see shape and movement more clearly than during a dark-sky encounter. At the same time, the description “variable” introduces substantial uncertainty. It might refer to an object changing orientation, a bright source distorted by atmospheric effects, an irregular cloud-like phenomenon or a point of light whose appearance altered as the observer’s eyes and viewing angle changed. The surviving summary does not explain what the officers meant.

The two observations should not automatically be treated as sightings of the same object. They occurred roughly 48 hours apart and differ in colour, apparent form, lighting conditions and reported motion. San Tifeo involved a red circular phenomenon moving along a stated course during the night; Corigliano involved a white, shape-changing phenomenon with no recorded direction in the morning. Their strongest connection is administrative and historical: both were Calabrian reports from Carabinieri during the intense Italian reporting period of December 1978.

## What official witness status adds

A report from serving Carabinieri officers has advantages over an anonymous newspaper claim. The witnesses were identifiable within an institution, were accustomed to making formal observations and had professional reasons not to invent an incident that might expose them to ridicule or disciplinary attention. The report also travelled far enough through official channels to be included in the Air Force’s national archive.

The institutional setting matters because the Italian Air Force was designated as the body responsible for collecting, checking and monitoring reports of unidentified aerial phenomena. Its stated purpose is connected to flight and national security rather than proving or disproving extraterrestrial visitation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Andreotti designò l&#x27;Aeronautica Militare quale Organismo Istituzionale deputato a raccogliere, verificare e monitorizzare le segnalazioni...</span></span></span>

Official provenance therefore establishes that a report existed and was taken seriously enough to record. It does not establish that every descriptive detail was physically accurate. Police officers receive training in observation, but they are not automatically experts in astronomy, atmospheric optics, aircraft identification or estimating the range of objects seen against an empty sky. Witness honesty and observational precision are separate questions.

The wording of the published files also needs careful interpretation. The Air Force entries say that the cases were catalogued as unidentified following examination of the data in the archive. That means no satisfactory identification was reached from the material available; it does not mean investigators established that the objects displayed extraordinary technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

This distinction is especially important because the same historical archive contains many December 1978 reports from Carabinieri, police personnel and private citizens across Italy. Nearby entries describe circular, spherical, oval and conical phenomena in several colours and at widely differing estimated altitudes. The variety of descriptions suggests a wave of reports rather than a single consistently observed craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

Within Calabria’s UFO history, San Tifeo and Corigliano are consequently best regarded as a small official case family: independent police observations preserved during a national flap, rather than a connected two-stage encounter.

## Why shape, speed and altitude remain uncertain

The most important weakness in both cases is the absence of measured range. An observer who does not know how far away a light is cannot reliably calculate its true size, altitude or speed. A small nearby object and a much larger distant one can occupy the same apparent angle in the sky. Apparent motion is similarly dependent on distance: a slowly drifting object nearby may seem faster than a remote object covering a much greater physical distance.

Modern technical assessments of unexplained aerial reports repeatedly identify this problem. Perspective and parallax can distort estimates of an object’s dimensions, velocity and direction, while single-observer descriptions become far more useful when combined with sensor data or observations from a second location.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/Information%20Papers/AARO_Effect_of_Forced_Perspective_and_Parallax_View_on_UAP_Observations_2024.pdf?ver=WTwJtQaCvC3BkRlBJCGLzg%3D%3D" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Effect of Forced Perspective and Parallax View on UAP Observations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">forced perspective and parallax are better equipped to assess a UAP’s characteristics and provide...</span></span></span>

The San Tifeo entry illustrates the limitation particularly well. It records a “low” altitude but an unknown speed, with no indication of the basis for either judgement. Without a known distance, “low” may mean that the object appeared close to the horizon, seemed large or bright, passed in front of terrain, or simply gave the impression of being near. Only the last of these would provide no dependable range information.

Corigliano presents a related problem. Its altitude and shape are both listed as variable, but the archive does not say whether those changes were continuous, sudden or merely apparent. A genuine object changing height and orientation is one possibility. So are shifting atmospheric distortion, an aircraft banking at a distance, a balloon changing aspect, or a bright astronomical source viewed through unstable air. None can be tested confidently without the original narrative, duration, bearing and angular measurements.

Researchers examining historical UFO records have long faced the same evidential obstacle: cases remain unresolved when the reports lack accurate information about altitude, size, speed and viewing geometry. ## Ordinary explanations remain possible

No specific conventional explanation has been demonstrated for either sighting in the publicly available record. It would therefore be misleading to label San Tifeo or Corigliano as solved. It would be equally misleading to argue that conventional possibilities were eliminated.

For San Tifeo, potential categories include an aircraft light, a meteor or fireball seen at an unfavourable angle, a balloon carrying illumination, or an astronomical object whose apparent movement was misjudged. Some of these fit individual features but conflict with others. A meteor could appear red and travel rapidly across a section of sky, for example, but the archive gives no speed or duration. An aircraft could follow a north-to-south course, but the description contains no navigation lights, engine sound or blinking pattern.

The Corigliano description is broad enough to admit several alternatives. A distant aircraft turning in morning sunlight could change apparent shape and brightness. A balloon might drift slowly and alter its outline. Atmospheric effects can make bright sources shimmer, elongate or seem to move. Yet the record provides no wind information, astronomical comparison, flight check or detailed chronology capable of favouring one explanation.

The fact that the sky was recorded as clear at Corigliano improves visibility but does not by itself exclude misidentification. Clear air can make distant aircraft, balloons and bright celestial objects easier to see while leaving their distance difficult to judge. Conversely, the missing weather field at San Tifeo prevents even a basic assessment of cloud cover, haze or wind.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1972-1990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

## The most defensible assessment

The San Tifeo and Corigliano reports are credible as records of genuine observations: Carabinieri personnel saw something they could not identify and formally reported it. Their inclusion in the Air Force archive makes them stronger historical evidence than an unattributed tale or a late recollection. They also show that Calabria participated in the wider surge of Italian reports during December 1978.

They are not, however, strong evidence for a structured unknown aircraft. The published files provide only compressed descriptive fields. There is no surviving public indication of photographs, radar confirmation, physical effects, multiple observation points, measured bearings or a detailed elimination of aircraft, astronomical bodies and balloons. The reports were classified as unidentified because the archived information did not yield an answer, not because an exotic origin was established.

For Calabria’s regional history, that is precisely why the cases matter. They demonstrate both the value and the limits of official witness testimony. The identity and professional status of the observers raise confidence that an unusual appearance was sincerely reported. The lack of measurements prevents confidence about what produced it. San Tifeo and Corigliano therefore remain unresolved police sightings, but they are evidentially incomplete rather than demonstrably extraordinary.
