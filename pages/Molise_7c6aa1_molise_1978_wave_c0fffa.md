## Introduction

[Molise]({{'what-really-happened-in-molises-ufo/' | relative_url }})’s 1978 UFO wave was not a dramatic single incident, but a short run of catalogue entries clustered around Campobasso, Termoli and, just over the year boundary, Isernia. Its importance is regional rather than spectacular: it ties Molise to Italy’s extraordinary national UFO surge of 1978, while also showing how thin the local evidence can be when a “wave” is reconstructed mainly from catalogue rows rather than full witness files. The strongest record is the Centro Ufologico Nazionale catalogue, which lists several Molise entries in November and December 1978, including repeated Campobasso reports and a Termoli entry after dawn on 14 December. The Italian Air Force’s public archive, by contrast, became institutionally central because of the 1978 wave, but later [official reporting]({{'official-route/' | relative_url }}) gives Molise only a very small official footprint. That contrast is the key to reading the case: Molise was present inside the national flap, but not at the evidential centre of it. ## Why 1978 mattered for Molise

Italy’s 1978 UFO wave was one of the largest and most culturally visible waves in the country’s modern UFO history. A retrospective article in *Il Tascabile* describes it as an unprecedented mass phenomenon, with monthly reports rising from a few dozen to hundreds, heavy press and television attention, and a sudden end after New Year 1979. It also notes that December was the month of saturation, with hundreds of cases and the famous Zanfretta abduction claim gaining national attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.iltascabile.com/scienze/dischi-volanti-italia/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iltascabile.com">[Il Tascabile]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iltascabile.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Tascabile Dischi volanti sull’Italia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Tascabile Dischi volanti sull’Italia</span></span></span>

For Molise, that national background matters because the region’s own entries are not famous in isolation. Campobasso and Termoli become significant because their dates fall inside the densest part of the Italian wave, especially the November-December acceleration visible in national catalogues. The value of the Molise material is therefore comparative: it shows that even a small region with a modest later UFO record was caught up in the same reporting surge that affected Abruzzo, Marche, Puglia, Campania, Liguria and other regions.

The institutional after-effect was also national. The Italian Air Force states that, after the 1978 wave, Prime Minister Giulio Andreotti designated the Air Force as the official body responsible for collecting, verifying and monitoring unidentified-object reports; the current procedure involves submitting reports through the Carabinieri and checking possible human, technical or natural [explanations]({{'explanations-d7031a/' | relative_url }}) before a case is listed as unidentified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Molise_7c6aa1_molise_1978_wave_c0fffa-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="1978 Wave illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Molise_7c6aa1_molise_1978_wave_c0fffa-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Molise_7c6aa1_molise_1978_wave_c0fffa-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## The Campobasso cluster

Campobasso is the clearest Molise focus in the 1978 material. The CUN national catalogue lists a Campobasso entry on 18 November 1978 at 23:00, followed by another on 24 November with an unspecified time. These are not detailed narrative case files in the public table; they are index entries giving date, time where known, place, province, region, a classification code and catalogue number. That means they show that reports were logged, but they do not by themselves tell us what the witnesses saw, how many witnesses there were, whether photographs existed, or whether any official investigation followed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/CASISTICA%20UFOLOGICA%20COMPLETA%2014%201%2016%20B.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionale</span></span></span>

The December entries make Campobasso look less like an isolated dot and more like part of a brief local run. The same catalogue lists Campobasso for the period 15-18 December 1978, then a more precise Campobasso entry at 04:00 on 16 December, and another at night on 17 December. Those entries sit among a dense national sequence that includes Abruzzo, Marche, Puglia, Tuscany, Liguria, Sicily and other regions in the same days.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/CASISTICA%20UFOLOGICA%20COMPLETA%2014%201%2016%20B.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionale</span></span></span>

That clustering is important, but it should not be overstated. A catalogue cluster can mean a real concentration of observations, but it can also reflect press attention, people becoming more alert to lights in the sky, repeated logging of similar reports, or later compilation choices. The Campobasso entries are therefore best treated as a regional signal within a national reporting wave, not as a confirmed sequence of extraordinary events.

## Termoli and the Adriatic edge

Termoli’s 1978 entry places Molise’s coast into the same broad Adriatic atmosphere that made neighbouring Abruzzo one of the best-known theatres of the 1978 wave. The CUN catalogue lists Termoli, in the province of Campobasso, on 14 December 1978 “after dawn”. In another CUN listing, the same period appears in a compressed form as a road-route style entry for Termoli/Sulmona, which is a useful reminder that catalogue transmission can introduce small differences in place description and formatting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/CASISTICA%20UFOLOGICA%20COMPLETA%2014%201%2016%20B.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionale</span></span></span>

The Adriatic connection matters because the 1978 coastal stories did not stop at regional borders. Abruzzo press retrospectives describe a so-called “Adriatic Triangle” between Ancona, the Gran Sasso and Pescara, with fishermen and coastal witnesses reporting lights, disturbed water, compass problems and other alarming phenomena. Later retellings also stress exaggeration, rumour and media amplification, while some accounts point to more ordinary physical explanations for at least part of the coastal disturbance, including gas releases from the seabed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rete8.it/cronaca/378quando-gli-ufo-invasero-labruzzo-ottobre-78/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rete8.it">[Rete8]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rete8.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">378quando gli ufo invasero labruzzo ottobre 78</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">378quando gli ufo invasero labruzzo ottobre 78</span></span></span>

Termoli sits south of that better-known Abruzzo-Marche zone, so it should not be folded uncritically into every dramatic “Adriatic Triangle” claim. Its value is narrower: it shows that Molise’s coastline was not absent from the December 1978 reporting environment. The evidence is still a catalogue entry rather than a recovered witness narrative, so the case remains weakly documented.<img src="{{"/assets/images/Molise_7c6aa1_molise_1978_wave_c0fffa-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="1978 Wave illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Molise_7c6aa1_molise_1978_wave_c0fffa-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Molise_7c6aa1_molise_1978_wave_c0fffa-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Isernia and the wave’s New Year tail

Isernia is the awkward but useful boundary case. The CUN catalogue does not show a 1978 Isernia entry in the same way it shows Campobasso and Termoli. Instead, it lists Isernia in the very early morning of 1 January 1979, immediately after a series of New Year entries elsewhere in Italy. Because *Il Tascabile* describes the national wave as ending abruptly after New Year 1979, the Isernia row fits naturally as a tail-end report of the 1978 wave rather than as a separate regional episode.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/CASISTICA%20UFOLOGICA%20COMPLETA%2014%201%2016%20B.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionale</span></span></span>

That distinction matters for a reader trying to understand the Molise pattern. A loose phrase such as “the 1978 wave across Campobasso, Termoli and Isernia” can make it sound as though all three places produced same-year, equally documented reports. The better reading is more precise: Campobasso and Termoli appear in late-1978 catalogue entries, while Isernia appears just over the calendar boundary, in the wave’s New Year aftershock.

## Molise inside Italy’s national wave

The national numbers make Molise’s position clearer. CUN’s 1978 statistical summary says 1978 was the peak year in its dataset, with 1,030 total sightings and 1,028 assigned to provinces. Of those, 199 were counted as higher-significance cases under its Hynek-style coding filter, excluding doubtful entries. In that filtered table, both Campobasso and Isernia are listed as zero.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/Avvistamenti1978.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on centroufologiconazionale.net.</span></span></span>

That does not mean there were no Molise reports in the broader catalogue. The detailed CUN list plainly includes Campobasso and Termoli entries. It means those entries did not survive that particular filter for stronger or closer cases. This is one of the most important interpretive points: Molise’s 1978 wave is visible in the broad catalogue, but it drops out when the dataset is narrowed to more substantial cases. Official Air Force reporting gives an even more conservative picture. Rai News, summarising Air Force data released in 2014, reported 445 official sightings from 1972 onwards, with 1978 the peak year at 69 official cases; Molise was at the bottom of the regional ranking with only one official case across the period being discussed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rainews.it/archivio-rainews/articoli/ufo-avvistamenti-segnalazioni-italia-aeronautica-218a7f1a-d128-4494-b464-066f409c5400.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rainews.it">[RaiNews]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rainews.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on rainews.it.</span></span></span>

The difference between these figures is not necessarily a contradiction. Private ufological catalogues, press archives and official military/security reporting count different things. A local newspaper mention, a private witness report and a case accepted into an official Air Force list are not the same evidential unit. Molise’s 1978 material sits mostly on the private-catalogue side of that divide.<img src="{{"/assets/images/Molise_7c6aa1_molise_1978_wave_c0fffa-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="1978 Wave illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Molise_7c6aa1_molise_1978_wave_c0fffa-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Molise_7c6aa1_molise_1978_wave_c0fffa-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## What can and cannot be concluded

The best-supported conclusion is modest: Molise participated in the Italian 1978 UFO wave through several late-year catalogue entries, especially Campobasso in November and December and Termoli on 14 December, with Isernia appearing as a New Year 1979 tail-end report. Those entries matter because they connect the region to Italy’s most important modern UFO reporting surge and to the official reorganisation that followed the wave. Aeronautica Militare+4Centro Ufologico Nazionale+4Centro Ufologico Nazionale<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/avvistamenti/CASISTICA%20UFOLOGICA%20COMPLETA%2014%201%2016%20B.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[centroufologiconazionale.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionale</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionale</span></span></span>

The main doubt is evidential depth. The available public entries do not provide detailed witness statements, environmental checks, astronomical analysis, radar records, photographs, aircraft correlations or Air Force conclusions for these Molise reports. Without that underlying material, the cases cannot be treated as strong unresolved incidents. They are better understood as logged reports within a wider social, media and observational surge.
