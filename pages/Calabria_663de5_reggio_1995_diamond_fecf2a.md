## Introduction

The 1995 Reggio [Calabria]({{'what-really-happened-in-calabrias-ufo/' | relative_url }}) diamond-shaped light report is a small but useful case in Calabria’s UFO history. The Italian Air Force archive records that, at about 21:30 on 29 August 1995, a private citizen in Reggio Calabria reported a diamond-shaped light, described as white with red and green, moving downward in clear sky. The record gives no speed, no altitude, no duration and no supporting photograph, radar trace or second named witness. After examining the archive data, the event was catalogued as an unidentified flying object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1991-2000.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Calabria_663de5_reggio_1995_diamond_fecf2a-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for 1995 Reggio" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
That makes the case interesting for the opposite reason from a spectacular close encounter. It shows how an unresolved official entry can still be weak as evidence. The colours resemble ordinary aircraft lighting, the downward movement could fit an approach or descent, and Reggio Calabria has an airport close to the city and the Strait of Messina. Yet the official record does not identify it as an aircraft, meteor, balloon or other known event. The sensible reading is therefore cautious: unresolved in the archive, but not strong enough to carry large claims.

## What the archive recorded in August 1995

The relevant entry appears in the Italian Air Force historical file for 1991 to 2000. It is listed as case number 14 for August 1995. The place is Reggio Calabria, the date is 29 August 1995, and the approximate time is 21:30. The form is given as “diamond-shaped”; the colour field records a white light with red and green; speed is “not indicated”; movement is “descending”; altitude is “not indicated”; the weather is clear sky; and the report was made by a private citizen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1991-2000.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

The archive’s conclusion is brief. It says that, on the basis of examining the data in the archive, the event was catalogued as an unidentified flying object. That phrase matters, but it should not be stretched. In the Air Force’s own public explanation of its OVNI process, a report is classed as unidentified when the checks do not find a technical or natural justification; the stated purpose of the system is flight safety and national security, not the confirmation of extraordinary origins.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1991-2000.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

The entry also sits among a short run of other August 1995 Italian reports. Just before Reggio Calabria, the same archive lists a 21 August case from Capri involving several spherical bodies with luminous trails, and a 25 August case from Gallipoli involving a circular white light with blue, yellow and red emissions. Both were also catalogued as OVNI after archive review. This clustering does not prove a single cause, but it shows that the Reggio case was not preserved as a long narrative; it was one concise line in a national tabular reporting system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1991-2000.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Calabria_663de5_reggio_1995_diamond_fecf2a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="1995 Reggio illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Calabria_663de5_reggio_1995_diamond_fecf2a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Calabria_663de5_reggio_1995_diamond_fecf2a-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Colour, shape and movement in witness descriptions

The vivid part of the report is the combination of shape and colour. “Diamond-shaped” gives the sighting a memorable visual identity, while white, red and green lights make it easy to imagine something structured rather than a single point. But the same details also create caution. At night, witnesses often infer shape from the arrangement, brightness or motion of lights rather than from a clearly illuminated solid body. The archive does not say whether the witness saw a physical outline, a pattern of lights, or a bright object whose glare looked angular.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1991-2000.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

The colours are especially important. Aircraft commonly use red, green and white position or navigation lights: red on the left side, green on the right, and white at the rear or on wingtips, with other white lights also used for visibility. A distant aircraft turning, descending or approaching can therefore present a changing pattern of red, green and white lights without being immediately recognised by a ground observer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pilotinstitute.com/airplane-lights/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pilotinstitute.com">[Pilot Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pilotinstitute.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pilot Institute Airplane Lights: What Each Light Does (Red/Green, Strobe,</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pilot Institute Airplane Lights: What Each Light Does (Red/Green, Strobe,</span></span></span>

## Why the case remains unresolved but limited

The strongest evidence for the case is that it appears in an official Italian Air Force archive rather than only in folklore, social media or a later UFO retelling. The Air Force’s public procedure says reports are submitted through the Carabinieri, checked for correlation with human activity or natural phenomena, and then published as unidentified when no technical or natural explanation is found.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span></span></span>

The weakness is that the entry does not show the underlying investigation. Readers do not get a witness statement, observation point, viewing direction, duration, angular size, sound, comparison with stars or buildings, or records of air traffic checks. Without those details, the label “unidentified” is best read as “not identified from the available archive data”, not as “demonstrated to be extraordinary”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Archivio_OVNI_periodo1991-2000.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica Militare</span></span></span>

This is a common problem in UFO and UAP work. NASA’s independent UAP study warned that analysis is often hampered by poor calibration, lack of multiple measurements, missing sensor metadata and lack of baseline data. The Reggio Calabria entry is a good local example of that problem: it preserves a sighting, but not enough measurable information to test competing explanations properly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nasa.gov.</span></span></span>

The most reasonable assessment is therefore tiered:<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--benefit" markdown="1">

* **Archive status:** officially catalogued as unidentified in the Italian Air Force historical file.
* **Evidential strength:** modest, because it appears to rest on a private-citizen report with no published corroborating instrument data.
* **Plausible ordinary explanations:** aircraft lights are a strong candidate because of the red, green and white colour combination and the descending movement near an airport.

## Why private-citizen reports need cautious reading

Private-citizen reports are valuable because many real sky events are first noticed by ordinary observers. They can alert investigators to aircraft incidents, meteors, satellite re-entries, drones, balloons or genuinely unexplained events.
