## Introduction

Beyond Zanfretta, Liguria has produced a long stream of less spectacular reports: lights over the coast, objects photographed near Genoa Airport, luminous spheres in the Savona hinterland and periodic clusters amplified by local news and social media. Most lack radar confirmation, trace evidence or independent documentation. Liguria therefore matters less as a region of proven unexplained aircraft than as a clear example of how genuine uncertainty, difficult terrain, aviation activity, weak imagery and media storytelling can combine to create a durable regional UFO tradition.

## The 1978 case that defined Ligurian UFO history

On the night of 6 December 1978, Zanfretta was patrolling near Marzano, a rural settlement within the municipality of Torriglia, north-east of Genoa. According to the account that became public, he approached a property after noticing unusual lights and believing that burglars might be present. He then radioed his employer in a highly distressed state, reportedly saying that the figures confronting him were not men. Colleagues later found him shocked and disorientated. He initially described strange lights, a large object and towering non-human figures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanfretta_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span></span></span>

The setting helped the story take hold. Torriglia lies in a mountainous, thinly populated area where wooded slopes, narrow roads and broken sightlines can make lights difficult to judge. A bright object seen above one ridge may appear close while actually being much farther away. At the same time, an isolated night patrol gave Zanfretta’s fear an immediate human credibility: whatever caused it, his colleagues appear to have regarded his distress as real.

The Carabinieri were informed, and an enquiry gathered accounts of unusual lights from people in the wider Torriglia and Propata area. Later summaries state that officers documented flattened grass at the property and collected dozens of statements. These points are often presented by believers as independent confirmation. They are more limited than that interpretation suggests. A ground mark does not identify its cause, and reports of lights elsewhere do not establish that witnesses saw the same object, let alone the beings described by Zanfretta.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanfretta_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span></span></span>

The safest conclusion is that an unusual and frightening incident was reported, colleagues found Zanfretta in a disturbed condition, and police took the matter seriously enough to record statements. None of that independently verifies an extraterrestrial encounter.

## Hypnosis made the story richer but the evidence weaker

The most elaborate parts of the Zanfretta narrative emerged after journalists and UFO investigators became involved. During hypnosis, he described being taken aboard a craft, examined by large reptile-like beings and told increasingly detailed stories about their origin and intentions. Further alleged encounters followed between 1978 and the early 1980s, eventually turning a single confusing night into an extended abduction saga.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanfretta_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span></span></span>

For readers assessing the case today, this is the central evidential problem. Hypnosis can increase the amount a person reports without increasing its accuracy. Experimental work has found that hypnotised people may become more confident in incorrect recollections, while suggestion, expectations and leading questions can shape what is remembered. The resulting experience may feel vivid and sincere even when some details have been unconsciously constructed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11832514/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[nih.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pub Med Central (PMC)the role of hypnosis in memory recall and false</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">by DG Leo · 2025 · Cited by 10 — In this paper, we review the current literature on the mechanisms behind the creation of false memori...</span></span></span>

Accounts of the Zanfretta sessions also indicate that investigators discussed possible questions and interpretations around the hypnotised witness. That makes it difficult to separate his original memory from ideas introduced during the investigation. Even Italian UFO researchers who considered the case important later acknowledged that the hypnosis had been compromised by outside intervention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanfretta_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span></span></span>

This does not prove that Zanfretta deliberately invented his story. A witness may be honest, frightened and mistaken at the same time. His sincerity is therefore not the same as independent evidence for the creatures, the spacecraft or the later abduction claims. The more spectacular the account became, the more dependent it was on a memory-recovery process incapable of reliably distinguishing fact from suggestion.

## What corroboration actually exists?

The case is often described as unusually well documented, but its supporting evidence needs to be separated into different levels.

**Relatively firm elements include:**


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Zanfretta was working as a security guard in the Genoa area.
* An incident was reported during a night patrol on 6 December 1978.
* Colleagues encountered him in a visibly distressed condition.
* The matter was reported to the Carabinieri.
* Other people in the district reported unusual lights.
* The story received substantial press and television attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanfretta_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Zanfretta UFO Incident</span></span></span>

</div>

**Elements that are reported but do not establish an extraordinary cause include:**


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Flattened or disturbed grass near the property.
* Witnesses seeing lights in the broader area.
* Later claims of further encounters.
* Zanfretta’s repeated descriptions under hypnosis.

</div>

**Crucial evidence that is absent or unavailable includes:**


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* A verified radar track linked to the alleged craft.
* Photographs or film of the original encounter.
* Recoverable material from the supposed object.
* Medical evidence uniquely indicating abduction.
* Independent witnesses to the beings themselves.
* A documented chain of custody for any alleged physical artefact.

</div>

Zanfretta later claimed that he possessed a mysterious sphere given to him by the beings, but the object was never produced for independent examination. Without access to the alleged artefact, the claim adds narrative interest rather than evidential weight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier_Fortunato_Zanfretta" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pier Fortunato Zanfretta</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pier Fortunato Zanfretta</span></span></span>

## Why 1978 changed official handling in Italy

Zanfretta’s first report occurred during a major Italian wave of UFO interest. In the wake of the numerous reports made in 1978, Prime Minister Giulio Andreotti assigned the Italian Air Force responsibility for collecting, checking and monitoring UFO notifications. The Air Force says the purpose is flight and national security rather than the investigation of extraterrestrial life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[Aeronautica Militare]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeronautica Militare OVNI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica MilitareOVNI - Aeronautica Militare - Ministero della DifesaSegnalazioni O.V.N.I., Oggetti Volanti Non Identificati. Unless a report contains precise timings, direction of travel, duration, weather conditions and independent sensor data, even the Air Force may have too little information to make a positive identification.

Official interest nevertheless matters. It shows that unidentified reports are not automatically dismissed and that some have genuine relevance to airspace awareness. It does not validate the more dramatic interpretations later attached to them.

## Coastline, airport and radar claims

Liguria’s geography encourages both observation and misinterpretation. The region is a narrow coastal strip backed by steep mountains. Residents have broad views over the Ligurian Sea, while Genoa, Savona and La Spezia contain busy ports, shipping routes, aircraft movements and extensive artificial lighting. Objects may be seen against a dark marine horizon with few visual references for judging scale or distance.

Genoa’s Cristoforo Colombo Airport is especially attractive to UFO stories because it sits on reclaimed land beside the sea. Yet aviation proximity can make conventional explanations more likely, not less likely. Aircraft approaching at an angle may seem stationary; landing lights can remain visible while navigation lights are obscured; helicopters, birds and nearby insects can appear unusually large in compressed or magnified video.

A widely circulated 2023 recording promoted as an object near Genoa Airport illustrates this problem. Local investigators argued that frame-by-frame examination showed a small object close to the camera, probably a gull, rather than a large craft moving through controlled airspace. They also noted that an actual aircraft-sized intrusion near the airport should have produced stronger operational or surveillance evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.primocanale.it/commenti/28918-da-zanfretta-a-boldi-quanti-avvistamenti-a-genova.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: primocanale.it">[Primocanale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">primocanale.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">28918 da zanfretta a boldi quanti avvistamenti a genova</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica militare italiana. Recent Ligurian coverage has focused on luminous spheres or elongated lights reported between Genoa, Savona, Finale Ligure, Albenga and the inland hills. * **Satellites:** recently launched satellite groups can appear as a straight procession of evenly spaced lights.
* **Aircraft and helicopters:** bright forward-facing lights may hide the familiar shape and coloured navigation lamps.

## Media attention has often outgrown the underlying evidence

The Zanfretta story moved quickly from a local incident to national entertainment. Television appearances, magazine features, hypnosis recordings, books and later documentaries gave the case an unusually long cultural life. Repetition made its most dramatic details familiar, but familiarity should not be confused with corroboration.

Modern coverage can produce the same effect more rapidly. A short video may be reported first as a “mystery”, then repeated by several outlets before any technical analysis appears. Once an extraterrestrial interpretation has been included in headlines and social posts, a later identification as a bird, aircraft or satellite attracts less attention.

There is also a selection effect. Ordinary explanations rarely become part of regional folklore, while unresolved or visually strange cases are retold for decades. This makes Liguria’s record appear more mysterious than the complete body of reports probably is. Specialist UFO investigators themselves commonly find that most photographs and reports have definite, probable or possible conventional explanations after detailed examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bufora.org.uk/sightings/analysis-of-ufo-photographs" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bufora.org.uk">[BUFORA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bufora.org.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on bufora.org.uk.</span></span></span>

Local press remains valuable because it can preserve dates, names, locations and witness descriptions that would otherwise disappear. Its reports are strongest when they link to original images, identify the observer, provide exact timings and include later corrections. They are weakest when they recycle claims from advocacy groups without independent checking.

## How strong is Liguria’s overall UFO record?

Liguria has one landmark case, several recurring observation zones and a continuing culture of public reporting. It does not have publicly available evidence showing repeated incursions by extraordinary craft.

The Zanfretta incident remains **historically important but evidentially disputed**. The initial distress, police attention and reports of lights justify treating it as a genuine event in Italian UFO history. The alien figures and abduction narrative are weakly supported because they rely on a single witness and were expanded through suggestive hypnosis. Later claims did not produce testable physical evidence.

The region’s airport and coastal videos are generally **weak cases unless supported by independent data**. The 2023 Genoa Airport object was plausibly identified as a nearby gull, demonstrating how a dramatic visual impression can collapse once scale and frame position are examined.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.primocanale.it/commenti/28918-da-zanfretta-a-boldi-quanti-avvistamenti-a-genova.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: primocanale.it">[Primocanale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">primocanale.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">28918 da zanfretta a boldi quanti avvistamenti a genova</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica militare italiana. Secondo Maggioni, nessun Ufo ha attraversato l&#x27;aeroporto di Genova. &quot;In caso contrario, sarebbe stato...R...</span></span></span>

Recent clusters of luminous spheres are best described as **reported but not established**. They may contain individual events that remain unresolved, but the published material does not justify treating the cluster as one coherent phenomenon. Increased reporting can reflect publicity, observer numbers and new aerial technologies as much as a real increase in unusual objects.

Liguria’s UFO history is therefore most revealing as a history of evidence under pressure. A frightened witness can be sincere without being correct. Several people can report lights without seeing the same thing. A police file can confirm that a claim was investigated without confirming its extraordinary interpretation. A case can remain unidentified because the evidence is inadequate, not because every ordinary explanation has been eliminated.

## The lasting regional picture

Liguria occupies an important place in Italian UFO culture because the Torriglia case joined a vivid personal story to the nationwide sighting wave of 1978. It helped turn the wooded Genoese hinterland into a recognised setting for Italian encounter lore and remains the region’s natural reference point whenever new lights are reported.

Later evidence has not strengthened the central extraterrestrial claim. Instead, advances in memory research have weakened confidence in the hypnotically recovered portions of Zanfretta’s testimony, while digital-video analysis has shown how easily birds and other nearby objects can imitate distant craft. The official Italian system confirms that aerial reports may deserve investigation for safety reasons, but it also defines “unidentified” narrowly: no adequate technical or natural explanation was established from the available information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aeronautica.difesa.it/en/2023/01/12/ovni/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautica.difesa.it">[difesa.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautica.difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeronautica MilitareOVNI12 Jan 2023 — Once the investigations are concluded, the incidents are published under Sightings on this page and...</span></span></span>

The balanced verdict is that Liguria possesses a rich and culturally influential UFO history, not a strong public case for extraterrestrial visitation. Its best-known incident remains unresolved at the level of what first frightened Zanfretta, but its later alien-abduction narrative is too dependent on unreliable reconstruction to carry comparable weight. Most other regional reports are isolated, poorly documented or plausibly conventional. What endures is not proof of visitors, but a revealing record of how uncertainty becomes testimony, investigation, media spectacle and local legend.
