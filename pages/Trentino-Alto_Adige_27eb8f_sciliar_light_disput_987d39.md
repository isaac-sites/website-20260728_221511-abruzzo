## Introduction

The Sciliar light was most probably connected with the launch of the American military weather satellite DMSP F18 on 18 October 2009. That evening, observers across Europe saw exceptionally bright clouds produced by the Atlas V rocket’s Centaur upper stage as it burnt and vented propellant high above Earth. Specialist satellite watchers had predicted the display before it occurred, and their timed observations closely match the account from Aica di Fiè near the Sciliar massif.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/118809/vandenberg-officials-launch-atlas-v-rocket/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[af.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

That does not mean every detail of the local testimony has been satisfactorily explained. Actress Monica Trettel and three companions described an enormous, silent white light that appeared stationary over the Bolzano basin for roughly ten minutes before moving rapidly in a zigzag direction towards Renon. Other local [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses-c6fbd0/' | relative_url }}) reportedly described a similar phenomenon. No photograph, measured bearing, original observatory log or complete reconstruction has been published alongside the explanation. The case therefore matters less as evidence of an extraordinary craft than as a revealing dispute over how a real aerospace event can be perceived against an Alpine landscape—and how an explanation can remain unpersuasive when its supporting work is not shown.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://franzmagazine.com/2012/01/26/monica-trettel-e-lincontro-ravvicinato-con-gli-ufo-sullo-sciliar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: franzmagazine.com">[Franz Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">franzmagazine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Franz Magazine Monica Trettel e l’incontro ravvicinato con gli Ufo sullo Sciliar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Franz Magazine Monica Trettel e l’incontro ravvicinato con gli Ufo sullo Sciliar</span></span></span>

## What the witnesses reported

Trettel’s account was published in January 2012 as publicity for a theatrical presentation about UFOs. She said the sighting had occurred “two years” earlier while she was at her home in Aica di Fiè, which overlooks the valley west of the Sciliar. According to her recollection, four people watched a huge white light apparently suspended over the Bolzano basin. It was completely silent and, in her comparison, looked powerful enough to illuminate a football pitch. After about ten minutes, it seemed to vanish quickly towards Renon with a zigzag movement. No one in the group took a photograph.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://franzmagazine.com/2012/01/26/monica-trettel-e-lincontro-ravvicinato-con-gli-ufo-sullo-sciliar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: franzmagazine.com">[Franz Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">franzmagazine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Franz Magazine Monica Trettel e l’incontro ravvicinato con gli Ufo sullo Sciliar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Franz Magazine Monica Trettel e l’incontro ravvicinato con gli Ufo sullo Sciliar</span></span></span>

She said that she contacted the amateur astronomical observatory at Collepietra the next day. In her telling, the observatory had also noticed the phenomenon and had received several reports. She further referred to a priest in the Braies valley who had reportedly seen a similar light descending slowly and silently about fifteen minutes before her own observation. A local newspaper subsequently carried an explanation linking the light to a launch from Vandenberg Air Force Base in California.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://franzmagazine.com/2012/01/26/monica-trettel-e-lincontro-ravvicinato-con-gli-ufo-sullo-sciliar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: franzmagazine.com">[Franz Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">franzmagazine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Franz Magazine Monica Trettel e l’incontro ravvicinato con gli Ufo sullo Sciliar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Franz Magazine Monica Trettel e l’incontro ravvicinato con gli Ufo sullo Sciliar</span></span></span>

Several features made the experience compelling to those present:

* **Apparent size:** the light was remembered not as a point resembling a star or aircraft lamp, but as a vast luminous area.
* **Silence:** no engine noise accompanied it, reinforcing the impression that it was neither a conventional aircraft nor a nearby helicopter.
* **Duration:** roughly ten minutes gave the witnesses time to watch rather than merely glimpse it.
* **Apparent manoeuvring:** the reported zigzag departure suggested controlled local motion rather than a diffuse astronomical phenomenon.
* **Multiple observers:** Trettel was not alone, and she believed reports from elsewhere in South Tyrol referred to the same object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://franzmagazine.com/2012/01/26/monica-trettel-e-lincontro-ravvicinato-con-gli-ufo-sullo-sciliar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: franzmagazine.com">[Franz Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">franzmagazine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Franz Magazine Monica Trettel e l’incontro ravvicinato con gli Ufo sullo Sciliar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Franz Magazine Monica Trettel e l’incontro ravvicinato con gli Ufo sullo Sciliar</span></span></span>

These points establish that the experience was vivid and shared. They do not, by themselves, establish the light’s distance, physical size or trajectory. Those are precisely the quantities that are hardest to judge when a bright object is seen without a known reference distance.

## How the launch explanation arose

The strongest technical match is the DMSP F18 mission. The United States Air Force confirms that an Atlas V launched the Defence Meteorological Satellite Program spacecraft from Vandenberg at 9.12 am local time on 18 October 2009, equivalent to 16.12 UTC and 18.12 summer time in Italy. The satellite was intended for a near-polar, Sun-synchronous orbit used to gather military meteorological and environmental data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/118809/vandenberg-officials-launch-atlas-v-rocket/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

The rocket did not simply disappear from relevance once the satellite reached orbit. The mission used a Centaur upper stage powered by liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen. United Launch Alliance’s mission material identifies this cryogenic stage and shows that it remained active after the initial ascent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ulalaunch.com/docs/default-source/news-items/av_dmspf18_mob.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ulalaunch.com">[ULA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ulalaunch.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ULAAV017_mob:Layout 1.qxd</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ULAAV017_mob:Layout 1.qxd</span></span></span>

Satellite observer Ted Molczan calculated on the day of the launch that a Centaur burn and propellant dump should become visible from a large part of Europe shortly after 19.17 UTC—21.17 in Italy. His alert was posted before the predicted event, which makes it more valuable than an explanation constructed only after reports appeared. French observers were warned in advance because their viewing position was expected to be favourable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://satobs.org/seesat/Oct-2009/0096.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: satobs.org">[SatObs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">satobs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on satobs.org.</span></span></span>

Reports then arrived from observers who were deliberately watching for the spacecraft. In Darmstadt, Germany, Gerhard Holtkamp saw a bright cone-shaped cloud at about 19.13 UTC, followed by another luminous cloud with a red central light. He tracked the Centaur passing close to Vega and described intermittent venting or “puffing”. The clouds remained conspicuous even near a city horizon. Other observers reported expanding rings, a brilliant cone and two bright moving objects associated with the satellite and upper stage. French satellite-watching networks received numerous accounts, photographs and drawings of the display.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://satobs.org/seesat/Oct-2009/0105.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: satobs.org">[SatObs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">satobs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sat Obs See Sat-L Oct-09: Centaur-Clouds</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sat Obs See Sat-L Oct-09: Centaur-Clouds</span></span></span>

The timing is striking. ## Why a distant rocket cloud could look local

A propellant cloud in near-Earth space can appear enormous because gases released into very low pressure spread across a wide angular area. Sunlight can illuminate the expanding material even after observers on the ground are in darkness. Modern examples show that released propellant or exhaust products can form bright cones, rings, spirals and diffuse white clouds visible across several countries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://earthsky.org/space/strange-spiral-in-the-night-sky-rocket-fuel-dump-aug-12-2025/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: earthsky.org">[EarthSky]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">earthsky.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Earth Sky Strange spiral in the night sky was a rocket fuel dump</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Strange spiral in the night sky was a rocket fuel dumpAugust 13, 2025 — 12 Aug 2025 — The released fuel can freeze into ice cryst...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 13, 2025</span></span></span>

The DMSP F18 observations demonstrate that this was not merely a generic possibility. Trained sky watchers recorded large expanding structures at the predicted time. One observer estimated that the concentric rings covered three or four degrees of sky—many times the apparent width of the Moon. Another saw two clouds following different paths, one associated with an earlier Centaur firing and another with later venting. Such changing structures could readily be interpreted as a huge object altering direction, particularly by people who did not know that a rocket stage was crossing the European sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://satobs.org/seesat/Oct-2009/0105.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: satobs.org">[SatObs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">satobs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sat Obs See Sat-L Oct-09: Centaur-Clouds</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sat Obs See Sat-L Oct-09: Centaur-Clouds</span></span></span>

The mountainous setting adds another source of ambiguity. A light viewed above the Bolzano basin or against the outline of Renon and the Dolomites may seem to occupy the landscape even when it is hundreds or thousands of kilometres away. Without binocular tracking, angular measurements or a known foreground crossing, the eye cannot reliably determine whether a luminous patch is above a nearby ridge, over a neighbouring valley or far beyond the atmosphere.

Apparent stationary behaviour is also compatible with a slowly changing line of sight. A distant cloud expanding or moving largely towards or away from an observer may show little obvious sideways motion for several minutes. As different portions brighten, fade or separate, the perceived centre can then appear to jump or zigzag. This does not prove that every reported movement was illusory, but it provides a plausible mechanism that fits the independently documented behaviour of the Centaur clouds.

Silence is not a difficulty for the launch interpretation. The display was produced far from South Tyrol and at orbital or near-orbital altitude. There would be no locally audible rocket sound accompanying the visible cloud.

## Why the public evidence remains incomplete

The launch explanation is persuasive at the level of date, time, mission type and observed European phenomenon. It is weaker as a publicly documented reconstruction of the exact view from Aica di Fiè. The surviving article presents the conclusion in a sentence, but not the calculations behind it. It does not provide the witnesses’ exact observation time, compass bearing, elevation, duration measured by a clock or the location of the reported Braies observation. Nor does it reproduce the Collepietra observatory’s original notes or the local newspaper article that first connected the event to Vandenberg.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://franzmagazine.com/2012/01/26/monica-trettel-e-lincontro-ravvicinato-con-gli-ufo-sullo-sciliar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: franzmagazine.com">[Franz Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">franzmagazine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Franz Magazine Monica Trettel e l’incontro ravvicinato con gli Ufo sullo Sciliar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Franz Magazine Monica Trettel e l’incontro ravvicinato con gli Ufo sullo Sciliar</span></span></span>

That missing material matters because several claims require separate testing:

**The ten-minute stationary phase.** A proper reconstruction would plot the Centaur and illuminated cloud against the horizon as seen from Trettel’s balcony. It would show whether the predicted angular movement was slow enough to be remembered as stationary.

**The movement towards Renon.** The reported direction should be compared with the upper stage’s apparent track, not merely with its orbital direction on a world map. Perspective can make an object’s sky path differ markedly from a witness’s geographical description.

**The zigzag.** A diffuse, changing cloud may create an impression of irregular motion, but that should be demonstrated using photographs or timed observations from comparable locations rather than assumed.

**The Braies report.** If the priest’s observation occurred roughly fifteen minutes earlier, its direction and timing could either strengthen the launch identification or expose a mismatch. The published retelling supplies too little detail to decide.

**The observatory confirmation.** An observatory receiving several calls establishes that the phenomenon was widely visible. It would be more informative to know whether staff independently identified the Centaur, recorded its position or merely observed an unusual light.

The absence of this documentation does not place the rocket explanation and an extraordinary-craft hypothesis on equal footing. One side is supported by a confirmed launch, advance predictions and independent observations of the resulting clouds across Europe. The alternative rests on visual impressions without imagery or instrument data. The evidence therefore favours the Centaur explanation, but the lack of a published local reconstruction helps explain why witnesses felt dismissed rather than answered.

## Did later reporting strengthen the UFO claim?

Later reporting strengthened the space-launch interpretation more than the UFO interpretation. The Air Force and United Launch Alliance records confirm the correct kind of mission from the named base. Contemporary satellite-observer archives show that the Centaur display was predicted before it occurred and then seen widely at the relevant time. The descriptions of cones, rings, brilliant clouds and intermittent venting resemble the extraordinary appearance reported near the Sciliar.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/118809/vandenberg-officials-launch-atlas-v-rocket/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[af.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

By contrast, no publicly accessible photograph, video, radar trace or formal witness file has emerged to establish a separate low-altitude object over Bolzano. Trettel’s later account also moved beyond the local incident into broad claims about historical spacecraft imagery and worldwide secrecy. Those wider arguments do not add evidence to the Sciliar sighting and should not be used to judge what was seen that evening.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://franzmagazine.com/2012/01/26/monica-trettel-e-lincontro-ravvicinato-con-gli-ufo-sullo-sciliar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: franzmagazine.com">[Franz Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">franzmagazine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Franz Magazine Monica Trettel e l’incontro ravvicinato con gli Ufo sullo Sciliar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Franz Magazine Monica Trettel e l’incontro ravvicinato con gli Ufo sullo Sciliar</span></span></span>

The fairest classification is therefore **plausibly explained, but poorly communicated**. The sighting was not invented: witnesses in South Tyrol appear to have observed a genuinely unusual and impressive light display. Its most likely cause was not a conventional aircraft and need not have been an astronomical body. It was probably an uncommon human-made phenomenon—the illuminated burn and propellant release of a rocket stage passing over Europe.

Within the UFO history of [Trentino-Alto Adige]({{ 'trentino-alto-adige/' | relative_url }}), the Sciliar light remains important because it demonstrates the difference between an unidentified experience and an unexplained event. For the witnesses, the light was unidentified at the time and seemed to behave like something close to the mountains. For investigators with access to launch records and orbital predictions, there was a strong external match. The enduring dispute arose because that match was reported as a conclusion rather than presented as a full, testable reconstruction.
